## 1. The BabyLM challenge (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026): setup, budgets, winners, curriculum, multimodal, non-backprop entries

### Takeaway
Four rounds (2023-2026) have converged on one message: at 10M/100M words, wins come from architecture and training-objective changes (LTG-BERT, ELC-BERT, GPT-BERT, diffusion MLM, mixture-of-experts), not from curricula and not from adding images; the multimodal track never produced a submission that beat its baselines and was dropped as a separate track in 2026. No round reports any entry trained without backpropagation; the nearest thing is a 2026 spiking-neuron workshop paper whose training rule is not stated in its abstract.

### Cited Findings

**2023 (1st round; CoNLL 2023 workshop; findings re-posted to arXiv April 2025)**
- [V] Tracks: Strict (~100M words English text), Strict-Small (~10M words), Loose (100M words plus unlimited non-linguistic data such as audio or vision). 31 papers, 162 models (118 Strict-Small, 24 Strict, 20 Loose). — [Findings of the BabyLM Challenge, arXiv 2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08165)
- [V] Winner of Strict and Strict-Small: ELC-BERT (LTG-BERT backbone; each layer receives a weighted sum of all earlier layers' outputs). Scores BLiMP / GLUE / MSGS / BLiMP-supplement / aggregate: Strict 0.85 / 0.78 / 0.47 / 0.77 / 0.74; Strict-Small 0.80 / 0.74 / 0.29 / 0.67 / 0.66. Loose winner: Contextualizer (0.86 / 0.73 / 0.58 / 0.63 / 0.73). Llama-2 "skyline" aggregate 0.71, i.e. the best 100M-word model beat a model trained on vastly more text. — [arXiv 2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1)
- [V] Curriculum learning: 13 teams (41.9%) tried it, the most popular approach; the paper's verdict is that these attempts were "largely unsuccessful, though some showed modest improvements". — [arXiv 2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08165)
- [V] Multimodal/grounded entries in 2023 were few and did not reliably improve accuracy (a vision-language model marginally beat baselines in Strict-Small; music pretraining gave minor gains on subtasks). Strict models did not greatly outperform Strict-Small despite 10x the data. The organisers state "Training for hundreds of epochs is not cognitively plausible." — [arXiv 2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1)

**2024 (2nd round; CoNLL 2024 BabyLM workshop, Miami; findings posted December 2024)**
- [V] Tracks: 10M-word text, 100M-word text, and a 100M-word multimodal (text + image) track. 64 models from 31 participants (35 Strict-Small, 19 Strict, 8 Multimodal). Participants could bring their own data within the word budget. — [Findings of the Second BabyLM Challenge, arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05149)
- [V] Winner of both text tracks: GPT-BERT, a hybrid causal + masked LM on the LTG-BERT backbone. Strict: BLiMP 86.1 / BLiMP-supp 76.8 / GLUE 81.5 / EWoK 58.4 (avg 75.7). Strict-Small: 81.2 / 69.4 / 76.5 / 54.6 (avg 70.4). The best Strict model came within 2.5 points of human BLiMP performance. EWoK (world knowledge) stayed near chance for most entries. — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1)
- [V] Multimodal: no winner awarded; "No submissions outperformed the baselines in the multimodal track"; best submission text avg 61.3 / vision avg 52.6 versus Flamingo baseline 65.2 / 54.5. Organisers call it essentially a negative result. — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1)
- [V] Curriculum learning: estimated effect on score was negative (beta = -3.6, p = 0.055, not significant); organisers conclude it is not an effective strategy here, singling out training-objective curricula as the more promising variant. Training FLOPs correlated positively with score (beta = 2.7, p < 0.01). — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1)
- [V] Outstanding papers were cognitively motivated: phoneme-based tokenization ("From Babble to Words"; standard BPE still scored higher on the main text benchmarks) and synthetic "variation sets" mimicking child-directed speech. — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1)

**2025 (3rd round; First BabyLM Workshop at EMNLP 2025, Suzhou, 8 Nov 2025; proceedings pp. 399-420)**
- [V] Four tracks: Strict (100M words), Strict-Small (10M), Multimodal (100M words, half paired image-text), and new Interaction (student may get feedback from a teacher LLM; at most 100M external words and 100M self-generated words). New exposure cap counting repeated passes: 100M words total for Strict-Small, 1B words for the others (about 10 epochs), motivated explicitly by developmental plausibility ("dozens or hundreds of repeated exposures are developmentally implausible"). Intermediate checkpoints required every 1M words to 10M, every 10M to 100M, every 100M to 1B, so learning curves can be compared with children's. — [Findings of the Third BabyLM Challenge](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Corpus (Strict, 100M words): CHILDES 29M, Project Gutenberg children's stories 26M, OpenSubtitles 20M, Simple English Wikipedia 15M, BNC dialogue 8M, Switchboard 1M. Multimodal corpus: half of that text plus Localized Narratives 27M words and Conceptual Captions 23M words with 2.9M images. — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Participation: 32 workshop papers, 12 challenge papers, 32 leaderboard models (Strict-Small 15, Strict 7, Interaction 10, Multimodal 1). — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] New "human-likeness" evaluations, awarded separately from NLP-task scores: reading-time prediction from surprisal, wug-style morphological generalization correlated with human response distributions (past tense; -ity/-ness nominalization), entity tracking, COMPS property inheritance, and word-learning age of acquisition (model AoA from surprisal curves correlated with WordBank child AoA, after Chang and Bergen 2022). Standing tasks: BLiMP, BLiMP supplement, EWoK, a reduced (Super)GLUE; for multimodal: VQA, Winoground, DevBench. — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Winners. Strict: CLASS-IT (instruction tuning of small LMs) for human-likeness; a masked-diffusion LM for NLP tasks. Strict-Small: MoEP (mixture of experts; "particularly high scores in the AoA task") for human-likeness; AMLM-Hard-Decay (adaptive masking of hard tokens) for NLP tasks. Interaction: BLM (teacher LLM scores student story completions; signal used through reinforcement learning). — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Table 3 scores (human-likeness / NLP / macro average). Strict: CLASS-IT 20.4 / 52.9 / 36.6; Simple-Diffusion 12.6 / 58.4 / 35.5; baseline GPT-BERT-mixed 24.2 / 62.7 / 43.5. Strict-Small: MoEP 31.5 / 53.2 / 42.3; AMLM-Hard-Decay 8.4 / 58.3 / 33.3; best baseline macro 37.4. Multimodal: the single entry (BitMar) -9.3 / 49.0 / 19.9, vision 26.7, against baselines Flamingo 4.1 / 55.8 / 29.9 (vision 49.3) and GIT 7.6 / 54.7 / 31.1 (vision 49.7). Interaction: BLM 20.8 / 54.4 / 37.6 against SimPO baseline 20.4 / 54.1 / 37.3. So in the 100M Strict track the previous year's winner, used as baseline, was not beaten on macro average. — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Curriculum: "Curriculum learning was again the most popular approach" but "the best-performing approaches were again based on modifications to the pretraining objective or the model architecture". Compute: no complete correlation between training FLOPs and performance this year (positive only in the Interaction track). — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Developmental-trajectory observations from checkpoints: BLiMP rises steadily with words seen; wug past-tense generalization is flat for the first 10-50M words and then improves (described as a possible shift from memorization/overgeneralization to generalization); entity tracking shows U-shaped scaling in Strict models. — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- [V] Outstanding papers and what reviewers praised: a Gricean-maxim benchmark showing models trained on 100M words "fall well short of children's abilities" in pragmatics; a token-wise gating method that does yield multimodal models beating the baselines (workshop paper, not a track entry) and that argues the track's provided vision embeddings constrain performance; a teacher-demonstration dialogue paper praised for "strong grounding in the developmental psychology literature". — [same](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)

**2026 (4th round; Second BabyLM Workshop at EMNLP 2026, Budapest, October 2026)**
- [V] Tracks: Strict (100M), Strict-Small (10M), new Multilingual (English, Dutch, Chinese; 100M tokens total). Multimodal and Interaction were removed as standalone tracks and folded into Strict/Strict-Small; stated reasons were too few submissions, Interaction entries not outperforming Strict models, and multimodal entries not beating baselines. Exposure caps and intermediate checkpoints retained; baselines GPT-BERT, SimPO, GPT-2 Small; a detoxified corpus was issued. — [BabyLM Turns 4 call for papers, arXiv 2602.20092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20092)
- [V] Findings abstract: "GPT-BERT remains a strong architectural foundation for sample-efficient pre-training"; more training epochs, recursion/looping (weight-tied iterated layers) and data preprocessing were associated with stronger performance; multilingual winners benefited from LLM-generated parallel data. (Only the abstract was read; winners and counts were not verified.) — [Findings of the Fourth BabyLM Challenge, pp. 479-492](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.37/)
- [V] 2026 workshop papers directly relevant to staged/grounded learning:
  - Age-of-acquisition curriculum (sentences ordered by CDI AoA of their words; GPT-2 Small; 3 seeds; budgets up to 100M words): model acquisition order matched human order at rho = 0.63-0.78 versus 0.09-0.22 for shuffled baseline, but the curriculum encodes human AoA at rho = 0.96 by construction; with presentation order partialled out alignment falls to about 0.3; no BLiMP advantage at any budget. Same paper audits the corpus against day-long home recordings (SEEDLingS, 44 infants, 358,300 noun tokens): BabyLM word frequencies match real infant input only moderately (rho = 0.42); real-input frequency predicts child AoA at rho = -0.79 versus -0.48 for corpus frequency. — [Huda 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.14/)
  - TinyLibrary: 46K illustrated children's-book pages (ages 3-13) used to train small vision-language models with five curriculum orderings; age-based ordering "shows no reliable advantage over random ordering"; no ordering gave a grounding advantage (Winoground at chance; VQA above chance but unstable). — [Varghese 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.27/)
  - "Augustinian BabyLM": initialising embeddings of visually grounded tokens from image regions (10M-word masked LM) leaves a persistent imprint and helps on COMPS property knowledge for the seeded words only; no effect on grammar benchmarks. — [Bylinina 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.13/)
  - Mutual exclusivity: 45 text-only GPT-2 models (2.9M-33.5M parameters) trained on AO-CHILDES show anti-ME repetition priming on 85-100% of items; never reverses with scale; author argues referential grounding may be required for ME. — [Cacioli 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.19/)
  - URCHIN: a spiking language model (single layer of 128 leaky integrate-and-fire neurons, Dale's-law excitatory/inhibitory connectome, 4.23M parameters, no attention). The abstract does not state the learning rule; it mentions a "parallel state-space model (SSM) scan that is GPU-efficient for training". — [Chiang 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.44/)
- [V] In the 2026 proceedings listing (first 58 titles read) no title mentions Hebbian, predictive-coding, forward-forward or other non-backprop learning. — [Proceedings of the Second BabyLM Workshop](https://aclanthology.org/volumes/2026.babylm-main/)

### Inferences
- Across 2023-2026 the organisers' own regression/qualitative summaries agree that ordering the data (curriculum) does not buy benchmark score; the 2026 workshop adds that apparent "child-like acquisition order" from an AoA curriculum is largely built in by the ordering itself. A staged curriculum is therefore not, by itself, a selling point in this community; it needs a control with the same content in shuffled order.
- BabyLM is entirely gradient-trained transformer/RNN territory. A backprop-free learner has no direct precedent there and cannot be expected to compete on BLiMP/GLUE; its natural comparison set is the older connectionist/Hebbian developmental literature (sections 3-6) plus CVCL-style grounded word-learning tests (section 2).
- URCHIN's training is very probably gradient-based (the abstract describes a GPU scan built for training), but this is an inference from the abstract, not a verified fact.
- The compute finding flips between years (2024: FLOPs help; 2025: no clear relation after the epoch cap; 2026: more epochs help within the cap), so "sample efficiency" claims should report words seen including repeats.

### Gaps
- 2026 English-track winners, submission counts and scores were not verified (only the findings abstract was read).
- Whether any single BabyLM submission in any year used a non-gradient learning rule cannot be ruled out absolutely: the 2023 and 2024 findings were read to roughly the first 100k characters, and individual system papers were not opened. None of the four findings papers nor the 2026 title list reports one.
- A psycholinguist-oriented synthesis (Wilcox et al. 2025, "Bigger is not always better") is cited by the 2025 findings but was not read.

## 2. Learning from a child's own view (head-camera corpora; the 2024 Science study and follow-ups)

### Takeaway
The benchmark result is CVCL (Science, 2 Feb 2024): a contrastive network trained on 61 hours of one child's head-camera video with transcribed speech scores 61.6% on a 4-alternative picture-choice test for 22 words (chance 25%) and 34.7% on 64 out-of-distribution object categories; a 2025/2026 follow-up replicates this across three children at about 43-51% on 41 categories. These are the closest published numbers to "naming held-out pictures at about 50%", but they are comprehension (pick the picture for a word), not production, and all use backprop over many epochs.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Vong, Wang, Orhan and Lake, "Grounded language acquisition through the eyes and ears of a single child", Science 383, 504-511 (2 Feb 2024). Data: SAYCam-S, one child aged 6-25 months, 61 hours of video, 600,000 frames paired with 37,500 transcribed utterances; about 1% of the child's waking hours. — [Science, doi 10.1126/science.adi1374](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374) (read from [this copy](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/cnn/2024-vong.pdf))
- [V] Model (CVCL): separate image and utterance encoders embedded in a joint space with a contrastive objective that pulls temporally co-occurring frame/utterance pairs together and pushes others apart; vision encoder initialised by self-supervision on the child's own frames. — [same](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374)
- [V] Test format: the model is given a word and must pick the referent among four candidate images (adapted from child testing procedures). Labeled-S (22 concepts, frames from the same child's videos): CVCL 61.6%. CLIP (400M web image-text pairs) 66.7% on the same test; 11 of 22 concepts were within 5% of CLIP. Controls: shuffled frame/utterance pairing 26.7% (chance); randomly initialised frozen vision encoder 38.0%; linear probe trained with direct labels 81.6%. — [same](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374)
- [V] Out-of-distribution test (Konkle objects, single objects on white background, 64 categories): CVCL 34.7% overall; 16 of 64 concepts above 50%, a further 42 above chance; shuffled and random-feature controls 25.6% and 23.4%; CLIP 99.4%, linear probe 90.7%. — [same](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374)
- [V] Authors' stated limitations: learns from independent still frames rather than temporally extended episodes (hurting verbs and abstract words); learns passively from recordings whereas children are active and embodied; revisits its training data over multiple epochs rather than learning continually from a stream. — [same](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374)
- [S] Further limitations and outside critique reported in coverage: input is transcribed text, not the speech signal, so segmentation is not modelled; data come from one child, raising generalisability doubts; the model did poorly on "hand", attributed to lacking a body. — [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/2024/02/ai-autonomously-learns-language-through-the-experience-of-a-single-child-in-groundbreaking-study-221166); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/02/01/1087527/baby-ai-language-camera/amp/)
- [V] Follow-up: Vong and Lake, "On the robustness of modeling grounded word learning through a child's egocentric input" (arXiv 19 Jul 2025, revised 8 Jan 2026; preprint). Three SAYCam children (S, A, Y; 6-32 months; about 2 h/week; about 500 hours), automatically transcribed with WhisperX (266K utterances, 1.77M frames). Labeled-S accuracy: manual transcripts 61.6%; Whisper transcripts 56.9%; trained on disjoint videos 57.1%; all S data (about 3.5x) 63.2%. On 41-category per-child tests: about 51% (S), 49% (Y), 43% (A). Konkle objects: about 36-37% (S), 30% (A), 28% (Y). Cross-child transfer above chance but below within-child. Performance tracked the number of well-aligned frame/utterance pairs (S 7.6%, A 4.4%, Y 4.9% above a CLIP-similarity threshold). No architecture was consistently best. Evaluation covers concrete nouns only, English only. — [arXiv 2507.14749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14749)
- [V] A speech-based counterpart with infant-scale exposure: Khorrami and Räsänen, "A model of early word acquisition based on realistic-scale audiovisual naming events" (arXiv June 2024; Speech Communication 2025). wav2vec 2.0 audio encoder + DINO image encoder; 1,049 hours of speech for the first "6 months", then 2/4/6 months of image-speech pairs with naming-event counts matched to infant statistics. Word-meaning test (2-alternative forced choice, 80 categories, chance 50%): 57.8% / 67.8% / 72.8% at simulated 8 / 10 / 12 months; word-form test (89 CDI words): 60.1% after audio only, 80.7% at 12 months; audiovisual learning without prior auditory learning stayed at chance. Comparison with CDI vocabulary is explicitly qualitative. Limitations: read captions rather than infant-directed speech, photographs rather than a child's view. — [arXiv 2406.05259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05259)
- [V] Review of the speech-first line: Räsänen, "Computational modeling of early language learning from acoustic speech and audiovisual input without linguistic priors" (arXiv March 2026, book chapter): self-supervised and visually grounded models increasingly learn aspects of speech without linguistic priors; trends are more realistic input and tighter links between model behaviour and infant findings. — [arXiv 2603.08359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08359)
- [S] Larger egocentric corpus: BabyView (arXiv 2406.10447; updated July 2025) reports 868 hours from 31 families (earlier version: 433 home hours + 63 preschool hours), children 6 months to 3 years; self-supervised language and vision models trained on it still trail models trained on curated data, especially for vision. — [BabyView, arXiv 2406.10447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10447)
- [S] BabyVLM (arXiv April 2025) argues existing vision-language benchmarks are mismatched to infant-scale models and reports that developmentally constrained models generalise poorly to DevBench and VQA. — [arXiv 2504.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09426v1)
- [R] Antecedents by the same group: self-supervised vision from SAYCam headcam video (Orhan, Gupta and Lake, NeurIPS 2020) and "Learning high-level visual representations from a child's perspective without strong inductive biases" (Orhan and Lake, Nature Machine Intelligence 2024); the SAYCam corpus itself (Sullivan et al., Open Mind 2021). — [arXiv 2007.16189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.16189); [arXiv 2305.15372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15372); [doi 10.1162/opmi_a_00039](https://doi.org/10.1162/opmi_a_00039)
- [R] Visual grounding in text LMs at small scale: Zhuang et al. (NAACL 2024), "Visual grounding helps learn word meanings in low-data regimes" — grounding helps word-meaning measures at very small data sizes but the advantage shrinks as text grows. — [arXiv 2310.13257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13257)

### Inferences
- The field's accepted reporting template for grounded word learning is: n-alternative forced choice with stated chance; a shuffled-pairing control (must fall to chance); a degraded-vision control; an upper bound (web-scale model or supervised probe); a held-out in-distribution test and a separate out-of-distribution test; several seeds. A "50% on held-out images" result is interpretable only with the number of alternatives and these controls.
- CVCL 61.6% (4AFC, 22 words) and 43-51% (4AFC, 41 words) are the right comparison row for a held-out picture test, but a system that produces the name (open-vocabulary naming) is solving a harder task than CVCL's picture choice, and should say so.
- The strongest critiques that a new small model could answer better than CVCL: learning in one pass/continually rather than multi-epoch; learning from speech sound rather than transcripts; active rather than passive learning.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed formal commentary on Vong et al. 2024 was located in this session; critiques cited come from press coverage and the authors' own limitations section.
- Exact cross-child transfer accuracies in Vong and Lake are in a heatmap, not text.
- Whether the Vong and Lake follow-up has a journal venue was not established (arXiv only as of Jan 2026 revision).

## 3. Cross-situational word learning and fast-mapping models

### Takeaway
Associative, hypothesis-testing ("propose but verify"), Bayesian and neural models all fit core cross-situational data; a 2024 large-scale comparison (44 conditions, 1,696 participants) found two best-fitting models and stressed that mechanisms are hard to tell apart from accuracy data. The classic developmental signatures (vocabulary spurt, mutual exclusivity, shape bias, fast mapping, U-shaped curves) have each been produced by simple associative or Hebbian systems, which makes them the natural emergent-behaviour tests for a small non-backprop learner.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Two dominant families: associative models track co-occurrence between all words and referents; hypothesis-testing models keep a single hypothesised referent per word (propose-but-verify). Propose-but-verify accounts imply more explicit/aware learning than gradual associative accounts. — [Roembke et al. 2023, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1175272/full)
- [S] The distinction has blurred; models from both frameworks can run on the same data. — [Trends in Cognitive Sciences review (Smith, Suanda, Yu)](https://cogdev.sitehost.iu.edu/labwork/TICSsmith.pdf)
- [S] Kachergis and Frank, "A large-scale comparison of cross-situational word learning models" (CogSci 2024): associative and hypothesis-testing models fit by cross-validation to 44 experimental conditions and 1,696 participants; two best-fitting models; discussion of model and mechanism identifiability; generalisation to further experiments including developmental data. — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9q62p3f8)
- [S] Fazly, Alishahi and Stevenson (Cognitive Science 2010): incremental probabilistic model that keeps word-referent hypotheses while accumulating co-occurrence statistics (a hybrid). — [paper PDF](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/colareadinggroup/readings/FazlyAlishahiStevenson2010_CrossSitLearning.pdf)
- [R] Same model, trained on child-directed utterances paired with scene representations, reproduces a vocabulary spurt, fast mapping and mutual-exclusivity-like behaviour without built-in constraints. — [doi 10.1111/j.1551-6709.2010.01104.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2010.01104.x)
- [R] Yu and Smith (Psychological Science 2007): adults learn word-referent pairs from ambiguous trials by cross-situational statistics; the founding associative demonstration. — [doi 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01915.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01915.x)
- [R] Propose but verify: Trueswell, Medina, Hafri and Gleitman (Cognitive Psychology 2013): learners carry one conjecture per word and are at chance after a wrong guess; Pursuit model (Stevens, Gleitman, Trueswell and Yang, Cognitive Science 2017) adds reinforcement-style strengthening of the single pursued hypothesis and fits both lab data and CHILDES-scale simulations. — [doi 10.1016/j.cogpsych.2012.10.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2012.10.001); [doi 10.1111/cogs.12416](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12416)
- [R] Bayesian: Xu and Tenenbaum (Psychological Review 2007) explain generalisation of a new word to the right taxonomic level from one or three examples; Frank, Goodman and Tenenbaum (Psychological Science 2009) infer speaker intention and lexicon jointly and derive mutual exclusivity and fast mapping. — [doi 10.1037/0033-295X.114.2.245](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.114.2.245); [doi 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02335.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02335.x)
- [R] Vocabulary spurt: McMurray (Science 2007) shows acceleration follows mathematically whenever many words of varying difficulty are learned in parallel, with no change of mechanism; McMurray, Horst and Samuelson (Psychological Review 2012) separate fast in-the-moment referent selection from slow associative learning (fast mapping is not learning). — [doi 10.1126/science.1144073](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1144073); [doi 10.1037/a0029872](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029872)
- [V] Hebbian/self-organising: Mayor and Plunkett, "A neurocomputational account of taxonomic responding and fast mapping in early word learning" (Psychological Review 117(1), 1-31, 2010): a visual self-organising map and an acoustic self-organising map linked by Hebbian connections; accounts for taxonomic responding, fast mapping and the late-infancy acceleration in word learning; joint attention selects efficient cross-modal synapses. — [doi 10.1037/a0018130 (record)](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/people/kim-plunkett/publication_modal/45927)
- [S] DevLex (Li, Farkas and MacWhinney, Neural Networks 2004): a growing semantic map and a growing phonological map connected by Hebbian associative links; addresses the vocabulary spurt. — [paper PDF](https://langev.com/pdf../../pdf/li04earlyLexicalDevelopment.pdf)
- [R] Shape bias: Colunga and Smith (Psychological Review 2005) trained a recurrent network with contrastive Hebbian learning on the statistics of early noun vocabularies; it generalised novel solid-object names by shape and non-solid names by material, as children do. — [doi 10.1037/0033-295X.112.2.347](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.112.2.347)
- [R] Picture-label autoassociator: Plunkett, Sinha, Møller and Strandsby (Connection Science 1992) trained a network on image/label pairs (backprop) and obtained a vocabulary spurt, comprehension before production, and prototype effects; U-shaped learning in the past tense depends on vocabulary size and type/token structure (Plunkett and Marchman, Cognition 1993). — [doi 10.1080/09540099208946620](https://doi.org/10.1080/09540099208946620); [doi 10.1016/0010-0277(93)90057-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(93)90057-3)
- [R] Neural networks and mutual exclusivity: Gandhi and Lake (NeurIPS 2020) show standard gradient-trained networks lack an ME bias; Vong and Lake (Cognitive Science 2022) show multimodal networks can do cross-situational learning from images and words. — [arXiv 1906.10197](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10197); [doi 10.1111/cogs.13122](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.13122)
- [V] 2026 confirmation in child-scale LMs: text-only models trained on child-directed speech show the opposite of ME. — [Cacioli 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.19/)

### Inferences
- Signature behaviours are cheap to test and highly valued: a novel-word/novel-object trial (mutual exclusivity), a one-shot novel-name retention test (fast mapping versus retention, following McMurray et al.), generalisation by shape versus colour/texture (shape bias), a cumulative vocabulary curve (spurt), and an error-rate-over-time curve on a quasi-regular mapping (U-shape).
- Because McMurray 2007 shows a spurt arises from almost any parallel learner, a spurt alone is weak evidence; ME and shape bias are more diagnostic, since gradient-trained networks and text-only LMs demonstrably lack ME.
- Mayor and Plunkett, DevLex, and Colunga and Smith are precedents for Hebbian/local-rule word-learning models published in top psychology venues (Psychological Review).

### Gaps
- The names of the two best-fitting models in Kachergis and Frank (2024) could not be extracted (PDF not machine-readable here).
- All [R] items in this section need a source check before quoting specifics.

## 4. Developmental robotics and embodied language learning

### Takeaway
The iCub/Epigenetic Robotics Architecture line shows that simple associative learning on a body reproduces infant word-learning effects and, at its best, generated a prediction (posture binds names to objects) that was then confirmed in new infant experiments; robot babbling-to-word-form and finger-counting studies are small-scale proofs of concept. Typical venues: PLoS ONE, Interaction Studies, IEEE TAMD/TCDS, ICDL(-EpiRob), Adaptive Behavior, Nature Machine Intelligence.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Morse, Benitez, Belpaeme, Cangelosi and Smith, "Posture affects how robots and infants map words to objects" (PLoS ONE, 18 March 2015): a humanoid robot model binds heard names to seen objects through shared association with body posture/spatial location; the model replicated existing infant data and generated novel predictions that were tested and supported in new studies with 12-18-month-olds. — [doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0116012 (record)](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/69097/); [ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/03/150318153916.htm)
- [S] Twomey, Morse, Cangelosi and Horst, "Children's referent selection and word learning" (Interaction Studies 17(1), 2016): iCub with a simple associative architecture shows mutual-exclusivity referent selection and child-like retention patterns (retained a word only when the context had two familiar competitors, not three or four); authors conclude ME behaviour needs only word-object association learning. — [Lancaster University release](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/news/articles/2016/toddler-robots-help-solve-how-children-learn-/)
- [R] The underlying Epigenetic Robotics Architecture (Morse, de Greeff, Belpaeme and Cangelosi, IEEE Transactions on Autonomous Mental Development 2010) is built from self-organising maps (colour, posture, word) linked by Hebbian-style associative connections with spreading activation — no backprop. — [doi 10.1109/TAMD.2010.2087020](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAMD.2010.2087020)
- [V] Lyon, Nehaniv and Saunders, "Interactive language learning by robots: the transition from babbling to word forms" (PLoS ONE, 13 June 2012): iCub "DeeChee" starts with random syllabic babble (V, CV, VC, CVC), tabulates syllable frequencies heard from a human teacher, biases its babble toward frequent syllables, and adds a syllable to its lexicon when the teacher gives approval. 34 naive adults, two 4-minute sessions each. Word forms usually appeared within a few minutes; best documented case learned three words (red, green, cross) plus three non-words; teacher recognition of the robot's salient words about 45%, 49% and 61% across experimental sets (trend not significant). — [PLoS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0038236](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0038236)
- [S] Number learning: iCub neural models linking finger configurations and number words (Di Nuovo, De La Cruz and Cangelosi, ICDL-EpiRob 2014 and 2015) find that learning number words in sequence together with finger configurations helps build an initial number representation; later work appeared in Nature Machine Intelligence (Di Nuovo and McClelland 2019, number-digit knowledge in a child-like robot) and Psychological Research (Pecyna, Cangelosi and Di Nuovo 2022, counting and pointing compared with child studies). — [bi-cultural iCub study record](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/id/eprint/11271); [arXiv 1907.05270](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05270); [Psychological Research](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00426-020-01428-8)
- [S] Field review: "Computational and Robotic Models of Early Language Development: A Review" (arXiv March 2019). — [arXiv 1903.10246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.10246)
- [R] Earlier landmarks: Roy and Pentland's CELL learned words from raw speech and camera images of objects (Cognitive Science 2002); Sugita and Tani grounded two-word verb-object commands in robot behaviour with recurrent networks and showed generalisation to unseen word combinations (Adaptive Behavior 2005). — [doi 10.1207/s15516709cog2601_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2601_4); [doi 10.1177/105971230501300102](https://doi.org/10.1177/105971230501300102)

### Inferences
- The most admired result type in this sub-field is not accuracy but a confirmed novel prediction about infants (Morse et al. 2015) or replication of a specific infant experimental pattern with the same protocol (Twomey et al. 2016).
- Sample sizes and task scales accepted in these venues are small (handfuls of words, minutes of interaction), provided the developmental claim is specific and the mechanism is transparent.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 robot word-learning study with headline numbers was located; the recent centre of gravity appears to have moved to egocentric-video and speech models (section 2).
- Details of the ERA learning rule and the Sugita and Tani generalisation numbers are recalled, not re-read.

## 5. One-word to two-word transition, pivot/slot frames, chunk-based learners on child-directed speech

### Takeaway
Quantitative models of early multi-word speech are almost all non-neural, incremental, count-based learners trained on CHILDES input and scored by how well they regenerate the child's own utterances or error rates: the chunk-based learner reconstructs about 60% of child utterances across languages, and MOSAIC reproduces cross-linguistic rates of optional-infinitive errors at matched utterance length. No neural or Hebbian model with an explicit, child-benchmarked one-word-to-two-word transition was found.

### Cited Findings
- [V] McCauley and Christiansen, CAPPUCCINO (CogSci 2011), precursor of the Chunk-Based Learner: incremental, on-line; uses backward transitional probabilities between words to discover multi-word chunks; comprehension scored as shallow parsing, production as reconstruction of the child's actual utterances from the chunk inventory. Average sentence production score over 13 CHILDES corpora: 59.8%, versus 52.3%, 49.6% and 54.2% for forward-probability-chunk, forward-trigram and backward-trigram alternatives; better on every corpus. Word-based statistics beat word-class statistics. — [CogSci 2011 paper](https://csl-lab.psych.cornell.edu/files/2021/01/2011-mc-cogsci.pdf)
- [V] McCauley and Christiansen, "Language learning as language use: a cross-linguistic model of child language development" (Psychological Review 126(1), 1-51, 2019): comprehension on 79 single-child corpora (English, French, German); production on over 200 single-child corpora in 29 languages; also captures findings on children's production of complex sentence types. (Abstract gives no percentages.) — [repository record](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/id/eprint/3028272)
- [S] MOSAIC (Freudenthal, Pine, Gobet and colleagues): learns from orthographically transcribed child-directed speech by building a discrimination network word by word, encoding progressively longer utterance-final (and later utterance-initial) phrases; word classes arise only from distributional overlap. It simulates the differing rates of optional-infinitive errors across English, Dutch, German and Spanish as utterance length grows; its output on verb-island phenomena resembles the child's data more than the mother's input does. A later version added a defaulting mechanism because earlier ones underestimated error rates. — [MOSAIC cross-linguistic paper](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/617/1/cross-linguistic-final-rev.pdf); [verb-island paper](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/2124/1/Jones-2000-verbIsland.pdf); [project description](https://reshare.ukdataservice.ac.uk/853921/)
- [R] Usage-based "traceback" evidence that sets the quantitative target: in a dense diary corpus of a 2-year-old, about 63% of multi-word utterances on the test day were exact repeats of earlier ones, and about three-quarters of the remainder needed only one substitution/addition operation (Lieven, Behrens, Speares and Tomasello, Journal of Child Language 2003). Bannard, Lieven and Tomasello (PNAS 2009) induced lexically specific slot-and-frame grammars from two children's speech and showed they predict held-out utterances better at age 2 than at 3, when more abstract grammars fit. — [doi 10.1017/S0305000903005592](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305000903005592); [doi 10.1073/pnas.0905638106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0905638106)
- [R] Neural/backprop reference points: Elman's "starting small" (Cognition 1993) claimed staged input or growing memory was necessary for a recurrent network to learn grammar; Rohde and Plaut (Cognition 1999) failed to replicate the advantage. Chang, Dell and Bock's Dual-path model (Psychological Review 2006) learns syntax from message-sentence pairs by error-based learning. — [doi 10.1016/0010-0277(93)90058-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(93)90058-4); [doi 10.1016/S0010-0277(99)00031-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(99)00031-1); [doi 10.1037/0033-295X.113.2.234](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.2.234)
- [V] Hebbian, grounded word order: Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou, "Simulated language acquisition in a biologically realistic model of the brain" (arXiv July 2025, under review; also a COSYNE 2025 poster). Neuron assemblies with Hebbian plasticity (weight multiplied by 1+beta, beta about 0.05), k-winners-take-all inhibition, random connectivity; explicitly "does not use backpropagation". Each sentence is presented together with active sensory/motor representations of its meaning. Learns concrete nouns and intransitive verbs, noun/verb category, and word order (SV/VS; six transitive orders), and generates withheld sentences; about ten sentences per word, growing roughly linearly with lexicon size. No benchmark accuracies and no quantitative comparison with child data; phonology, function words and abstract words are not modelled. — [arXiv 2507.11788](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11788v1); precursor [arXiv 2306.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15364)
- [V] LM-versus-child stage comparison: Evanson, Lakretz and King (ACL 2023) trained 48 GPT-2 models from scratch, probed with 96 tests (BLiMP, Zorro, BIG-Bench) at each step and compared with 54 children aged 18 months to 6 years: models acquire skills in a systematic order, but in parallel (late skills improve from the first steps), and only some stages match children's. — [arXiv 2306.03586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03586)
- [V] Productivity tests borrowed from child-language research: Vázquez Martínez and Yang, "Measuring form and function in language models" (arXiv 2026; Second BabyLM Workshop). Determiner-noun overlap (does a noun appear with both "the" and "a", against a Zipfian expectation) and a discourse measure. Children aged 2-3 (Manchester corpus, 12 dyads) pass both (overlap 0.251 observed vs 0.242 predicted). Of 45 models (10M words to 156B words), 30 pass the formal overlap test, including some trained on about 10M words, but only two pass both, and no model with a child-comparable budget does. — [arXiv 2605.28616](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28616)

### Inferences
- A "two words" stage is most convincingly evaluated the way CBL and MOSAIC are: (a) held-out reconstruction of real child two-word utterances from a bag of their words, against n-gram baselines; (b) mean length of utterance trajectory; (c) ratio of frozen repeats to slot-filler novel combinations (traceback-style); (d) productivity of a frame with held-out fillers, against a frequency-matched expectation (the Yang overlap test). Demonstrating that novel word pairs are produced for unseen picture combinations corresponds to (d).
- Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou is the closest published non-backprop comparison for grounded word-order learning; it is a proof of concept with constructed input and no child benchmark, which leaves room for a model that does report child-referenced numbers.
- The Elman versus Rohde and Plaut history, plus four years of BabyLM, means staged input must be justified by an ablation, not assumed.

### Gaps
- No verified per-language production scores for the 2019 CBL paper (abstract says only "high").
- Exact optional-infinitive rates per language and utterance-length band in MOSAIC were not retrieved.
- No source located that models Braine-style pivot grammar acquisition quantitatively from grounded input; the search returned nothing on a simulated one-to-two-word transition as such.

## 6. Connectionist models of vocal learning: babbling, imitation/echo, reward-modulated or Hebbian sensorimotor learning

### Takeaway
Babbling models are the one area where local, reward-modulated or Hebbian learning is the norm: spiking networks with dopamine-gated STDP learn to produce syllabic (canonical) babble, and Hebbian-coupled sensory and motor maps learn vowel imitation. Evaluation is by developmental trajectory against yoked controls and by listener or recogniser intelligibility; recent (2025) work swaps in self-supervised speech representations and backprop.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Warlaumont and Finnegan, "Learning to produce syllabic speech sounds via reward-modulated neural plasticity" (PLoS ONE 2016): reservoir of 1,000 Izhikevich spiking neurons (80% excitatory) driving agonist/antagonist motor neurons for jaw and lip muscles of the Praat articulatory synthesizer; dopamine-modulated STDP on reservoir-to-motor synapses (potentiation only, with normalisation). Reward when a vocalisation's auditory salience exceeds an adaptive threshold. Over 2 simulated hours (7,200 trials; 5 runs per condition) salience rose (beta = 0.42, p < .001) and syllable nuclei per vocalisation rose (beta = 0.31), reaching about two syllables per vocalisation versus under one for yoked controls, which did not improve. Limitations: one motor degree of freedom, adult vocal tract, reward computed outside the network, no perception. — [PLoS ONE / PMC4726623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4726623)
- [S] Related: a 2013 version improved relative to controls after two hours of reinforcement from a human listener; a 2015 version (Yeh et al., ICDL-EpiRob) showed sound amplitude rising relative to yoked controls. — [ICDL-EpiRob 2013 paper](https://annewarlaumont.squarespace.com/s/WarlaumontICDLEpiRob2013.pdf); [Yeh et al. 2015](https://annewarlaumont.squarespace.com/s/YehEtAl_2015_ICDLER.pdf)
- [V] Robot babble to word forms via heard-syllable frequency and social approval (Lyon et al. 2012; see section 4). — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0038236)
- [S] DIVA: neural model of speech motor control with an articulatory synthesizer, in which learning is simplified into a babbling phase (learning sensorimotor mappings) and an imitation phase. — [Journal of Child Language review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-child-language/article/neurocomputational-modeling-of-speech-motor-development/CDB04B7A7319CB60CD757292AF5D94ED)
- [S] Murakami et al. (ICDL-EpiRob 2015): babbling and imitation of vowels with a 3D vocal tract, reinforcement learning and reservoir computing. — [cited in arXiv 2204.09381](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09381)
- [R] Westermann and Miranda (Brain and Language 2004): motor and auditory self-organising maps coupled by Hebbian connections during babbling yield vowel categories and imitation; Moulin-Frier, Nguyen and Oudeyer (Frontiers in Psychology 2014): curiosity-driven (learning-progress) exploration of a vocal tract self-organises into stages — no phonation, then unarticulated, then articulated sounds — and imitation of ambient speech emerges when it becomes learnable. — [doi 10.1016/S0093-934X(03)00345-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0093-934X(03)00345-6); [doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2013.01006](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.01006)
- [R] Caregiver-in-the-loop imitation: Howard and Messum's Elija (PLoS ONE 2014) discovers sounds by reward-driven exploration, then learns word pronunciations from caregivers' reformulations in three languages. — [doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0110334](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0110334)
- [S] Recent: "Artificial vocal learning guided by speech recognition" uses the VocalTractLab articulatory synthesizer, simulates learning from canonical babbling to first words, and treats intelligibility as the success measure. — [preprint record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381609560_Artificial_vocal_learning_guided_by_speech_recognition_What_it_may_tell_us_about_how_children_learn_to_speak)
- [S] Lavechin and Hueber (EMNLP 2025), "From perception to production: how acoustic invariance facilitates articulatory learning in a self-supervised vocal imitation model": speaker-invariant wav2vec 2.0 layers support learning an audio-to-articulation mapping that drives a synthesizer intelligibly; authors note the vocal tract does not grow and there is no babbling/exploration phase. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1217/)
- [R] Comparative review: Pagliarini, Leblois and Hinaut, "Vocal imitation in sensorimotor learning models: a comparative review" (IEEE Transactions on Cognitive and Developmental Systems 2021). — [doi 10.1109/TCDS.2020.3041179](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCDS.2020.3041179)

### Inferences
- For a babbling stage, the accepted evidence is a trajectory (canonical-syllable rate or salience over time), a yoked or no-plasticity control, and several runs with statistics; for an echo stage, intelligibility to human listeners or an independent recogniser, or recognition by caregivers as in Lyon et al. (45-61%).
- Published models treat babbling, imitation and word meaning in separate systems. A single network that passes babbling, echo and grounded naming stages in sequence is unusual; the gap named by Lavechin and Hueber (no exploration phase in modern imitation models) points the same way.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 model was found that combines reward-modulated babbling with later grounded word meaning in one system; absence in this search is not proof of absence.
- Stage timings in Moulin-Frier et al. and Elija's intelligibility numbers are recalled only.

## 7. Which models use local/Hebbian/non-backprop rules, and how the field judges small developmental models today

### Takeaway
Non-backprop developmental language models exist and have been published in strong venues, but all sit outside the BabyLM mainstream: Hebbian self-organising-map lexicon models, Hebbian robot architectures, reward-modulated STDP babbling, count-based chunk learners, and Hebbian neuron-assembly models. Reviewers now expect child-referenced evaluation — age-of-acquisition correlations, comparison of response patterns and error profiles with children, minimal-pair suites, learning trajectories, strong controls — and increasingly distrust bare accuracy and built-in curricula.

### Cited Findings
- Local/Hebbian/non-gradient learners identified in this survey:
  - [V] Mayor and Plunkett 2010: two self-organising maps + Hebbian cross-modal links (Psychological Review). — [record](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/people/kim-plunkett/publication_modal/45927)
  - [S] DevLex 2004: growing self-organising maps + Hebbian associative links (Neural Networks). — [paper PDF](https://langev.com/pdf../../pdf/li04earlyLexicalDevelopment.pdf)
  - [V] Warlaumont and Finnegan 2016: dopamine-modulated STDP (PLoS ONE). — [PMC4726623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4726623)
  - [V] Lyon et al. 2012: frequency tables + approval-triggered lexicon entry (PLoS ONE). — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0038236)
  - [V] CAPPUCCINO/CBL 2011-2019: on-line transitional-probability counting and chunk storage (CogSci; Psychological Review). — [CogSci 2011](https://csl-lab.psych.cornell.edu/files/2021/01/2011-mc-cogsci.pdf)
  - [S] MOSAIC: incremental discrimination-network growth (Cognitive Science and others). — [paper](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/617/1/cross-linguistic-final-rev.pdf)
  - [V] Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou 2023/2025: Hebbian assemblies with k-winners-take-all, explicitly no backprop (arXiv, under review). — [arXiv 2507.11788](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11788v1)
  - [S] Eugenio, "Hebbian learning the local structure of language" (arXiv March 2025): Hebbian model learning word-like/morphological structure; not evaluated on BabyLM-style tasks. — [arXiv 2503.02057](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2503.02057v1)
  - [R] Colunga and Smith 2005 (contrastive Hebbian learning; shape bias) and the Epigenetic Robotics Architecture (self-organising maps + Hebbian association). — [doi 10.1037/0033-295X.112.2.347](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.112.2.347); [doi 10.1109/TAMD.2010.2087020](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAMD.2010.2087020)
- Backprop-trained (for contrast): CVCL, Khorrami and Räsänen, all BabyLM winners, Plunkett et al. 1992, Dual-path. — [Science 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374); [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- Evaluation instruments currently expected:
  - [V] Minimal-pair grammar suites: BLiMP and BLiMP supplement in every BabyLM round; Zorro used with BLiMP and BIG-Bench in child-versus-model stage comparisons. — [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf); [Evanson et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03586)
  - [V] Age-of-acquisition correlation: model word-learning curves (sigmoid fits to surprisal over training) correlated with WordBank child AoA; a hidden task and a prize criterion in BabyLM 2025. — [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
  - [V] Human-pattern (not accuracy) comparison: DevBench (NeurIPS 2024 oral), seven lexical/syntactic/semantic tasks with child and adult behavioural data; models that score higher also resemble human response patterns more; more training moves responses toward adult patterns. — [arXiv 2406.10215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10215)
  - [V] Psychometric fit and generalisation: reading-time prediction, wug tests scored against human response distributions, entity tracking, property inheritance. — [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
  - [V] Developmental trajectories via required intermediate checkpoints. — [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
  - [V] Controls and bounds in grounded word learning: shuffled pairing, random features, web-scale and supervised upper bounds, in- and out-of-distribution tests, multiple seeds, multiple children. — [Science 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374); [arXiv 2507.14749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14749)
- Critiques shaping reviewer attitudes:
  - [V] Accuracy and well-formedness are insufficient evidence of child-like knowledge; productivity and discourse function must be tested separately (some models match children's choices at about 0.89-0.90 yet fail). — [Vázquez Martínez and Yang 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28616)
  - [V] AoA alignment can be an artefact of the curriculum that encodes it; content of the input matters more than order; standard corpora match real infant input only moderately. — [Huda 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.14/)
  - [V] Repeated exposure is treated as developmentally implausible and is capped; compute is reported. — [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
  - [S] Adult-centric benchmarks are a poor fit for child-scale models (BabyReasoningBench, arXiv January 2026); developmentally constrained vision-language models transfer poorly to existing benchmarks (BabyVLM); a multilingual developmental data benchmark exists (BabyBabelLM, arXiv October 2025). — [arXiv 2601.18933](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18933); [arXiv 2504.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09426v1); [arXiv 2510.10159](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10159)
  - [V] Pragmatics remains far below child level at 100M words; world-knowledge (EWoK) near chance. — [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf); [BabyLM 2024 findings](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1)
- [R] Position papers that define the standards: Warstadt and Bowman, "What artificial neural networks can tell us about human language acquisition" (2022) — a model is most informative when its input and learning are no richer than a child's; Frank, "Bridging the data gap between children and large language models" (Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2023); Dupoux, "Cognitive science in the era of artificial intelligence: a roadmap for reverse-engineering the infant language-learner" (Cognition 2018) — realistic input, scalable model, comparison with humans on the same tests. — [arXiv 2208.07998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07998); [doi 10.1016/j.tics.2023.08.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2023.08.007); [arXiv 1607.08723](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08723)

### Inferences
- Closest published comparisons for a small backprop-free network that babbles, echoes, names held-out pictures at about 50% and combines two words over picture-book pages, in order of similarity: (1) Mayor and Plunkett 2010 and DevLex — Hebbian cross-modal word learning with developmental signatures; (2) Morse et al. ERA on iCub — Hebbian associative grounding judged against infant experiments; (3) Warlaumont and Finnegan 2016 and Lyon et al. 2012 — babbling and babble-to-word stages; (4) Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou 2025 — Hebbian grounded nouns, verbs and word order; (5) Vong et al. 2024 and Vong and Lake 2025 — the reference numbers and test protocol for held-out picture-word accuracy; (6) TinyLibrary 2026 — the only located study training on illustrated children's-book pages; (7) McCauley and Christiansen CBL — the reference protocol for early multi-word production.
- What would count as interesting to this field, judged from what gets published and awarded: an emergent child-like signature that gradient-trained models lack (mutual exclusivity, shape bias); a learning curve or acquisition order matched to child norms without that order being supplied by the curriculum; single-pass or continual learning from realistic quantities of input; out-of-distribution generalisation with proper controls; one system spanning stages that the literature models separately; or a new prediction about children. A raw accuracy figure, or a staged curriculum without a shuffled-order ablation, would not.
- A backprop-free model would be judged against the cognitive-modelling bar (Psychological Review, Cognitive Science, CogSci proceedings, ICDL, IEEE TCDS, Developmental Science) rather than the BabyLM leaderboard; the BabyLM Workshop does accept non-competition papers on cognitive modelling and evaluation, as its 2025 and 2026 programmes show.

- No explicit published statement by reviewers or editors on how non-backprop developmental language models are received was found; the judgement above is inferred from findings papers, award citations and benchmark design.
- No benchmark designed specifically for staged (babble, echo, first words, two words) evaluation of a single learner was located.
- The learning rule of the 2026 spiking BabyLM entry (URCHIN) is unverified.
- Recalled-only references ([R]) in this section were not re-read.
