# Brain-inspired whole-system architectures and memory mechanisms (multi-organ, local-rule, continually learning systems) — state of the literature as of November 2026

Verification legend used on every finding:
- **[V]** = number/claim read this session in the primary source (full text or the paper's own abstract page).
- **[S]** = seen only in a search-result summary/snippet of the source this session; the paper itself was not opened. Treat numbers as probably right but unconfirmed.
- **[R]** = recalled from prior knowledge; not re-verified this session. The link is to the paper the claim comes from. Check before quoting numbers.

Reference system the relatives are compared against (from the brief): a small online, one-experience-at-a-time, no-backprop learner made of (a) a competitive-learning visual cortex with decorrelation, (b) associative word/concept cells with dendrite-like compartments trained by a delta rule, (c) a one-shot episodic store, (d) a reward-modulated selector among routes (copy / recall / habit), (e) growth and pruning.

## 1. Complementary learning systems (CLS) theory and its modern implementations (fast hippocampus-like store + slow neocortex-like learner, replay / sleep consolidation), including local-rule implementations

### Takeaway
CLS is still the organizing theory for "fast one-shot store beside a slow learner", and every modern ML implementation that reaches respectable class-incremental accuracy trains the slow learner with backprop; the implementations that use only local rules (the Leabra/emergent hippocampus-cortex family, sleep-STDP spiking models) are faithful to the biology but have only been shown on toy problems of roughly 10-30 items. Replay of internal/feature-level representations rather than raw inputs is the main point of convergence between the neuroscience and the ML results.

### Cited Findings

**Theory**
- [R] McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly (Psychological Review, 1995) is the founding statement: a slow-learning neocortex with overlapping distributed codes extracts structure but suffers catastrophic interference if new items are learned in a focused block; a fast-learning hippocampus with sparse, pattern-separated codes stores individual episodes and replays them interleaved with old knowledge so the cortex can integrate them gradually. — [McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)
- [R] Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland (Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2016) updated the theory: hippocampal replay can be weighted (not a faithful statistics-preserving copy), the hippocampus itself supports some generalization, and cortex can learn rapidly when new information is consistent with existing schemas. — [Kumaran et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.05.004)
- [S] The standard summary of the split: hippocampal formation "rapidly learns distinct observations, forming sparser, non-overlapping and therefore non-interfering representations"; cortex "incrementally learn[s] regularities across many observations"; replay is interleaved to avoid catastrophic forgetting. — [Kowadlo et al. 2020, arXiv 2010.15999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15999)
- [R] McClelland, McNaughton & Lampinen (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B, 2020): interference from new learning depends on how the new item projects onto the knowledge structure already in the network; schema-consistent items can be absorbed quickly with little interference, and only similar old items need to be interleaved. — [McClelland et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0637)
- [R] Sun, Advani, Spruston, Saxe & Fitzgerald (Nature Neuroscience, 2023), "Organizing memories for generalization in complementary learning systems": unregulated transfer of everything from the fast store to the slow learner can hurt generalization when experiences are noisy/unpredictable; consolidation should be gated by how predictable the content is, so some memories should stay hippocampal. — [Sun et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01382-9)

**Local-rule implementations**
- [V] Singh, Norman & Schapiro (PNAS 119(44), Oct 2022) built a hippocampus (C-HORSE, with monosynaptic and trisynaptic pathways) plus a single neocortical hidden layer in the emergent framework. Learning is local and error-driven via contrastive Hebbian learning — the paper says CHL "computes errors locally but behaves similarly to the backpropagation algorithm"; during sleep, plus/minus phases are defined by stability of activity, with short-term synaptic depression moving the network between attractors and oscillating inhibition distorting them. Plain Hebbian learning during sleep "caused substantial forgetting of unique features" (supplement). — [Singh et al. 2022, Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9636926/fullTextXML)
- [V] Same paper, Simulation 1: 15 "satellite" exemplars in 3 categories, 7 features each; trained to 66% on feature inference, then one 30,000-cycle NREM epoch; shared (category) features improved significantly (P < 0.001) while unique features were preserved; replay showed a within-category preference of 39.4% vs. 28.5% chance. Simulation 2 (two environments of 10 items each, AB-AC-style overlap): alternating NREM/REM for five cycles improved the new environment without catastrophic loss of the old one; NREM-only gave the largest new-environment gain but substantial deterioration of the old; REM-only gave no new gain and no damage; a blocked schedule (five NREM then five REM) protected the old environment less than alternation. — [Singh et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9636926/fullTextXML)
- [V] Limits stated by Singh et al.: rate-coded units (oscillations are an abstraction), no modelling of wake interference/decay, no mechanism for prioritizing which memories replay, no sequential replay, parameters tuned by search; the authors call the simulations demonstrations rather than a full account. Scale is tens of items. — [Singh et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9636926/fullTextXML)
- [S] Zheng, Liu, Nishiyama, Ranganath & O'Reilly (PLOS Computational Biology, Oct 2022), "Theremin" (Total Hippocampal ERror MINimization): extends local error-driven learning to CA3; compared with the group's earlier Hebbian hippocampus model it "has significantly increased capacity and learning speed". Earlier, Ketz, Morkonda & O'Reilly (PLOS CB 2013) showed theta-phase error-driven learning in hippocampal subfields gives "significantly greater capacity" than Hebbian learning. — [DOAJ record of Zheng et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/10f3b924453744ea83e3bb1bf567bdbe); [Ketz et al. listing](https://colorado.academia.edu/NicholasKetz)
- [S] The textbook Leabra hippocampus simulation (AB-AC paired associates) "is able to learn rapidly and with much reduced levels of interference compared to the prior cortical model of this same task". — [CompCogNeuro sims, ch8/hip](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/CompCogNeuro/sims/ch8/hip)
- [V] Kowadlo, Ahmed & Rawlinson (AI 2020, Australasian Joint Conference; arXiv Oct 2020) implement CLS as an artificial hippocampal network ("AHA" line) and extend Omniglot one-shot classification with an instance-recognition test (same exact sample after one exposure, with noise and occlusion). Abstract-level results only: classification "comparable to existing supervised ANNs" without domain-specific inductive biases, and significantly better than nearest-neighbour on instance recognition under occlusion/noise. — [arXiv 2010.15999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15999)
- [S] Sleep in spiking networks with local rules: a spiking model "mitigated catastrophic forgetting by constraining the network synaptic weight state to the previously learned manifold when off-line reactivation was interleaved with new training". — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10438306)

**Backprop-trained implementations (for calibration)**
- [V] van de Ven, Siegelmann & Tolias (Nature Communications 11:4069, 2020), "brain-inspired replay" (BI-R): five modifications of generative replay — generator merged into the classifier via feedback connections, class-conditional replay from a Gaussian-mixture prior, context gating, internal replay of hidden representations (above frozen pre-trained conv layers), and distillation. Numbers given in the text: BI-R+SI reached 0.904 ± 0.005 on 100-task permuted MNIST and 0.344 ± 0.002 on class-incremental Split CIFAR-100 (0.892 and 0.334 without replay-through-feedback). The text says BI-R "outperforms the other methods but remains well below joint training" on class-incremental CIFAR-100. Stated limits: depends on pre-trained conv layers; conditional replay and gating need class labels; images only. — [van de Ven et al. 2020, Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7426273/fullTextXML)
- [V] Jun, Marupudi, Shah & Varma (arXiv, 15 Jul 2025): VAE as neocortex (pattern completion) + modern Hopfield network as hippocampus (pattern separation), class-incremental Split-MNIST with no task labels and no stored raw-image buffer: 89.71% (best sweep model 91.01%) vs. 95.55% offline upper bound, 67.75% sequential VAE without replay, 8.92% untrained. The VAE is trained with backprop; the Hopfield store is not. Only Split-MNIST was tested. — [arXiv 2507.11393](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11393v1)
- [S] Spens & Burgess (Nature Human Behaviour, 2024), "A generative model of memory construction and consolidation": episodes are first encoded in a modern Hopfield network (hippocampus) and replayed to train generative (VAE-style) cortical networks, which "gradually take over". — [Spens & Burgess 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01799-z.pdf)
- [R] Hayes et al. (Neural Computation, 2021), "Replay in deep learning: current approaches and missing biological elements", catalogues what ML replay leaves out of biological replay (selective/prioritized replay, replay of compressed internal representations, replay during offline states, generative rather than veridical replay). — [arXiv 2104.04132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04132)

### Inferences
- The closest published relative of "one-shot episodic store beside slow delta-rule associative cells, no backprop" is the Leabra/emergent family (C-HORSE, Theremin, Singh et al. 2022). Its own findings are directly relevant: (i) pure Hebbian hippocampal learning has lower capacity than local error-driven learning; (ii) pure Hebbian replay during sleep damages item-specific detail; (iii) replay focused only on recent material overwrites older cortical knowledge, and alternating recent-focused and remote-focused replay fixes it. A training-mix shift between stages is the wake-time analogue of (iii).
- Every CLS implementation with competitive benchmark numbers freezes or pre-trains the feature extractor and replays at the feature level. For a system whose sensory cortex stays plastic, this is a known unsolved interaction: the stored episodic keys drift when the cortex changes (the SDM paper in section 2 reports exactly this failure).
- Sun et al. (2023) and Kumaran et al. (2016) both argue against indiscriminate consolidation; "keep some things in the episodic store" is a theoretically supported design choice, not a shortcut.

### Gaps
- No source found (and none recalled) that shows a fully local-rule CLS system at the scale of even CIFAR-level class-incremental learning with a plastic feature extractor. The local-rule CLS models are all at tens-of-items scale.
- Exact effect sizes for Singh et al. Simulation 2 are only in figures/supplement and were not extracted.
- Theremin's capacity numbers were not verified from the paper.
- The Kowadlo et al. learning rule (whether fully local) and quantitative Omniglot results were not confirmed from the full text.

## 2. Continual learning without backprop or with local rules: metaplasticity, synaptic consolidation, sparse/dendritic gating, generative replay, sleep-like replay — what works for class-incremental or curriculum shifts

### Takeaway
For class-incremental learning (the hard case, and the one a shifting training mix resembles), weight-protection methods alone fail at roughly "last task only" accuracy, while replay, sparse high-dimensional expansion with update-only-active-synapses, and context gating each recover a large part of the gap — but nothing replay-free and backprop-free reaches joint-training accuracy. The strongest backprop-free result found is the fruit-fly "sparse expansion + frozen-other-synapses perceptron" model, which beat EWC, SI and MAS on class-incremental Split CIFAR-10 when run on frozen pre-trained features.

### Cited Findings

**Baseline: weight protection fails in the class-incremental setting**
- [V] Tadros, Krishnan, Ramyaa & Bazhenov (Nature Communications 13:7742, Dec 2022), Table 1, class-incremental accuracy (%): sequential training MNIST 19.49 / Fashion-MNIST 19.67 / CIFAR-10 19.01; EWC 20.37 / 21.39 / 18.54; SI 21.38 / 22.05 / 22.84; OWM 77.04 / 58.35 / 34.23; sleep replay consolidation (SRC) alone 48.47 / 41.68 / 44.55; rehearsal with 0.75% old data 79.91 / 55.19 / 39.39; rehearsal + SRC 86.47 / 67.82 / 58.24; joint training 98.02 / 87.86 / 72.43. — [Tadros et al. 2022, Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9755223/fullTextXML)
- [R] van de Ven, Tuytelaars & Tolias (Nature Machine Intelligence, 2022; earlier arXiv 2019) define task-, domain- and class-incremental learning and show that regularization methods that work in the first two collapse in class-incremental learning, where replay-type methods are needed. — [van de Ven et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00568-3); [arXiv 1904.07734](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)

**Sleep-like replay with a local rule**
- [V] SRC mechanism (Tadros et al. 2022): after ordinary backprop training on a task, ReLU units are swapped for Heaviside (spike-like) thresholds, weights are scaled layer-wise, the network is driven by noisy binary inputs whose per-pixel firing probability equals the mean intensity of past data (no old examples stored), and a Hebbian rule strengthens co-active pre/post pairs and weakens weights when post fires without pre. Authors report old-task units become more active in sleep and class representations become sparser and less correlated. Stated limits: SRC alone is well below rehearsal and joint training; conv layers were frozen; CIFAR-10 used features from a network pre-trained on Tiny ImageNet; thresholds/scales were tuned by a genetic algorithm; cost is about one extra task of training. — [Tadros et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9755223/fullTextXML)
- [S] A 2025 preprint applies SRC to recurrent networks trained with Equilibrium Propagation (a local rule) and reports that SRC "significantly improves RNN's resilience to catastrophic forgetting", with awake rehearsal adding further benefit. — [arXiv 2508.14081](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14081v1)
- [S] A follow-up found sleep replay "not only rescued previously learned information that had been catastrophically forgotten following new task training but often enhanced performance in prior tasks, especially for tasks trained with limited data". — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10544257)

**Sparse / dendritic context gating**
- [V] Iyer, Grewal, Velu, Souza, Forest & Ahmad (Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 2022; arXiv Dec 2021), Active Dendrites Networks: each unit has dendritic segments that match a context vector and modulate the unit before a k-winners-take-all layer. Permuted MNIST (single shared 10-way head, chance 10%): 94.6% at 10 tasks and 81.4% at 100 tasks with task prototypes given; 94.3% and 76.9% with task inferred by online prototype clustering; 97.2% and 91.6% when combined with SI. Meta-World MT10 multi-task RL: 87.5% vs. 76.6% for an MLP baseline (10 runs). Networks: two hidden layers of 2,048 units (permuted MNIST, 5% activation sparsity); 2,800 units with 10 segments each (MT10, 25% sparsity). Training is end-to-end backprop. Stated limits: needs tests beyond permuted MNIST/MT10, context-vector generation is weak, SI combination needs task identity during training, only feedforward basal dendrites modelled. — [Iyer et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2022.846219/full)
- [R] Masse, Grant & Freedman (PNAS, 2018), context-dependent gating (XdG): gating a random, mostly non-overlapping subset of units per task plus synaptic stabilization gave about 95% mean accuracy over 100 permuted-MNIST tasks, versus roughly 70% for EWC and 82% for SI alone (the 70/82 figures are also quoted in the van de Ven 2020 text as coming from this paper [V]). Requires the task identity as input. — [arXiv 1802.01569](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01569); [van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7426273/fullTextXML)

**Sparse expansion + local associative rule (no backprop)**
- [V] Shen, Dasgupta & Navlakha (arXiv Jul 2021; [S] published in Neural Computation 2023), "FlyModel": a two-layer circuit — sparse, high-dimensional random expansion with winner-take-all, then an associative layer where only synapses from active units to the correct output are changed and all others are frozen. The abstract calls the rule "strikingly similar to the classic perceptron learning algorithm" with two modifications critical for reducing forgetting. No backprop. — [arXiv 2107.07617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07617); [search summary citing NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10517683)
- [V] Bricken, Davies, Singh, Krotov & Kreiman (ICLR 2023), "Sparse Distributed Memory is a Continual Learner": an MLP built from Kanerva's SDM (Top-K activation, L2-normalized inputs and weights, no biases, non-negative weights), trained with backprop/SGD without momentum. Class-incremental Split CIFAR-10 on frozen 256-d ConvMixer embeddings (pre-trained on ImageNet32), 10K neurons, no replay and no task labels: SDMLP 0.71, FlyModel 0.82, MAS 0.67, EWC 0.65, SI 0.44, plain ReLU MLP 0.21, SDMLP+EWC 0.86, oracle 0.93. Ablations (1K neurons): Adam 0.23 and RMSProp 0.20 versus SGD 0.63 ("stale momentum" keeps updating inactive neurons); removing L2 normalization or adding biases dropped accuracy to 0.20. — [arXiv 2303.11934 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11934)
- [V] Limits stated by Bricken et al.: Top-K creates dead neurons, fixed by annealing k from layer size down to target ("GABA switch"); avoiding dead neurons "seems to require a static data manifold" — jointly training the SDM layer with the ConvMixer produced many dead neurons and continual learning failed; pruning weights caused catastrophic forgetting. — [arXiv 2303.11934 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11934)
- [S] Zou, Zang & Ji (arXiv Feb 2025) re-examine the fly circuit for class-incremental learning on CIFAR-100 and CUB-200-2011 and report it improves both stability and plasticity; "FlyPrompt" (ICLR 2026) uses mushroom-body-style sparse expansion to route inputs to task-wise experts. — [arXiv 2502.01427](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01427); [arXiv 2602.01976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01976)

**Metaplasticity / synaptic consolidation**
- [S] Laborieux, Ernoult, Hirtzlin & Querlioz (Nature Communications, 2021): the hidden real-valued weights of binarized networks are treated as metaplastic variables (the larger the hidden weight, the harder to flip); on sequential permuted MNIST accuracy is "equivalent to elastic weight consolidation, but without any change to the loss function" and without task boundaries or stored data; also Fashion-MNIST, MNIST-USPS and CIFAR-10/100 features. — [arXiv 2101.07592](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.07592); [arXiv 2003.03533](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03533)
- [R] EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., PNAS 2017) and Synaptic Intelligence (Zenke, Poole & Ganguli, ICML 2017) are the canonical per-synapse importance methods; SI computes importance online from each synapse's own trajectory. — [arXiv 1612.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796); [arXiv 1703.04200](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04200)
- [R] Fusi, Drew & Abbott (Neuron, 2005; cascade model) and Benna & Fusi (Nature Neuroscience, 2016) give the theory: synapses with multiple hidden states on a range of timescales extend memory lifetime far beyond simple bounded synapses, with capacity scaling close to linearly in the number of synapses in the Benna-Fusi model. — [Fusi et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.02.001); [Benna & Fusi 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4401)

**Replay selection when the mix changes**
- [S] Saxena, Shobe & McNaughton (PNAS 119(27), 2022), similarity-weighted interleaved learning (SWIL): interleaving only old items with substantial representational similarity to the new material learns new information "with similar accuracy and minimal interference, using a much smaller number of old items per epoch"; shown on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100; data efficiency and speed-up grow roughly in proportion to the number of non-overlapping classes stored. — [UCI release](https://news.uci.edu/2022/07/05/solving-algorithm-amnesia-reveals-clues-to-how-we-learn); [eScholarship copy](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1rw1b1m4)

**Other local-rule continual learners**
- [S] Ororbia et al., "Lifelong neural predictive coding" (arXiv 2019): a predictive-coding-style network with local updates paired with a basal-ganglia-like task selector for cumulative learning. — [arXiv 1905.10696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10696)
- [R] Song et al. (Nature Neuroscience, 2024), "prospective configuration": energy-based/predictive-coding networks that first infer target activity and then change weights; the paper reports advantages over backprop in online learning, small-batch learning and continual learning on small benchmarks. — [Song et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01514-1)
- [V] Li & Choa (arXiv Jan 2026), a backprop-free feedback-Hebbian network (centered Hebbian covariance + Oja stabilization + a local supervised term): on a two-pair association task, sequential A-then-B training produced LTD-like suppression of the earlier association in forward weights while the feedback pathway kept a trace; alternating training maintained both. Evidence is a deliberately minimal task. — [arXiv 2601.06758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06758)
- [V] Atmuri & Bhogarajula (arXiv 29 Jul 2026), "CMP": sparse relational codes (k-WTA over products of fixed random role embeddings), a two-tier competitive memory (fast buffer + slower register, similarity-threshold writes), a predictive-coding residual stage, and a linear readout trained by a delta-style rule; no gradients pass through the feature modules. On 15-domain byte-level domain-incremental language modelling without replay or task IDs: backward-transfer (forgetting, in bits-per-byte) +0.148 versus +2.790 for a naively fine-tuned Transformer and +2.246 for Online EWC (about 6.5M parameters, 3.8M-byte budget). But absolute quality is poor: about 3.1-3.3 bits per byte, "well below" a comparable Transformer; no advantage on Split-MNIST (reported as a null result); adding depth made it worse (3.48 vs. 3.27 BPB); custom benchmark, code not public, not peer reviewed. — [arXiv 2607.26523](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26523v1)

### Inferences
- The pattern across FlyModel, SDMLP, Active Dendrites and XdG is the same: forgetting falls when (1) codes are sparse and high-dimensional so different inputs touch different units, and (2) learning touches only the synapses of currently active units. A delta rule on dendrite-like compartments over a k-WTA code is in this family. The published cost is also consistent: dead units, sensitivity to k, and failure when the upstream representation keeps moving.
- A change in training mix between stages is, in benchmark terms, somewhere between domain- and class-incremental learning. The verified numbers say per-synapse protection alone (EWC/SI at about 20% on class-incremental MNIST) will not be enough; some form of rehearsal — even 0.75% of old data, or statistics-only sleep replay — changes the outcome qualitatively (SRC 48%, rehearsal+SRC 86%).
- SWIL plus McClelland et al. (2020) imply that rehearsal after a mix change need not be uniform: replaying only the old items that are similar to the new material is the theoretically motivated minimum.
- Low forgetting with low absolute accuracy (CMP 2026, feedback-Hebbian 2026) is the typical profile of 2026 local-rule continual learners; forgetting metrics alone overstate how well they work.

### Gaps
- FlyModel's own benchmark numbers and the exact form of its two perceptron modifications were not read from the full paper; the 0.82 figure is the SDM paper's re-implementation.
- No class-incremental numbers were verified for metaplasticity methods; Laborieux et al. were only seen via preprint snippets.
- Exact BI-R (without SI), generative replay, and joint-training numbers for class-incremental CIFAR-100 are only in figures of van de Ven et al. 2020 and were not extracted.
- A July 2026 preprints.org review on backprop-free continual learning surfaced in search but returned HTTP 403; its contents are unverified. — [preprints.org 202607.0555](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202607.0555)

## 3. Large-scale brain-like cognitive architectures: what each achieved and how it was received

### Takeaway
Each whole-brain-style architecture bought one thing at the cost of another: Spaun got many tasks from one spiking model by solving for its weights offline (it does not learn new tasks); Leabra/emergent got local error-driven learning in many region-specific models but stayed at small scale; HTM and its Thousand Brains successor got fast, sparse, local, continual learning but narrow benchmarks; the assembly-calculus line got provable Hebbian word and syntax learning but only for tiny grounded lexicons. None is competitive with deep learning on standard benchmarks, and the most common criticism of each is exactly the piece it left out.

### Cited Findings

**Spaun / Semantic Pointer Architecture (Nengo)**
- [S] Spaun (Eliasmith et al., Science, Nov 2012): about 2.5 million spiking neurons, "8 billion connections", functional groups mapped to brain areas (vision, working memory, basal-ganglia action selection, motor arm), eight tasks from image sequences. — [Choo 2018 thesis record](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/handle/10012/13308); [phys.org 2012](https://phys.org/news/2012-11-spaun-human-brain-simulator-tasks.html); primary: [Eliasmith et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225266)
- [S] Spaun 2.0 (Choo, PhD thesis, Waterloo, 2018): "approximately 6.6 million neurons, can perform 12 cognitive tasks", adds general instruction following, motor adaptation to unknown force fields, and 256x256 colour vision. — [Choo 2018](https://www.compneuro.uwaterloo.ca/publications/choo2018.html)
- [S] Reception/limits: weights are obtained by least-squares optimization rather than a biological learning process, which a philosophy-of-mind commentary called a serious flaw; it "can only carry out the tasks given it and cannot learn anything new"; press reported about 2.5 hours of compute per second of behaviour in 2012; the authors acknowledge it says little about development. — [philosophyofbrains.com commentary](https://philosophyofbrains.com/?p=98); [Nature news 2012](https://www.nature.com/doifinder/10.1038/nature.2012.11914); [Globe and Mail 2012](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/amp/technology/science/brain/meet-spaun-the-worlds-biggest-brain-that-works/article7260055/)

**Leabra / emergent (and successors)**
- [S] Leabra combines "a biologically-plausible form of error backpropagation" (local activation-difference learning, O'Reilly 1996) with Hebbian associative learning and inhibitory competition; the architecture's 20 principles are set out in O'Reilly, Hazy & Herd (Oxford Handbook chapter, 2016/2017). — [O'Reilly, Hazy & Herd](https://ccnlab.org/papers/OReillyHazyHerd16.pdf)
- [R] The family includes region-specific models that map onto the "organs" in the brief: hippocampal episodic memory (Norman & O'Reilly 2003; Theremin 2022), and prefrontal/basal-ganglia working-memory gating trained by dopamine-like reward signals (PBWM; O'Reilly & Frank, Neural Computation 2006). — [O'Reilly & Frank 2006](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976606775093909)
- [V] The Singh et al. 2022 hippocampus-cortex sleep model (section 1) is implemented in emergent. — [Singh et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9636926/fullTextXML)

**Hierarchical Temporal Memory and the Thousand Brains Project**
- [R] HTM theory: neurons with many dendritic segments acting as independent pattern detectors that put the cell in a predictive state; sparse distributed representations; online Hebbian-style learning on segments (Hawkins & Ahmad, Frontiers in Neural Circuits, 2016); sequence memory that learns continuously (Cui, Ahmad & Hawkins, Neural Computation, 2016). — [Hawkins & Ahmad 2016](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2016.00023); [arXiv 1512.05463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05463)
- [S] HTM's best-known benchmark result is anomaly detection on the Numenta Anomaly Benchmark: HTM scored 70.1 (first) vs. 69.9 and 64.6 for the next entries — figures from Numenta's own work. An independent 2016 re-implementation argued that "the reported performance of HTM on NAB can be explained with only first-order transition memory" and that higher-order sequence memory "has not been demonstrated to have a benefit on any real problem" (informal blog replication). — [Papers with Code table](https://www.paperswithcode.com/paper/unsupervised-real-time-anomaly-detection-for); [floybix 2016](https://floybix.github.io/2016/07/01/attempting-nab); [NAB paper, arXiv 1510.03336](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03336)
- [V] Thousand Brains Project, "Monty" (white paper arXiv Dec 2024; first quantitative paper Leadholm, Clay, Knudstrup, Lee & Hawkins, arXiv 6 Jul 2025). Learning modules bind sensed features to locations in object reference frames — described as an instantaneous associative operation, "the simplest form of Hebbian learning" — with path integration of movement; updates are sparse and local. On 77 YCB objects: 98.6% recognition with 0° median rotation error on learned rotations; 95.1% / 3° with feature noise; 93.0% / 4.5° on novel rotations; 88.1% / 6° with noise + novel rotations; 73.1% / 7° with noise + novel rotations + all objects recoloured. — [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1); [white paper arXiv 2412.18354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18354)
- [V] Monty vs. ViT: with one view per object, Monty about 50% vs. about 30% for a from-scratch ViT (chance 1.3%); after 8 views per object, 88% accuracy; a ViT pre-trained on 14M images matches Monty's classification learning efficiency but not pose. In a 77-task continual setting Monty shows "only minor degradation" from similar-object interference while the pre-trained ViT forgets catastrophically (exact figures in plots). About 4M parameters vs. 86M; training uses about 34,000x fewer FLOPs than the from-scratch ViT (the text's pre-trained-ViT factor is given both as 528,000,000x and "nine orders of magnitude", which are inconsistent). — [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1)
- [V] Monty limits stated by authors: "still in a nascent stage of development"; only 3D object perception evaluated; learning was supervised (unsupervised learning "beyond the scope"); no compositional/hierarchical objects, object behaviours, or world-changing actions; inference cost grows linearly with the number of stored object models. — [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1)

**Assembly calculus / NEMO (neuronal assemblies)**
- [S] Papadimitriou, Vempala, Mitropolsky, Collins & Maass (PNAS, 2020): a formal model of brain areas with random connectivity, k-winners-take-all inhibition and multiplicative Hebbian plasticity, with operations on assemblies such as project, associate and merge. — [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10208049); primary: [Papadimitriou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2001893117)
- [S] Mitropolsky, Collins & Papadimitriou (TACL 2021), "A biologically plausible parser": each word is a pre-existing assembly in a LEX area; the system parses English sentences by assembly operations. Dabagia, Papadimitriou & Vempala (2021/COLT 2022) asked "How can the brain learn through assembly representations?" and showed assemblies can learn to classify well-separated classes. A 2024 extension has assemblies record statistics and use ambient noise to make probabilistic choices, reproducing low-order statistics of a small corpus. — [arXiv 2108.02189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.02189v1.pdf); [arXiv 2110.03171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.03171v2); [arXiv 2406.07715](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07715)
- [V] Mitropolsky & Papadimitriou (arXiv 27 Jun 2023), "The Architecture of a Biologically Plausible Language Organ": areas LexN, LexV, Phon, Visual, Motor and about 10 context areas; n = 10^6 neurons per area with cap k = 10^3 in the main example (a figure run uses n = 10^5, k = 50-100, p = 0.05); weight multiplied by (1+β) when pre fires at t and post at t+1, β = 0.06-0.1. From two-word grounded sentences (noun activates a visual assembly, verb a motor assembly, with the phonological assembly) it learns which words are nouns and verbs and forms stable word assemblies linked to meaning. Training sentences grow roughly linearly with lexicon size; the authors consider 10-20 sentences per word plausible; individual-word tutoring cut training time by over 40% early on. Limits stated: proof of concept; main example lexicon of 5 words; no abstract words, function words, syntax, generation or transitive verbs; phonology pre-initialized; synchronous firing. — [arXiv 2306.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15364v1)
- [V] Mitropolsky & Papadimitriou (arXiv/bioRxiv 15 Jul 2025), "Simulated Language Acquisition in a Biologically Realistic Model of the Brain": starting from no knowledge, the system learns word meanings, noun/verb category, word order, and generation of novel sentences "from a modest number of grounded sentences", using six principles: excitatory neurons, brain areas, random synapses, Hebbian plasticity, local inhibition, inter-area inhibition. [S] Word order is carried by plastic synapses between ROLE areas and SUBJ/VERB/OBJ areas with mutual inhibition selecting the next constituent; results seen cover 3-word sentences. — [arXiv 2507.11788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11788); [bioRxiv copy](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.15.664996v1.full.pdf)

**Sparse distributed memory and modern Hopfield networks as episodic memory**
- [R] Kanerva's SDM (1988) stores patterns across many hard locations activated by proximity in a high-dimensional binary space; Bricken & Pehlevan (NeurIPS 2021) showed Transformer attention closely approximates SDM read/write, and noted SDM's mapping onto cerebellar circuitry. — [arXiv 2111.05498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05498)
- [R] Dense associative memories (Krotov & Hopfield, 2016) and "Hopfield Networks is All You Need" (Ramsauer et al., 2020) give Hopfield-type stores with capacity super-linear (up to exponential) in dimension and one-step retrieval equivalent to attention; storage is one-shot (write the pattern). — [arXiv 1606.01164](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01164); [arXiv 2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)
- [S][V] Use as hippocampus: Spens & Burgess 2024 and Jun et al. 2025 (section 1) both use a modern Hopfield network as the one-shot episodic store feeding a slow generative cortex; a 2025 "Latent Structured Hopfield Network" combines a Hopfield attractor with an autoencoder, trained end-to-end, for episodic recall under occlusion and noise. — [Spens & Burgess 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01799-z.pdf); [arXiv 2507.11393](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11393v1); [arXiv 2506.01303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01303)

**Global-workspace-style architectures**
- [S] Dossa et al. (Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience, 2024): an embodied agent built to satisfy global-workspace indicator properties, navigating a realistic 3D multimodal environment, "performs better and more robustly than a standard recurrent architecture at smaller working memory sizes"; larger workspaces did not help significantly. — [Dossa et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncom.2024.1352685)
- [S] Chateau-Laurent & VanRullen (arXiv Mar 2025), "Learning to Chain Operations by Routing Information": a workspace-style router with fewer parameters than LSTMs/Transformers outperforms them on unseen addition operations. A Dec 2025 preprint found the broadcast mechanism "also amplifies noise", making the system fragile without higher-order control. — [arXiv 2503.01906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01906v2); [arXiv 2512.19155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19155)
- [R] Earlier: Goyal et al. (ICLR 2022) shared-workspace bottleneck among modules; VanRullen & Kanai (Trends in Neurosciences, 2021) roadmap for a "global latent workspace". All of these are trained with backprop. — [arXiv 2103.01197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01197); [arXiv 2012.10390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10390)

**Action selection among memory routes (copy / recall / habit)**
- [R] Daw, Niv & Dayan (Nature Neuroscience, 2005): arbitration between a habitual (model-free) and a deliberative controller by their relative uncertainty. Blundell et al. (2016, "Model-Free Episodic Control") and Pritzel et al. (2017, "Neural Episodic Control"): a hippocampus-like table of one-shot stored outcomes drives action early in learning, far more sample-efficiently than slow value learning, and is overtaken by the slow learner later. — [Daw et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1560); [arXiv 1606.04460](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04460); [arXiv 1703.01988](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01988)
- [R] Three-factor rules (pre x post x neuromodulator, with eligibility traces) are the standard local formulation of reward-modulated learning; reviewed by Frémaux & Gerstner (Frontiers in Neural Circuits, 2016). — [Frémaux & Gerstner 2016](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2015.00085)
- [S] "NeuroSynth" (arXiv Jul 2026): a continual-RL architecture with a hippocampal-inspired "plan" pathway and a cortical-inspired "habit" pathway. — [arXiv 2607.28663](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28663)

### Inferences
- By component, the nearest published relatives of the reference system are: assembly calculus/NEMO (word cells learned by Hebbian plasticity from grounded input; closest overall on the "acquires words" axis); HTM/Monty (dendritic segments, sparse codes, one-shot associative binding, continual learning); Leabra-family models (hippocampus + cortex + reward-gated basal ganglia with local error-driven rules); FlyModel/SDM (sparse expansion + delta/perceptron rule); episodic-control RL (one-shot store competing with a slow habit system). No single published system found combines all five organs with growth/pruning.
- Reception has a common shape: systems that hand-specify structure (Spaun weights, NEMO's pre-wired areas and pre-initialized phonology, Monty's supervised object labels and built-in reference frames) are criticized for what is engineered rather than learned; systems that learn everything locally stay small.
- NEMO's reported sample cost (about 10-20 grounded sentences per word, linear in lexicon size, with tutoring on single words cutting time by over 40%) is the only published quantitative yardstick found for Hebbian word learning; it also supports curricula that mix single-word tutoring with sentences.

### Gaps
- Spaun's per-task accuracies and the Science paper's own limitations section were not read; Spaun details are from secondary coverage and the Spaun 2.0 thesis abstract.
- No independent peer-reviewed evaluation of HTM sequence memory or of Monty was found; all Monty numbers are from the developers' own preprint (not yet peer reviewed as far as this search found).
- The 2025 NEMO paper's lexicon sizes and sentence counts could not be extracted (PDF not parseable here); only the abstract was verified.
- No reception/critique literature specific to the assembly-calculus language work was found.
- Leabra has no single headline benchmark; no quantitative comparison of Leabra-family models against deep learning was located.

## 4. Networks that grow: neurogenesis-inspired and constructive networks, structural plasticity, self-organizing incremental networks, growing neural gas

### Takeaway
Growing a network is an old and repeatedly successful way to add capacity without overwriting — growing neural gas (1994/95), grow-when-required, SOINN, cascade-correlation, progressive networks — but in practice it is used for prototype/topology learning on fixed features or with task labels; recent (2023-2026) work shows growth itself can cause forgetting and needs a trigger and a protection mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Fritzke, "A Growing Neural Gas Network Learns Topologies" (NIPS 1994, published 1995): an incremental network that learns topology with a Hebb-like rule (competitive Hebbian edge creation), has "no parameters which change over time and is able to continue learning, adding units and connections, until a performance criterion is met". — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1994/hash/d56b9fc4b0f1be8871f5e1c40c0067e7-Abstract.html)
- [S] GNG supports node deletion as well as insertion; a 2018 extension (Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery) adds three mechanisms so the network tracks both gradual and sudden distribution changes in evolving streams; growing-only variants suit incremental learning, grow-and-shrink variants suit streaming data. — [Springer 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-018-0571-0); [Wikipedia: Neural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_gas)
- [R] Grow-When-Required (Marsland, Shapiro & Nehmzow, Neural Networks 2002) inserts a node when the best-matching unit's activity is below a threshold and it is already habituated (i.e., novelty that existing units cannot absorb), rather than on a fixed schedule; SOINN (Furao & Hasegawa, Neural Networks 2006) adds noise-node removal and a two-layer structure for online unsupervised clustering without a preset number of nodes. — [Marsland et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(02)00078-3); [Furao & Hasegawa 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2005.04.006)
- [R] Parisi, Tani, Weber & Wermter (Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 2018): a CLS-inspired "growing dual-memory" system — an episodic recurrent GWR network that grows quickly and a semantic one that grows slowly using labels, with replay of episodic trajectories — evaluated on CORe50 incremental object recognition; Parisi et al.'s 2019 Neural Networks review ("Continual lifelong learning with neural networks") surveys growth-based approaches alongside regularization and replay. — [arXiv 1805.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10966); [arXiv 1802.07569](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07569)
- [R] Deep-learning constructive methods: Progressive Neural Networks (Rusu et al., 2016) add a new column per task with lateral connections and freeze old columns (zero forgetting, linear growth, needs task identity); Dynamically Expandable Networks (Yoon et al., 2017/ICLR 2018) selectively retrain, expand and split units; "Neurogenesis Deep Learning" (Draelos et al., 2017) adds units to an autoencoder when reconstruction error on new classes is high and protects old knowledge with "intrinsic replay". — [arXiv 1606.04671](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04671); [arXiv 1708.01547](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01547); [arXiv 1612.03770](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03770)
- [R] Structural plasticity as prune-and-regrow of connections at fixed size: Sparse Evolutionary Training (Mocanu et al., Nature Communications 2018) and RigL (Evci et al., ICML 2020) match dense-network accuracy at high sparsity by periodically removing weak connections and growing new ones. — [Mocanu et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04316-3); [arXiv 1911.11134](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11134)
- [S] Self-Expanding Neural Networks (arXiv Jul 2023): a neurogenesis-inspired expansion score decides when, where and what to add during training. — [arXiv 2307.04526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04526v1)
- [S] "Overcoming Growth-Induced Forgetting with Sparsity in Task-Agnostic Continual Learning" (SparseGrow, arXiv Aug 2024): identifies forgetting caused by the act of growing itself and counters it with layer expansion plus gradient gating to keep updates sparse; domain- and class-incremental settings. — [arXiv 2408.10566](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10566v5)
- [S] NORACL (arXiv Apr 2026): starts compact and adds neurons only when internal signals show representational or plasticity saturation, paired with online EWC; reported accuracy comparable to or better than oracle-sized fixed networks with fewer parameters (secondary summaries). — [arXiv 2604.27031 mirror](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2604.27031v1); [summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/noracl-neurogenesis-for-oracle-free-resource-adaptive-continual-learning)
- [V] Counter-evidence on pruning: in the SDM continual learner, pruning weights (randomly or by smallest magnitude) "caused catastrophic forgetting". — [arXiv 2303.11934 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11934)
- [V] Monty grows by adding object models; about 4M parameters after learning all 77 YCB objects, with inference cost linear in the number of stored models — the authors propose future model-merging rules to curb this. — [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1)

### Inferences
- The well-tested growth triggers are all novelty/saturation signals local to the network (GWR: low best-match activity in an already-habituated unit; Neurogenesis DL: high reconstruction error; NORACL: saturation), not schedules. Growth by a fixed schedule has little support in this literature.
- Growing-prototype systems (GNG/GWR/SOINN/Monty) get one-shot learning and no forgetting essentially by allocating new units, and their documented weakness is unbounded growth and inference cost; this is why every such line eventually adds deletion or merging.
- Two verified cautions apply to grow-and-prune designs: growth can itself induce forgetting (SparseGrow) and pruning by weight magnitude can destroy old memories in sparse-code systems (SDMLP).

### Gaps
- No recent (2024-2026) quantitative benchmark was verified for GNG/GWR/SOINN-style systems on standard class-incremental tasks; CORe50 numbers for the growing dual-memory model were not re-checked and are omitted.
- NORACL and SENN numbers were seen only in secondary summaries.
- No source found on growth and pruning in a fully local-rule multi-area system beyond the prototype networks above.

## 5. Dendritic computation models used for learning (multi-compartment neurons, dendritic error signalling, burst-dependent plasticity): results and scaling

### Takeaway
Two distinct uses of dendrites exist in the literature: dendrites as error-carrying compartments that let local rules approximate backprop (reaches CIFAR-10 comfortably, ImageNet poorly), and dendrites as context-gating or nonlinear sub-units that add capacity and reduce interference (works well, but usually trained with backprop). In February 2026 the first direct experimental evidence of neuron-specific, signed instructive signals in apical dendrites was published in Nature.

### Cited Findings
- [R] Single neurons with dendrites compute far more than a point unit: Poirazi, Brannon & Mel (Neuron, 2003) showed a pyramidal cell behaves like a two-layer network of sigmoidal dendritic subunits; Beniaguev, Segev & London (Neuron, 2021) needed a temporally convolutional network of about 5-8 layers to reproduce a layer-5 pyramidal neuron's input-output function, with NMDA nonlinearity the main cause of the depth. — [Poirazi et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(03)00149-1); [Beniaguev et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.07.002)
- [R] Urbanczik & Senn (Neuron, 2014): a two-compartment neuron in which the dendrite learns to predict somatic spiking; the local mismatch between dendritic prediction and somatic activity drives a delta-rule-like update. This is the canonical "delta rule on a dendritic compartment". — [Urbanczik & Senn 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.030)
- [R] Guerguiev, Lillicrap & Richards (eLife, 2017), "Towards deep learning with segregated dendrites": feedback arriving at a separate apical compartment supplies a local teaching signal; demonstrated on MNIST with hidden layers improving over a single-layer network. — [Guerguiev et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.22901)
- [S] Sacramento, Costa, Bengio & Senn (NeurIPS 2018), dendritic cortical microcircuits: errors arise at apical dendrites as the mismatch between lateral-interneuron predictions and top-down feedback, approximating backprop without separate phases. — [arXiv 1810.11393](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.11393v1.pdf)
- [V] Payeur, Guerguiev, Zenke, Richards & Naud (Nature Neuroscience, 2021; bioRxiv 2020), burst-dependent plasticity: bursts multiplex top-down error with bottom-up events. Ensemble-level deep network results: CIFAR-10 test error 20.1% after 400 epochs ("similar to" gradient descent in the same network); ImageNet top-5 error 56.1% — much better than fixed feedback weights, but with a remaining gap the authors attribute to not being able to use recurrent linearizing input at conv layers for memory reasons. Stated limits: results are for ensemble (population-averaged) units, need validation in detailed spiking simulations, supervised static inputs only, abstract gating term. — [bioRxiv 2020.03.30.015511](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.03.30.015511v2.full)
- [S] BurstCCN (Greedy et al., NeurIPS 2022): burst multiplexing via connection-type-specific short-term plasticity, single-phase learning, propagates backprop-like errors across multiple layers; Capone et al. (ICML 2022) use bursts and dendritic segregation for target-based learning, tested on a 3D trajectory task and a "button & food" task rather than image benchmarks. — [arXiv 2206.11769](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.11769.pdf); [Capone et al. 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/capone22b.html)
- [V] Dendrites as context gates: Active Dendrites Networks (section 2) — 91.6% on 100-task permuted MNIST with SI, 87.5% vs. 76.6% on Meta-World MT10; trained with backprop. — [Iyer et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2022.846219/full)
- [S] Chavlis & Poirazi (Nature Communications, 2025), "Dendrites endow artificial neural networks with accurate, robust and parameter-efficient learning": dendritic ANNs with structured, restricted connectivity are more accurate, far more parameter-efficient and more robust on several image benchmarks — trained with backprop. A NeurIPS 2026 listing ("Dendritic Structure Enables Parameter-Efficient Neural Representations") reports a DendriNet layer on ImageNet-1k (status not verified). — [Chavlis & Poirazi 2025 record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-025-56297-9.html); [arXiv 2404.03708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.03708v1); [NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157281)
- [S] Experimental evidence: Francioni, Tang, Toloza, Brown & Harnett (Nature, 25 Feb 2026; bioRxiv 2023), "Vectorized instructive signals in cortical dendrites during a brain-computer interface task": in mouse retrosplenial layer-5 neurons, two populations controlling a BCI in opposite directions received opposing error-like signals in their distal apical dendrites; optogenetically activating layer-1 NDNF+ interneurons disrupted the dendritic signals and impaired learning. Only about 8-10 neurons were directly tied to the BCI; presented as first evidence of a neuron-specific ("vectorized") teaching signal. — [MIT News, Mar 2026](https://news.mit.edu/2026/neurons-receive-precisely-tailored-teaching-signals-as-we-learn-0309); [McGovern Institute](https://mcgovern.mit.edu/2026/02/25/neurons-learn)

### Inferences
- A delta rule on dendrite-like compartments has solid single-layer precedent (Urbanczik-Senn; HTM segments; Active Dendrites) and now direct biological support for the idea that the error is delivered per neuron to dendrites. What the literature has not shown is that such rules scale through depth: the best verified ImageNet number for a dendritic local rule is 56.1% top-5 error (2020/21), with CIFAR-10 at about 80% accuracy.
- The successful dendritic systems for continual learning use dendrites for gating by context rather than for carrying error. A system that uses compartments for both is not something found in this search.
- The 2026 Nature result strengthens the case for supervised/teacher-driven local rules (as opposed to purely Hebbian ones) as biologically legitimate.

### Gaps
- No post-2022 result was found scaling burst-dependent or dendritic-error learning to competitive ImageNet accuracy; the search for "dendritic localized learning without backprop scaling" returned nothing relevant.
- Sacramento et al. and Guerguiev et al. MNIST error figures were not re-verified and are omitted.
- The Francioni et al. Nature paper was read only through institutional news coverage.

## 6. Decorrelation / whitening as a biologically plausible mechanism (lateral inhibition, anti-Hebbian learning, similarity matching networks)

### Takeaway
Hebbian feedforward plus anti-Hebbian lateral learning is the best-theorized local mechanism in the field — it is derivable from a similarity-matching objective and provably performs online PCA/whitening — and decorrelating inputs demonstrably speeds learning in deep networks (2024). Purely Hebbian competitive feature learning with soft winner-take-all reaches about 80% on CIFAR-10 but only 27% on ImageNet (2023).

### Cited Findings
- [R] Földiák (Biological Cybernetics, 1990), "Forming sparse representations by local anti-Hebbian learning": Hebbian feedforward weights + anti-Hebbian lateral inhibitory weights + adaptive thresholds yield sparse, decorrelated codes; the origin of this design. — [Földiák 1990](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02331346)
- [S] Pehlevan & Chklovskii (Allerton 2015), "Optimization theory of Hebbian/anti-Hebbian networks for PCA and whitening": adding a decorrelating term to the similarity-matching objective yields online PCA and whitening algorithms implemented by single-layer networks with local rules; the theory predicts under-used neurons dropping out, and motivates whitening by information transmission under output noise (as in retinal centre-surround fields). — [arXiv 1511.09468](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.09468)
- [S] Pehlevan, Sengupta & Chklovskii (Neural Computation, 2018; arXiv 2017), "Why do similarity matching objectives lead to Hebbian/anti-Hebbian networks?": variable substitution turns the objective into a min-max problem that formalizes the competition between Hebbian feedforward and anti-Hebbian lateral rules; introduces Principal Subspace Whitening; anti-Hebbian lateral connections keep neurons' feature vectors from aligning. — [arXiv 1703.07914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.07914)
- [S] Lipshutz, Pehlevan & Chklovskii (ICLR 2023): in linear recurrent Hebbian/anti-Hebbian whitening networks, mediating the lateral interaction through interneurons makes synaptic dynamics converge much faster than direct lateral connections — "logarithmic versus linear convergence times". — [arXiv 2209.10634](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.10634v2.pdf)
- [S] Dalm, Offergeld, Ahmad & van Gerven (arXiv May 2024), "Efficient Deep Learning with Decorrelated Backpropagation": a local decorrelating lateral weight matrix at each layer's input gives "a more than two-fold speed-up and higher test accuracy compared to backpropagation" for networks up to an 18- to 50-layer ResNet; the authors caution that decorrelation has overhead so epoch-level speed-ups are not automatically wall-clock speed-ups. — [arXiv 2405.02385](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02385v5)
- [V] Journé, Garcia Rodriguez, Guo & Moraitis (ICLR 2023, notable top 25%), SoftHebb: soft winner-take-all Hebbian learning with no feedback, targets or error signals, up to five hidden layers plus a linear classifier: MNIST 99.4%, CIFAR-10 80.3%, STL-10 76.2%, ImageNet 27.3%. — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883)
- [R] Krotov & Hopfield (PNAS, 2019), "Unsupervised learning by competing hidden units": a local competitive rule with global inhibition (anti-Hebbian for runner-up units) learns a hidden layer whose features, with a supervised top layer, gave about 1.46% test error on MNIST and roughly 50% accuracy on CIFAR-10 — close to an end-to-end backprop network of the same single-hidden-layer shape. — [arXiv 1806.10181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10181)
- [R] Diehl & Cook (Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience, 2015): STDP + lateral inhibition + adaptive thresholds, unsupervised, about 95% on MNIST with 6,400 excitatory neurons. — [Diehl & Cook 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2015.00099)
- [S] 2025 follow-ups in vision: Hebbian CNNs with improved biological plausibility (Neural Networks, 2025) and SPHeRe (arXiv Oct 2025), a Hebbian-inspired unsupervised method claiming competitiveness within modern deep-learning frameworks. — [arXiv 2501.17266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17266); [arXiv 2510.14810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14810v1)
- [S] Counterpoint (PLOS Computational Biology, 2023, EPFL): in a spiking STDP model individual neurons can learn localized oriented receptive fields "circumventing the need for input preprocessing, such as whitening, or population-level lateral inhibition". — [EPFL record](https://infosciences.epfl.ch/record/309568/files/journal.pcbi.1011844.pdf)
- [S] Caution on depth (arXiv Jul 2026 preprint on local rules implementing a SIGReg-style objective): when decorrelation is applied at every layer, identity covariance is a fixed point of the update, which may hinder learning in depth (fragment only). — [arXiv 2607.21622](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21622)
- [V] In sleep-replay consolidation, representations of different classes became "more sparse and less correlated" after sleep, i.e., decorrelation accompanies reduced interference. — [Tadros et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9755223/fullTextXML)

### Inferences
- A competitive-learning cortex with decorrelation is the single best-supported organ of the reference system: there is a normative derivation (similarity matching), convergence theory, and positive deep-network evidence that decorrelated inputs speed learning downstream.
- Verified scale limit: unsupervised Hebbian/WTA feature hierarchies plateau around 80% CIFAR-10 and 27% ImageNet (2023). Expect a Hebbian sensory cortex to be an adequate front end for small visual vocabularies and a bottleneck beyond that.
- The Lipshutz et al. result suggests that if lateral decorrelation is slow to settle, routing it through a small interneuron population rather than direct lateral weights is the theoretically faster arrangement (shown only for linear networks).
- Decorrelation interacts with continual learning in both directions: decorrelated/sparse codes reduce interference (Tadros; FlyModel; SDM), but a continually adapting decorrelating stage changes the code that downstream associative and episodic stores were keyed on.

### Gaps
- No dedicated review article on biologically plausible whitening/decorrelation was found; the material is primary papers.
- Krotov & Hopfield and Diehl & Cook numbers are recalled, not re-verified.
- No source was found that measures how a continually plastic decorrelating layer affects downstream memory stability; this interaction appears unstudied.

## 7. 2024-2026 work combining several of these into one learner that acquires words or language; explicit assessments of whether integrated, emergent, non-backprop systems are a promising path

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 the only published line that acquires words and basic syntax in a brain-like, backprop-free system is the assembly-calculus/NEMO work (2023, 2025), and it does so with tiny grounded lexicons and short sentences; other 2025-2026 "brain-like language" systems either train with backprop or reach low absolute quality. Assessments agree that local rules still trail backprop at scale but note the gap narrowing for predictive-coding/equilibrium methods (ImageNet top-5 error 13.2% vs. 12.2% for backprop in a 2026 result), and name continual/online learning and efficiency — not accuracy — as where such systems may pay off.

### Cited Findings

**Integrated word/language learners**
- [V] NEMO language organ (Mitropolsky & Papadimitriou, arXiv Jun 2023): Hebbian plasticity, "without backpropagation"; learns noun/verb category and word-meaning assemblies from grounded two-word sentences; about 10-20 sentences per word; 5-word main example; no syntax or generation yet. — [arXiv 2306.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15364v1)
- [V] Successor (arXiv/bioRxiv 15 Jul 2025): adds word order and generation of novel sentences from "a modest number of grounded sentences", for any word order; six stated principles (excitatory neurons, areas, random synapses, Hebbian plasticity, local inhibition, inter-area inhibition). [S] Results seen cover 3-word sentences. — [arXiv 2507.11788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11788)
- [S] Eugenio (arXiv Mar 2025), "Hebbian learning the local structure of language": a hierarchy of neurons that learns to tokenize words from text by Hebbian learning, plus a second stage binding tokens into embeddings — not grounded and not a whole-system model. — [arXiv 2503.02057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02057)
- [V] "Dragon Hatchling" (BDH; Kosowski et al., Pathway, arXiv 30 Sep 2025): a language model formulated as a scale-free graph of locally interacting neuron particles, with working memory at inference relying on "synaptic plasticity with Hebbian learning using spiking neurons"; claims to rival GPT-2 on language and translation at 10M-1B parameters with sparse, positive, monosemantic activations. The abstract does not state the training method; it has a GPU-friendly attention-like formulation, so it should not be assumed backprop-free. — [arXiv 2509.26507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26507)
- [V] CMP (arXiv Jul 2026; section 2): local-rule sparse-code + two-tier competitive memory + delta-rule readout, continual byte-level language modelling; about 95% less forgetting than a fine-tuned Transformer but about 3.1-3.3 bits/byte, and no benefit from depth. — [arXiv 2607.26523](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26523v1)
- [V] Monty (2025): the nearest sensorimotor whole-system relative — Hebbian-like associative binding, rapid and continual learning — but perception only, supervised, no language. — [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1)
- [S] Other 2025-2026 hippocampus-inspired continual learners found (not language, mostly backprop-trained or unverified): HiCL (dentate-gyrus-style top-k sparsity module, arXiv Aug 2025); NeuroSynth (hippocampal "plan" + cortical "habit" pathways for continual RL, arXiv Jul 2026). — [arXiv 2508.16651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16651); [arXiv 2607.28663](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28663)

**Assessments of the path**
- [R] Bartunov et al. (NeurIPS 2018), "Assessing the scalability of biologically-motivated deep learning algorithms and architectures": target propagation and feedback-alignment variants did well on MNIST/CIFAR but fell far behind backprop on ImageNet, especially in locally connected architectures — the standard citation for "local rules do not scale". — [arXiv 1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04587)
- [R] Lillicrap, Santoro, Marris, Akerman & Hinton (Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 2020), "Backpropagation and the brain": argue the brain likely approximates gradient-based credit assignment using feedback-induced activity differences, rather than learning with purely Hebbian rules. — [Lillicrap et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3)
- [S] A 2024 comparison of biologically plausible methods (arXiv 2406.16062) reports that the most biologically plausible rules (Hebbian, STDP) generally perform worse than backprop on image classification, with predictive coding and Forward-Forward closest to brain activity patterns, and lists scaling without loss of biological accuracy as an open problem (seen via third-party summary only). — [summary page](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/towards-biologically-plausible-computing-a-comprehensive-comparison)
- [S] Scaling progress 2025-2026: μPC (Innocenti, Achour & Buckley, NeurIPS 2025) trains 100+ layer (up to 128-layer) residual predictive-coding networks stably "on simple classification tasks"; a mid-2025 preprint trains a 15-hidden-layer PC model on Tiny ImageNet with two regularizers; a NeurIPS 2026 listing reports a 10-layer convolutional PCN (VGG10) trained on full ImageNet with a centered Equilibrium Propagation variant at 13.23% top-5 error vs. 12.2% for backprop, described as the first ImageNet-scale PCN/EP result. — [μPC, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7ea5ad70c984262c064ee9859f7e5f56-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2506.23800](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23800); [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/155056)
- [S] "Hebbian Learning with Global Direction" (arXiv Jan 2026) states that relying only on local information without global task objectives limits scalability, and proposes neuromodulator-like global signals as the remedy. — [arXiv 2601.21367](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2601.21367)
- [V] Etter (arXiv 6 Nov 2025), "The brain as a blueprint: a survey of brain-inspired approaches to learning in AI": deep learning's past successes came from brain-like features, but standard models rely on "biologically implausible error propagation algorithms" and "struggle to accumulate knowledge incrementally"; the true synaptic learning rule remains unknown; recent neuroscience on error computation, propagation and synaptic integration is presented as a source of further progress. — [arXiv 2511.04455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04455)
- [S] Schmidgall et al. (arXiv 2023), "Brain-inspired learning in artificial neural networks: a review": surveys local learning, neuromodulation/three-factor rules and meta-learned plasticity with the aim of "more adaptive, energy-efficient, and continuously learning AI systems". — [arXiv 2305.11252](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11252)
- [S] One researcher's public position (blog-level, 2025-2026): large searches over thousands of candidate learning rules failed to beat backprop on ImageNet, and backprop may itself be biologically implementable through vector error feedback — the view the Feb 2026 Nature dendrite paper is cited to support. — [aggregator post](https://digg.com/ai/epc4jimo); [MIT News 2026](https://news.mit.edu/2026/neurons-receive-precisely-tailored-teaching-signals-as-we-learn-0309)
- [R] Field-level position papers: Zador et al. (Nature Communications, 2023), "Catalyzing next-generation AI through NeuroAI" (argues for embodied, sensorimotor benchmarks and learning from animal-like efficiency); Kudithipudi et al. (Nature Machine Intelligence, 2022), "Biological underpinnings for lifelong learning machines" (lists neurogenesis, neuromodulation, metaplasticity, context-dependent gating, replay/sleep and multisensory integration as the mechanisms to combine); Ororbia (2023), survey of neurobiologically plausible credit assignment. — [Zador et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37180-x); [Kudithipudi et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00452-0); [arXiv 2312.09257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09257)

### Inferences
- A system that learns grounded words online with only local rules across several interacting organs has one direct published precedent (NEMO), and that precedent is at the scale of a handful to dozens of words and 2-3-word sentences, with phonology and area wiring pre-specified. Results at or beyond that scale with an emergent (rather than pre-wired) structure would be in largely unoccupied territory — which also means there is no established benchmark to compare against.
- The literature's verdict is split by goal. For matching backprop accuracy at scale, purely Hebbian/local systems are assessed negatively (Bartunov 2018; 2024 comparison; 27% ImageNet for SoftHebb), and the methods that are closing the gap (predictive coding, equilibrium propagation, dendritic error) are approximations of gradient descent rather than emergent Hebbian systems. For continual, one-shot, low-compute learning, local/sparse/associative systems show real, verified advantages (Monty; FlyModel; SDM; sleep replay), and that is where reviews place their promise.
- The mechanisms Kudithipudi et al. (2022) list as needing to be combined (neurogenesis, neuromodulation, metaplasticity, context gating, replay/sleep) coincide almost exactly with the organs of the reference system; published work has combined at most two or three at a time.
- Judging such a system by generalization and structure rather than benchmark accuracy is consistent with how its nearest relatives (NEMO, Monty, Singh et al.) present their own results — but those papers are also criticized or self-limited precisely for small scale, so claims should be scoped the same way.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2025-2026 position paper was found that specifically evaluates integrated, multi-organ, emergent, non-backprop learners as a research programme; the assessments found concern local learning rules in general.
- Whether BDH is trained with backprop was not confirmed from its full text.
- The 2026 EP-on-ImageNet result and the 2024 biologically-plausible-methods comparison were seen only via listing/secondary summary.
- No 2024-2026 work was found that combines a plastic Hebbian visual cortex, dendritic associative cells, an episodic store, reward-modulated route selection and growth/pruning in one language-acquiring learner; the search returned no result matching the full combination (searches run Nov 2026 on generic terms; an unindexed or differently-named system could exist).
- BabyLM-style developmentally plausible language-model work (small-data training) was not searched; it is backprop-based and outside the local-rule scope, but may hold relevant curriculum findings.
