# Publishing venues and reception for an independent researcher's backprop-free, developmentally staged learning paper (status: 18 Nov 2026)

Reading note: every policy line carries the date of the statement where the source gives one. "Official" means the venue's own page was read in this session; "secondary" means press, aggregator or third-party mirror. Deadlines for 2027 cycles were mostly not yet posted on 18 Nov 2026, so 2026 dates are given as the pattern to expect.

## 1. arXiv as of late 2026: endorsement, moderation, CS-specific rules, categories, cross-listing, AI policy

### Takeaway
Since 21 Jan 2026 an unaffiliated first-time submitter cannot be auto-endorsed in any arXiv section and must obtain a personal endorsement from an established arXiv author in the target endorsement domain; arXiv staff will not waive this. arXiv allows AI tools but requires "significant use" to be reported in the paper, forbids listing AI as an author, has (per its CS chair, not a formal help page) a one-year ban for unchecked LLM output, and since 1 Oct 2026 caps submitters at two new submissions per month.

### Cited Findings

**Endorsement**
- "arXiv requires that users be endorsed before submitting their first paper to arXiv or a new category"; "At least one positive endorsement is required per endorsement category" (official help page, undated, links to the 21 Jan 2026 post) — [arXiv endorsement help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)
- 21 Jan 2026 (official blog): automatic endorsement now requires BOTH "an institutional email address from an academic/research institution" AND "previous authorship on an existing paper which has been accepted to the arXiv" endorsement domain. Anyone not meeting both must "follow the second path and seek personal endorsement". Stated reason: "stemming the flood of low-quality, non-scientific submissions"; "institutional email addresses are no longer a sufficient credential". Previously endorsed authors keep their endorsements. "arXiv staff cannot waive endorsement requirements or provide a personal endorsement for authors." — [arXiv blog, 21 Jan 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/01/21/attention-authors-updated-endorsement-policy/)
- The rule was piloted in Mathematics on 10 Dec 2025 before going site-wide — [arXiv blog, 10 Dec 2025](https://blog.arxiv.org/arxiv/2025/12/10/updated-endorsement-policy-for-arxiv-mathematics)
- Who can endorse: an author with a required number of papers in the endorsement domain (number varies by area and is not stated on the page); "we only count papers that have been submitted between three months and five years ago"; the endorser must be a registered owner of those papers and hold an active positive endorsement in the area. Endorsement domains are mostly top-level areas (the page gives q-bio as an example; physics is split by subject class) — [arXiv endorsement help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)
- Mechanics: start a submission, pick the category, receive an email with a six-character endorsement code and link; find recent related arXiv papers (ideally ones you cite), use the "Which authors of this paper are endorsers?" link on the abstract page, and email an eligible endorser with the code, optionally with an ORCID/profile link or the manuscript. Mass emailing and repeated requests are called inappropriate — [arXiv endorsement help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)
- What endorsers are asked: endorsement "is not peer review"; the endorser should know the person or have seen the paper, need not verify correctness, but should confirm fit to the subject area and should not endorse authors unfamiliar with basic facts of the field. An endorser can submit a "do not" response, which is recorded as a negative vote; decisions are private — [arXiv endorsement help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)

**Moderation and reasons for decline or reclassification**
- Moderators check scholarly form ("carefully prepared sections, figures, tables, references", neutral professional language), scholarly interest (originality, novelty, significance), category fit, rights, and duplication. Moderation "is not a peer-review process" and moderators give no feedback — [arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
- Listed grounds for decline include: lacking originality/novelty/significance; plagiarism or misrepresentation; personal or political statements; course projects, research proposals, news; work needing "significant review and revision"; topic not covered. Prior declined or delayed submissions draw closer scrutiny. Moderators can move a paper to the "most relevant category" and add or remove cross-lists — [arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
- Appeals: authors may appeal a classification or decline; arXiv "may require that a paper be accepted for publication in a conventional journal"; "decisions upon appeal are final" with no feedback — [arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
- Announced papers are permanent ("part of the permanent scholarly record"); versions can be replaced but not removed — [arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
- 1 Oct 2026 (official blog): "arXiv now limits submitters to up to two submissions per calendar month", plus at most three active submissions; rejected submissions count toward the monthly limit; applies "across all submitters and categories" as "a stopgap". Reason: 40,363 submissions in Sept 2026 vs 20,569 in Sept 2024, with thin, "salami" and dense AI-written papers named. The same post says AI may be used as a research tool if disclosed and standards are met — [arXiv blog, 1 Oct 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)
- From 11 Feb 2026 (announced 21 Nov 2025) every submission must include a full English version (secondary summary that links the official post) — [CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-2026-endorsement-english-ai-policy-changes); official post: [arXiv blog, 21 Nov 2025](https://blog.arxiv.org/2025/11/21/upcoming-policy-change-to-non-english-language-paper-submissions/) (not read directly)
- Governance: arXiv is becoming an independent nonprofit; first CEO and board announced 30 Jul 2026; USD 17.2M multi-year philanthropic commitments announced 23 Sep 2026 — [arXiv blog 2026 index](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026). A secondary source dates the move out of Cornell to 1 Jul 2026 and calls it structural, not a service change — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-independent-nonprofit-cornell-spinout)

**CS-specific rule on review and position papers**
- 31 Oct 2025 (official blog): review/survey articles and position papers in arXiv CS "must now be accepted at a journal or a conference and complete successful peer review" before submission, with "the peer reviewed journal reference and DOI metadata"; workshop review "generally does not meet the same standard of rigor ... and is not enough". Framed as a moderation-practice change because these were never official content types. Ordinary research papers are not affected. Rejected authors must appeal rather than resubmit — [arXiv blog, 31 Oct 2025](https://blog.arxiv.org/?p=1740)

**Categories and cross-listing**
- cs.NE: "Covers neural networks, connectionism, genetic algorithms, artificial life, adaptive behavior." cs.LG: "Papers on all aspects of machine learning research ...". cs.CL: "Covers natural language processing" (artificial-language work not appropriate unless it addresses natural-language issues). cs.AI: "Covers all areas of AI except Vision, Robotics, Machine Learning, Multiagent Systems, and Computation and Language". q-bio.NC: "Synapse, cortex, neuronal dynamics, neural network, sensorimotor control, behavior, attention" — [arXiv category taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/category_taxonomy)
- Cross-listing: "It is rarely appropriate to add more than one or two cross-lists"; "Bad cross-lists will be removed"; excessive cross-listing is "bad etiquette". Cross-lists can be added later from the user page — [arXiv cross-list help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/cross.html)
- Precedent for category choice: a single-author Hebbian language-model paper (Mar 2025) sits in cs.CL with cross-lists to cs.AI and q-bio.NC — [arXiv 2503.02057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02057); a local-learning (Forward-Forward variant) paper sits in cs.LG alone — [arXiv 2501.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09238)

**AI-assisted work and AI authorship**
- Official moderation page (undated): arXiv will "continue to require authors to report in their work any significant use of sophisticated tools", naming "text-to-text generative AI among those that should be reported"; by signing, authors "each individually take full responsibility for all its contents, irrespective of how the contents were generated"; "generative AI language tools should not be listed as an author" — [arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
- Mid-May 2026: the arXiv CS section chair announced (on X and in a Nature interview, not on a formal arXiv page) that "incontrovertible evidence" of unchecked LLM output (hallucinated references, leftover AI meta-comments, placeholder data) leads to a one-year submission ban, after which submissions must first be accepted at a peer-reviewed venue — [SMU Libraries explainer, 10 Jun 2026](https://library.smu.edu.sg/topics-insights/arxiv-tightens-policy-hallucinated-references-what-researchers-should-know-about); [Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/news/ban-authors-submitting-ai-content-welcome-unenforceable). Secondary reports add that LLM use itself is not banned and that moderators flag and section chairs confirm — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-one-year-ban-unchecked-ai-content)

### Inferences
- For an unaffiliated first-time author the endorsement step is now the main gate. The realistic route is one personal endorsement from an author of a paper the manuscript cites and builds on, sent with the manuscript and a public profile; a polished draft with working code makes that request much easier to grant. Endorsement is per domain: one cs endorsement should cover a cs.NE/cs.LG/cs.CL primary, while a q-bio.NC primary would need a q-bio endorser (inferred from the "endorsement domain" wording; the page does not say whether cross-list categories need their own endorsement).
- Category fit: cs.NE is the best literal match (neural networks, connectionism, artificial life, adaptive behaviour). cs.LG is the highest-traffic and most heavily moderated; cs.CL is defensible only if the language-acquisition behaviour is the headline. One primary plus at most one or two cross-lists (for example cs.NE primary, cross-list cs.LG and q-bio.NC or cs.CL) matches the stated etiquette.
- The paper must read as an original research article, not a position or "vision" piece: the CS position-paper rule and the "needs significant review and revision" ground are the likeliest hold reasons for a single-author, brain-inspired manuscript with large claims. Concrete experiments, baselines, a proper reference list and neutral language reduce that risk.
- An AI coding assistant that did a substantial part of implementation and experimentation falls under "significant use of sophisticated tools" on a plain reading, so it should be reported in the paper (methods or acknowledgments), never as an author. Every reference must be verified by hand: a single hallucinated citation is the named trigger for the one-year ban.
- Because posted versions are permanent and submissions are rate-limited to two per month, splitting the work into several thin papers is now both discouraged and capped; one solid paper with versioned updates is the fit.

### Gaps
- The numeric endorser threshold per domain and the definition of "institutional email" are not published on the help page.
- The one-year-ban rule was not found on an official arXiv help page; scope (CS only vs arXiv-wide) and appeal procedure are reported inconsistently by secondary sources.
- No official list of "typical hold reasons" specific to cs.NE/cs.LG was found; the list above is the general moderation page. Anecdotal reports of reclassification (for example cs.LG to cs.NE or to physics.gen-ph/"general" categories) were not verified.
- Whether moderators treat unaffiliated authors differently after endorsement is not documented.

## 2. Alternatives and complements to arXiv

### Takeaway
TMLR is the strongest peer-reviewed complement: free, double-blind, and explicitly accepts well-supported work of modest significance. Zenodo gives an immediate DOI with no gatekeeping (useful for code, data and a citable preprint while endorsement is pending); bioRxiv and PsyArXiv carry scope and affiliation risks; ReScience C and JOSS fit only side-products (a replication, or the software itself).

### Cited Findings
- TMLR acceptance rests on two questions: "Are the claims made in the submission supported by accurate, convincing and clear evidence?" and "Would at least some individuals in TMLR's audience be interested in knowing the findings of this paper?"; "Papers should be accepted if they meet the criteria, even if the contribution or significance of the work is modest." — [TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- TMLR reviewer guide: novelty is not a necessary criterion for acceptance; reviews are due within two weeks of assignment — [TMLR reviewer guide](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/reviewer-guide.html)
- TMLR: "imposes no fees or payments to authors, reviewers, action editors, or editors-in-chief"; double-blind; action editor assigned "within a week"; prior posting "on preprint servers such as arXiv and bioRxiv" is allowed; rejections can be revised and resubmitted as a new linked submission; papers should not "make bold statements unsupported by empirical or rigorous evidence" or "incorrectly claim novelty" — [TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- TMLR launch targets (2022, may not reflect current practice): reviews within 4 weeks, decisions within 2 months — [TMLR launch announcement](https://medium.com/@hugo_larochelle_65309/announcing-the-transactions-on-machine-learning-research-3ea6101c936f)
- TMLR LLM policy: "LLMs may be used as general-purpose assistive tools"; "Authors are fully responsible for content"; "LLMs are not eligible for authorship"; largely LLM-generated submissions will be closely examined. Certifications include Featured, Reproducibility, Survey and J2C (journal-to-conference presentation) — [TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- Zenodo: scope "All fields of research. All types of research artifacts."; "Anyone may register as user of Zenodo."; no pre-publication moderation described; items "retained for the lifetime of the repository" (tied to CERN); "Data files are versioned"; withdrawal is "an exceptional action" leaving a tombstone — [Zenodo policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/)
- bioRxiv: screening is a basic check for non-scientific content and risk, not peer review; authors must register "and supply an affiliation" so an oversight entity can be contacted about misconduct — [bioRxiv submission page](https://www.biorxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript)
- One unverified first-person account says bioRxiv rejected a single-author preprint from an "independent researcher" and asked for a PI at an established institution as corresponding author, a rule the author says is not in public documents — [blog post (single account)](https://newsletter.nathanthornhill.com/p/the-rule-biorxiv-never-wrote)
- PsyArXiv moved to pre-moderation on 19 Aug 2025: preprints are not public until approved, normally within 24–72 hours; the commonest rejection cause is metadata/manuscript mismatch — [PsyArXiv moderation change notice](https://improvingpsych.org/2025/08/20/changes-to-moderation-at-psyarxiv-preprints/)
- ReScience C: platinum open-access, peer-reviewed, "targets computational research and encourages the explicit replication of already published research"; requires original open-source code; accepts failed replications if documented and the original authors were contacted; authors may not replicate their own work — [ReScience C about](https://resciencec.theoj.org/about)
- JOSS: publishes papers about research software, not new research results; requires OSI licence, at least six months of public development history, evidence of research use; pre-trained ML models and notebooks are out of scope; "There are no fees"; generative AI "is permitted for most aspects" but an "AI usage disclosure" (tools and versions, scope, confirmation of human review) is mandatory, and incomplete disclosure "may be considered an ethical breach" — [JOSS submission guide](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submitting.html)

### Inferences
- TMLR's criteria map almost exactly onto a paper whose value is behaviour and methodology rather than benchmark numbers: the bar is that each claim is matched by evidence, so honest negative results with narrow claims are an asset, and overclaiming is the main rejection risk. Double-blind review also removes the affiliation signal.
- A workable sequence for an unaffiliated author: Zenodo deposit of code, data and a manuscript snapshot (DOI, timestamp) → seek arXiv endorsement → arXiv posting → TMLR submission (arXiv posting is permitted). OpenReview hosts TMLR, so reviews become public and citable.
- bioRxiv is a poor fit: affiliation is expected, and a small artificial network without neural or behavioural data sits at the edge of its life-science scope. PsyArXiv is plausible only if the paper is framed as a cognitive/developmental model with child data comparisons.
- JOSS is not a home for the main paper (it excludes research results) but could later host a reusable simulation library if developed in the open for six months or more. ReScience C fits only a replication of someone else's published local-learning result, which would itself be a credible baseline-building side paper.

### Gaps
- OSF Preprints (generic) policies were not retrieved; the March 2026 PsyArXiv policy revision text was not retrieved.
- Current real-world TMLR turnaround (2026) was not found on an official page; only the 2022 targets and per-step deadlines.
- bioRxiv's handling of unaffiliated authors rests on one unverified blog account; the official page only says an affiliation must be supplied.

### Inferences
- Scope ranking for a small growing network that babbles, echoes, names pictures and starts two-word answers, with honest negatives: (1) NeurIPS DevAI workshop and BabyLM workshop (developmental framing, small scale is the point; BabyLM does not require entering the challenge); (2) ICDL and IEEE TCDS (developmental learning is the core scope; staged curricula and babbling are native topics); (3) CCN 2-page abstract or 8-page paper and CogSci (expects explicit links to human cognitive/developmental data); (4) ALIFE and cs.NE-style venues (growth, self-organisation, open-ended structure); (5) Neural Computation / Neural Networks for the learning rule itself once baselines are strong; (6) PLOS CB and Frontiers only if the fee is acceptable and the biological claim is central.
- For an unfunded author the binding costs are APCs and conference registration/travel, not submission. Zero-cost archival routes are TMLR and the subscription option of hybrid journals; workshop papers are cheap in fees but usually require one registered presenter.
- Note the arXiv CS rule treats workshop review as insufficient only for review/position papers; a workshop research paper can still be posted to arXiv as a normal research article.
- Timing from today (18 Nov 2026): the next windows are CogSci 2027 (about 1 Feb 2027 if the pattern holds), CCN 2027 full papers (about mid-Feb) and 2-pagers (about early Apr), ICDL and ALIFE 2027 (Mar–Apr), BabyLM 2027 (May via ARR or July direct), NeurIPS 2027 workshops (early Sep). TMLR and journals are rolling.

## 4. What reviewers and the community expect

### Takeaway
Published guidance converges on: claims scoped to the evidence, limitations stated openly, error bars defined and computed over multiple runs, full training details, a reproducibility route (code strongly expected), and ablations that show which component drives the behaviour. The best-known cautionary tales for brain-inspired work are scaling studies that reported largely negative results and an independent ablation showing a "brain-inspired" hierarchy contributed little.

### Cited Findings
- NeurIPS paper checklist: "Claims in the paper should match theoretical and experimental results"; aspirational goals are acceptable as motivation if clearly marked as not achieved; limitations must be discussed and reviewers are instructed "to not penalize honesty concerning limitations" — [NeurIPS paper checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- Same checklist on statistics: error bars must be "suitably and correctly defined"; "The method for calculating the error bars should be explained"; it must be clear whether they are standard deviation or standard error; 1-sigma is acceptable if stated, 2-sigma preferred when normality is not verified. Training details ("data splits, hyperparameters, how they were chosen") and compute (hardware, per-run and total, and whether the project used more compute than reported) must be given — [NeurIPS paper checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- Same checklist on code: release is not mandatory, but "we do require all submissions to provide some reasonable avenue for reproducibility" — [NeurIPS paper checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- Run counts: 3–10 runs per task is described as prevalent; the recommended reporting is interquartile mean, performance profiles and bootstrap confidence intervals rather than dichotomous significance tests (NeurIPS 2021 Outstanding Paper) — [Agarwal et al., arXiv 2108.13264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13264)
- TMLR rejects papers that "make bold statements unsupported by empirical or rigorous evidence" or "incorrectly claim novelty over existing published work", and accepts modest contributions whose claims are supported — [TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- Scaling critique: a 2018 study tested biologically motivated algorithms (target propagation, feedback alignment variants) on MNIST, CIFAR and ImageNet, described its results as largely negative, and noted per-layer tuning was needed — [Bartunov et al., arXiv 1807.04587](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04587). A follow-up claimed sign-symmetry narrows the gap on ImageNet (ResNet-18 top-1 error 37.91% vs 33.14% for backprop) — [Xiao et al., arXiv 1811.03567](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.03567)
- Ablation critique: an independent analysis (15 Aug 2025) of a brain-inspired "Hierarchical Reasoning Model" from an AI start-up reproduced its ARC-AGI-1 score approximately (32% on the semi-private set) but found the hierarchical architecture had "minimal impact" (a same-size standard transformer came within about 5 points), while the less-emphasised outer refinement loop and data augmentation drove performance — [ARC Prize HRM analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)
- Existing comparison and survey resources a reviewer may expect to see cited: a survey of neurobiologically plausible credit assignment — [arXiv 2312.09257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09257); a comparative study of biologically plausible learning methods — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16062)
- Developmental comparison practice: one BabyLM-line paper defines a "neural age of acquisition" by analogy with the child measure (the age at which 50% of children produce a word) and evaluates against CDI-derived word sets — [arXiv 2405.13828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.13828). Wordbank is the open archive of MacArthur-Bates CDI data used for such norms — [CogSci proceedings paper](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cognitivesciencesociety/article/49307/galley/37268/download). BabyLM requires intermediate checkpoints to measure learning efficiency over exposure — [BabyLM 2026 call](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20092v2)
- Negative results have formal homes: ReScience C accepts documented failed replications — [ReScience C about](https://resciencec.theoj.org/about); TMLR's Reproducibility certification rewards added baselines and ablations — [TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)

### Inferences
- Minimum baseline set a reviewer in this area will look for: (a) chance and a frozen-random-features + linear/ridge readout on the same inputs (to show learning inside the network matters); (b) a backprop-trained network of matched size, data and exposure (stated as an upper reference, not a competitor); (c) at least one published local rule (feedback alignment, Forward-Forward or a Hebbian/predictive-coding variant) run on the same tasks; (d) the model with each developmental stage or growth mechanism removed or reordered. The HRM episode shows that if the paper does not ablate its headline mechanism, someone else will.
- Equal tuning effort for baselines should be stated explicitly (hyperparameter budget per method), since tuning asymmetry is a recurring objection to bio-plausible results.
- Seeds: 5–10 independent seeds per condition with bootstrap confidence intervals is within the documented norm (3–10); for a small network, more is cheap and removes a standard objection. State what the error bar is.
- Generalisation tests carry the paper when accuracy does not: held-out items, novel combinations (for example unseen two-word pairings), and stage-order controls. Curriculum-order confounds matter: where a model's acquisition order matches children because the curriculum was built from child norms, reviewers will discount it unless order is controlled.
- Comparison with child data should be modest and quantitative (for example rank correlation of acquisition order with CDI/Wordbank norms, or qualitative stage ordering babble → echo → naming → two-word), with explicit statements of where the analogy fails (exposure quantity, modality, absence of social interaction).
- Negative results are well received when (i) the claim tested is clear, (ii) the failure is diagnosed with an ablation or analysis, and (iii) the abstract does not promise more than the results. Guidance from NeurIPS and TMLR explicitly protects honest limitation reporting.
- Words that trigger scepticism in this area: "biologically plausible" without specifying which constraints are respected (locality, no weight transport, online, no global error), "brain-like", "AGI", and "outperforms backprop". Stating precisely which information each update uses is the accepted fix.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative "how to review a brain-inspired paper" guideline was found; expectations above are assembled from general ML checklists and specific critiques. The widely cited essay on troubling trends in ML scholarship (explanation vs speculation, failure to identify sources of gains) is relevant but was not retrieved this session, so it is not cited.
- No venue-specific required number of seeds exists in the sources read; 3–10 is a described norm, not a rule.
- CCN, CogSci and ICDL reviewer guidelines were not read; their weighting of human-data comparison is inferred from scope.
- No direct evidence was found on acceptance outcomes for negative-result papers in this subfield.

## 5. Disclosure norms when an AI coding assistant did substantial implementation and experimentation

### Takeaway
Every policy read agrees on three points: AI tools cannot be authors, human authors bear full responsibility for all content, and use beyond routine editing should be disclosed. Venues differ on coding help: NeurIPS (2025–26 text) exempts "basic coding assistance", ICLR 2027 lists "implementing methods" as a required disclosure, ACL's checklist asks about AI assistants in general, and JOSS demands a formal AI usage statement.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv (official, undated): authors must "report in their work any significant use of sophisticated tools", including text-to-text generative AI; each author takes "full responsibility for all its contents, irrespective of how the contents were generated"; "generative AI language tools should not be listed as an author" — [arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)
- arXiv, 1 Oct 2026: AI may be used as a research tool if disclosed and the work meets standards — [arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)
- NeurIPS checklist item 16: "Does the paper describe the usage of LLMs if it is an important, original, or non-standard component of the core methods in this research?"; declaration is not required when LLMs are used only for writing, editing or formatting — [NeurIPS paper checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- NeurIPS 2025 LLM policy: authors are fully responsible for all content "including text, figures, and methodology"; disclosure not needed for writing/editing/formatting that does not affect methodology, rigor or originality; LLMs cannot be authors — [NeurIPS 2025 LLM policy](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2025/LLM)
- NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations & Datasets guidance: authors should document "any important, original, or non-standard use of agents or LLMs in their methodology"; "spell checking, grammar editing, or basic coding assistance do not need to be disclosed"; agents and LLMs cannot be listed as authors — [NeurIPS 2026 E&D reviewer guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/EvaluationsDatasetsReviewerGuidelines)
- NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track (2 Jun 2026) is stricter: papers must be substantially human-written; a detector flagged 28.2% (273/969) of submissions at a 100% score; authors with significant AI involvement were asked for an audit trail — [NeurIPS blog, 2 Jun 2026](https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/06/02/ai-generated-papers-in-the-neurips-2026-position-paper-track/)
- ICLR 2026: any LLM use must be disclosed in the paper and the submission form; significant LLM roles in ideation or writing need a separate section; undisclosed extensive use can be desk-rejected; LLM-produced falsehoods count as an author Code of Ethics violation — [ICLR blog, policies on LLM usage](https://blog.iclr.cc/?p=1046); [ICLR blog, 19 Nov 2025](https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/11/19/iclr-2026-response-to-llm-generated-papers-and-reviews/)
- ICLR 2027: a mandatory AI-disclosure section (outside the page limit). Required disclosures include AI used for "implementing methods", designing methodology or experiments, proposing or refining hypotheses, and interpreting results; "creating or editing software code" and drafting text are in the recommended list. Suggested structure: tasks where AI was used, tasks where it was not, and a statement that all AI-assisted work was reviewed and the authors take responsibility — [ICLR 2027 AI policy for authors](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors)
- ACL: the Responsible NLP checklist asks "If you used AI assistants, did you include information about their use?" (a 2026 example answer describes limited grammar and coding assistance) — [ACL 2026 checklist example](https://aclanthology.org/attachments/2026.acl-long.663.checklist.pdf); [ARR responsible NLP checklist](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch). The original ACL 2023 policy states code assistance does not remove authors' duty to ensure correctness of methods and results — [ACL 2023 policy](https://2023.aclweb.org/blog/ACL-2023-policy/)
- Nature Portfolio (third-party mirror, updated 30 Mar 2026): LLMs cannot be authors; LLM use to generate text "should be documented in the Methods section"; AI-assisted copy editing of human-written text need not be declared; generative-AI images are not permitted — [mirror of Nature AI guidelines](https://www.enago.jp/responsible-ai-movement/publisher-ai-guidelines/nature-ai-guidelines)
- TMLR: LLMs allowed as general-purpose assistive tools; authors fully responsible; LLMs not eligible for authorship — [TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- JOSS: mandatory "AI usage disclosure" covering tools and versions, where and how they were used, and confirmation that "human authors reviewed, edited, validated all AI-assisted outputs" — [JOSS submission guide](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submitting.html)

### Inferences
- When an assistant wrote a substantial share of the code and ran or proposed experiments, this is beyond "basic coding assistance" under any of these policies. The safe, portable form is a short dedicated statement (methods or a titled "AI use" section, repeated briefly in acknowledgments and the repository README) naming the tool and version, the tasks it performed (implementation, experiment scripts, analysis, drafting), the tasks it did not perform, and how the human author verified the outputs (reran experiments, read the code, checked every citation). This satisfies arXiv, TMLR, ICLR 2027, ACL and JOSS wording simultaneously.
- The assistant must not be listed as an author anywhere, including arXiv metadata; an acknowledgment is the accepted place for credit.
- The trend from 2025 to 2027 is toward more disclosure of methodological AI use (ICLR's move from a general rule to a mandatory section; arXiv's ban for unchecked content), so over-disclosure is the low-risk choice. For a single-author independent paper, a clear verification statement also pre-empts the suspicion that the work is unreviewed machine output, which is the stated target of arXiv's 2026 measures.
- Human-verified, reproducible evidence (seeded scripts, logs, released code) is the practical counterpart of the "full responsibility" clause: it lets the author demonstrate checking rather than assert it.

### Gaps
- The official Nature Portfolio AI policy page redirected to a login flow and could not be read; wording comes from a third-party mirror.
- The NeurIPS 2026 main-track author handbook text was not located; the 2025 policy and the 2026 E&D guidance were used as proxies.
- No policy read addresses fully agentic experimentation (an assistant autonomously launching and analysing runs) by name, except ICLR 2027's task list; how reviewers treat such disclosures in practice was not found.
- EMNLP/ACL 2026 call wording on AI assistance was not read directly.

## 6. Examples of independent or hobbyist researchers gaining traction with backprop-free or brain-inspired work (2022–2026)

### Takeaway
No well-documented case was found of an unaffiliated individual whose backprop-free or brain-inspired arXiv paper gained wide traction in 2022–2026; the visible successes in this niche come from famous authors, labs or start-ups. The clearest documented lesson is negative: brain-inspired claims that drew attention were quickly stress-tested by outside ablations.

### Cited Findings
- The reference point for attention in this area is a late-2022 paper by a senior, well-known researcher proposing two forward passes instead of forward/backward; it reported that the method was slower and generalised less well on toy problems, and was presented at NeurIPS 2022 — [Forward-Forward paper (PDF)](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/FFA13.pdf); [explainer, Dec 2022](https://bdtechtalks.com/2022/12/19/forward-forward-algorithm-geoffrey-hinton/)
- A single-author arXiv paper with no affiliation shown on the abstract page, "Hebbian learning the local structure of language" (3 Mar 2025; cs.CL, cross-listed cs.AI and q-bio.NC; 14 pages), shows that a local, unsupervised Hebbian language model can be posted in mainstream categories; no journal acceptance is noted — [arXiv 2503.02057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02057). A companion paper describes learning that is entirely local with no global loss — [arXiv 2506.23293](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23293v1)
- "Mono-Forward" (v1 16 Jan 2025, v2 7 May 2026; cs.LG; three authors, affiliation not shown on the abstract page) isolates whether Forward-Forward's gap comes from locality or from its objective, and reports competitive accuracy with less memory — [arXiv 2501.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09238)
- A start-up's brain-inspired reasoning model (June 2025) drew wide attention with a 41% ARC-AGI-1 claim from a 27M-parameter model; an outside team's analysis two months later verified about 32% on hidden data and showed the brain-inspired hierarchy mattered little compared with an iterative refinement loop and augmentation — [ARC Prize HRM analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)
- Context on reception in 2026: arXiv received 40,363 submissions in Sept 2026 and attributes part of the growth to low-quality AI-assisted papers, which raises baseline scepticism toward unaffiliated single-author work — [arXiv blog, 1 Oct 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)

### Inferences
- Features shared by the niche papers that did get read: one sharp, testable question; a small model whose result can be reproduced on modest hardware; a recognised external benchmark or comparison; and a decomposition or ablation that explains why it works. Papers that led with a brain narrative and a headline number attracted attention first and corrective scrutiny second.
- For an unaffiliated author, credibility substitutes are: released code and data with one-command reproduction, a venue stamp (TMLR acceptance or a workshop paper at DevAI/BabyLM/CCN), precise scoping of claims, and an explicit negative-results section. Traction in this area in 2025–2026 appears to run through reproducibility by others more than through arXiv listing alone.
- A behaviour-and-methodology paper (staged development, generalisation probes, ablations, honest failures) is differentiated from the flood of benchmark-claim papers and is aligned with what DevAI, BabyLM and ICDL explicitly solicit.

### Gaps
- This question is the weakest-supported part of these notes. Searches did not surface a verified independent or hobbyist author in this niche with measurable traction (citations, follow-up work, venue acceptance); affiliation status of the single-author Hebbian paper and of the Mono-Forward authors was not confirmed (the arXiv abstract page simply shows none).
- Citation counts, social-media reach and follow-up adoption for any of the listed papers were not checked.
- Community discussion threads (forums, researcher blogs) about endorsement experiences of independent authors after the January 2026 rule change were not found in this session.
