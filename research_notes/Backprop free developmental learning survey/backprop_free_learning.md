# Backprop-free and local learning: state of the art as of November 2026

Verification legend used on every finding: **[V]** = number or claim read in the fetched primary source (arXiv abstract/HTML, proceedings page); **[V-table]** = read in a comparison table of another fetched primary paper (so second-hand but from a paper, not an aggregator); **[S]** = taken from a search-result summary of the primary source, page not opened by me; anything recalled from memory without a source is listed only under Gaps. All accuracies are top-1 test accuracy in % unless stated. "BP" = end-to-end backpropagation.

## 1. Main families: best published results, venue and year

### Takeaway
On small convolutional nets (5-11 layers) and CIFAR-scale data, several local methods now sit within about 0-4 points of backprop on the same architecture (predictive coding, equilibrium propagation, target propagation, layer-local contrastive SSL, the best forward-forward variant); on full ImageNet and on deep/residual networks every family still shows a clear gap or has only one or two unreplicated preprints. Purely Hebbian, feedback-free methods are the furthest behind: about 80% CIFAR-10 and 27% ImageNet top-1 (2022-23), with no later result I could find that moves those numbers.

### Cited Findings

**Forward-Forward (FF) and descendants**
- Original FF: Hinton, arXiv Dec 2022; two forward passes (positive/negative data), per-layer "goodness" objective; presented as preliminary work that "works well enough on a few small problems to be worth further investigation" [V] — [arXiv 2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345)
- Original FF numbers as tabulated later: 4-layer MLP, MNIST 98.69, CIFAR-10 59.00 [V-table] — [ASGE, arXiv 2509.12394v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12394v2). Hinton's own CIFAR-10 result for non-convolutional locally connected nets was about 41% test error [S] — [arXiv 2212.13345 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13345)
- FF-family progression (all [V-table]): CaFo 2023 CNN3 CIFAR-10 67.43 / CIFAR-100 40.76; SymBa 2023 MLP3 59.09 / 29.28; CwC 2024 CNN4 78.11 / 51.23; DeeperForward 2025 ResNet18 88.72 / 53.09 — [ASGE, arXiv 2509.12394v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12394v2)
- Best FF-family result found: ASGE (Gong, Staszewski, Xu; arXiv v1 Sep 2025, v2 22 Jan 2026; preprint). VGG8: CIFAR-10 90.50, CIFAR-100 65.34, ImageNet top-1/top-5 23.04/43.08. VGG11: 90.62 / 65.42 / ImageNet 26.21/47.49. A widened "ResNet18-CH×4": ImageNet 51.58/75.23. BP on VGG8: CIFAR-10 91.68, CIFAR-100 69.11, ImageNet 46.16/72.02. So about 1 point behind on CIFAR-10, about 4 on CIFAR-100, about 23 on ImageNet for the like-for-like VGG8; the ResNet18 number has no matched BP baseline on the page [V] — [arXiv 2509.12394v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12394v2)
- Self-Contrastive FF (SCFF, Chen et al.), unsupervised: MNIST 98.7 (MLP), CIFAR-10 80.75 (CNN), STL-10 77.3 (CNN); 80.13 on STL-10 after BP fine-tuning on labels; published Nature Communications 1 July 2025 [S] — [arXiv 2409.11593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11593). A later paper tabulates SCFF as 80.60 / 77.14 / Tiny ImageNet 35.67 [V-table] — [arXiv 2601.21683](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21683v1) (small conflict between the two sources, probably reproduction vs. reported)
- Hierarchical FF (HFF): 25.70% ImageNet-1K from scratch on VGG16, 65.96% with transfer learning; CIFAR-100 54.34 [S, not opened] — search summary citing [arXiv 2509.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12394v1)
- Diagnostic paper accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Wu, Liu, Chen, 27 Sep 2026): FF's gap to BP "widens with depth and scale"; theory says FF layer representations collapse toward rank one with depth and that per-layer optimisation chases a drifting input distribution [V] — [arXiv 2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33240). Their controlled CIFAR-10 experiment on a 12-layer CNN: strictly layer-local 53.02, blocks of 2 layers 58.09, of 3 67.57, of 4 72.22, of 6 76.21, one global block 78.74, BP 85.67 [V] — [arXiv 2609.33240 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33240)
- Independent 9-algorithm comparison (UCL, arXiv June 2024): FF MLP MNIST 96.99, CIFAR-10 39.48; their FF CNN failed (15.66 on MNIST) [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Predictive coding networks (PCN)**
- PCX benchmark (Pinchetti et al., arXiv 2407.01163, v2 Feb 2025; ICLR 2025 [venue from search summary]). Best PC variant vs best BP: VGG-5 CIFAR-10 89.47 vs 89.43; CIFAR-100 67.19 vs 66.28; Tiny ImageNet 46.40 vs 44.90. VGG-7: 88.40 vs 89.91; 64.76 vs 65.36; 41.15 vs 46.08. ResNet-18: CIFAR-10 62.45 vs 93.21; CIFAR-100 29.45 vs 72.32; Tiny ImageNet 15.95 vs 58.00. PC CIFAR-10 falls 89.47 → 88.40 → 87.19 (VGG-9) → 62.45 (ResNet-18) while BP rises. Epoch time VGG-7 on Tiny ImageNet: 7.59 s BP vs 54.60 s PC [V] — [arXiv 2407.01163v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v2)
- Meta-PCN (Ha et al., ICLR 2026): attributes deep-PCN instability to prediction-error imbalance and exploding/vanishing errors; on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet claims statistically significant gains over standard PCNs and to outperform BP "in most tested configurations"; abstract gives no numbers or depths [V, abstract only] — [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/319e084cd710c3db50a9f9dd7a02a9a8-Abstract-Conference.html)
- First ImageNet-scale PCN (Kerjan, Høier, Scellier, arXiv 2 June 2026, preprint): 10-layer convolutional PCN (VGG10) trained with centered equilibrium propagation, full-size ImageNet top-5 test error 13.23% vs 12.2% for BP; claimed first demonstration of both PCNs and EP training at ImageNet scale. Top-1 not given in the abstract [V] — [arXiv 2606.03584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584)
- 100+ layer residual PCNs via a depth-μP parameterisation (Innocenti et al., May 2025) [S, secondary summary, unverified] — [emergentmind PCN topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/predictive-coding-networks-pcns)
- 2024 comparison paper: PC CNN MNIST 99.12, CIFAR-10 56.12, CIFAR-100 16.32 vs BP 99.59 / 73.64 / 52.45 (small 3-conv CNN, untuned relative to specialist papers) [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Equilibrium propagation (EP)**
- Holomorphic EP (Laborieux & Zenke, NeurIPS 2022): exact gradients from finite-amplitude oscillating nudges; "first benchmark for EP on the ImageNet 32×32 dataset", stated to match an equivalent BP-trained network; also BP-level on CIFAR-10/100 deep CNNs. Exact figures not retrieved [S, abstract quote] — [arXiv 2209.00530](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00530)
- EP with random-sign nudging (Laborieux et al.), CIFAR-10 78.5; original two-phase EP listed at 13.4 on the same table [V-table, row alignment flagged as tentative by the extraction] — [SoftHebb paper, arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- 2026: EP-trained VGG10 PCN on full ImageNet, 13.23% top-5 error (see PCN above) [V] — [arXiv 2606.03584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584)
- 2024 comparison paper: EP MLP MNIST 93.81, CIFAR-10 16.93; EP CNN failed to converge on CIFAR-10 in their generic setup [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Feedback alignment (FA) / direct feedback alignment (DFA)**
- Bartunov et al., NeurIPS 2018, "Assessing the scalability of biologically-motivated deep learning algorithms": many algorithms do well on MNIST, "but for CIFAR and ImageNet the TP and FA variants perform significantly worse than BP, especially for networks composed of locally connected units" [S, abstract quote] — [NeurIPS 2018 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2018/poster/11892)
- ImageNet top-1 as tabulated in 2022: FA 6.9 (5-layer CNN, Bartunov et al.), DFA 6.2 (AlexNet, Crafton et al.), sparse DFA 2.8 (AlexNet) [V-table] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- Launay et al. 2020, "Direct Feedback Alignment Scales to Modern Deep Learning Tasks and Architectures" (arXiv 2006.12878). Transformer LM on WikiText-103 (6 layers, 8 heads, d=512, FF 2048, 32k BPE), best validation perplexity: DFA "macro" (feedback per block) 95.0 → 52.0 after optimiser tuning; DFA "micro" (per sub-layer) 182 → 93.3; BP 34.4 → 29.8; top-layer-only baseline 428. NeRF-Synthetic PSNR 25.41 (DFA) vs 31.01 (BP). Graph nets on Cora/CiteSeer/PubMed within 0.2-7 points of BP. Authors: DFA can train Transformers but "not on par with BP"; did not test image CNNs; about 10,000 V100 GPU-hours [V] — [arXiv 2006.12878](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.12878)
- Sign-symmetry (Xiao, Chen, Liao, Poggio, ICLR 2019, "Biologically-plausible learning algorithms can scale to large datasets"): networks trained with sign-symmetric feedback reported as "approaching" BP on ImageNet [S] — [arXiv 1807.04587 summary](https://api.emergentmind.com/papers/1807.04587)
- GrAPE (Caillon et al., ICLR 2026): states DFA "fails on deep convolutional networks" and Transformers due to misalignment; narrows the gap with Jacobian-alignment guarantees but still needs periodic backprop recalibration [S, review-page summary] — [liner review](https://liner.com/ko/review/scaling-direct-feedback-learning-with-jacobian-alignment-guarantees)
- Optical DFA hardware (Wang et al., 2024) claimed to scale to more than one billion parameters [S] — [arXiv 2409.12965](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12965v1)
- 2024 comparison paper, 3-conv CNN: FA CIFAR-10 59.60 / CIFAR-100 33.30; DFA 58.38 / 22.91; BP 73.64 / 52.45 [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Target propagation (TP / DTP)**
- Ernoult et al., ICML 2022, "Towards Scaling Difference Target Propagation by Learning Backprop Targets": 6-layer VGG-like net (5 conv + 1 FC); CIFAR-10 89.38 vs BP 89.07; ImageNet 32×32 top-5 60.54 vs BP 61.25; about 13 points above earlier DTP results on CIFAR-10 [S, numbers from search summary of the PDF/PMLR page] — [PMLR v162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/ernoult22a.html), [arXiv 2201.13415](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13415)
- 2024 comparison paper: TP CNN CIFAR-10 50.88, CIFAR-100 21.20 [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Hebbian deep learning (soft winner-take-all and relatives)**
- SoftHebb (Journé, Garcia Rodriguez, Guo, Moraitis; arXiv Sep 2022; ICLR 2023 oral per the ICLR listing). Greedy layer-wise conv net, softmax soft-WTA with a Hebbian + soft anti-Hebbian update, no labels/targets/feedback in hidden layers; a supervised linear classifier on top (cross-entropy, 50-200 epochs). Width 96 kernels (48 for ImageNet) ×4 per layer; 3 hidden conv layers for MNIST/CIFAR-10, 4 for STL-10, 5 for ImageNet (160 px). Hidden layers trained for one unsupervised epoch. Results vs BP on the same architecture: MNIST 99.35 vs 99.45; CIFAR-10 80.31 vs 83.97; STL-10 76.23 vs 74.51; ImageNette 80.98 vs 85.30; ImageNet top-1 27.3 (one seed; BP not run). Random-weight version of the same net: STL-10 68.2, ImageNet 14.0 [V] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883), [ICLR 2023 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12759)
- Same paper, single conv layer on CIFAR-10: SoftHebb 71.10, hard WTA 62.69, random weights 63.93, BP 72.42. Fully connected shallow nets: CIFAR-10 43.9 (SoftHebb) vs 55.7 (BP) [V] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- Other Hebbian/WTA entries in SoftHebb's tables: hard-WTA variants 72.2 (Grinberg et al.) and 64.6 (Miconi) on CIFAR-10 [tentative alignment]; hard WTA 54.8 and K-means (Dundar et al.) 74.1 on STL-10 [V-table] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- Authors' stated limits: vision only; only width-scaled conv nets; 5-6 layers maximum; "Backprop in more complex models significantly outperforms SoftHebb"; weight sharing not literally plausible [V] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- Generic (untuned) Hebbian and STDP in the 2024 comparison: Hebbian CNN MNIST 83.05, CIFAR-10 29.86; STDP CNN 91.67 / 33.19; neither converged on CIFAR-100 [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Local contrastive / greedy layer-wise / block-local losses**
- CLAPP++ (Wu, Delrocq, Gerstner, Bellec; arXiv 29 Jan 2026, preprint), layer-local self-supervised rule, VGG-style net, linear probe: CIFAR-10 81.18 (best variant) vs BP-SSL 80.49-80.69; STL-10 79.62 vs 80.36-81.97; Tiny ImageNet 37.78 vs 36.78-37.55. Earlier local SSL on the same table: CLAPP 2021 STL-10 73.6; LPL (Halvagal & Zenke 2023) CIFAR-10 59.4 / STL-10 63.2; stochastic FF (Zhu 2025) CIFAR-10 76.96. Abstract also claims ImageNet results; numbers were beyond the part of the page I read. Authors: matching BP "has been notoriously hard" for local rules [V] — [arXiv 2601.21683](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21683v1)
- Greedy InfoMax (ResNet-50) STL-10 81.9; SimCLR ResNet-50 (BP, 1000 epochs) STL-10 91.5 / ImageNet 76.5 as the BP-SSL reference [V-table] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- Belilovsky et al., ICML 2019, "Greedy Layerwise Learning Can Scale to ImageNet": sequential 1-hidden-layer auxiliary problems give a CNN that exceeds AlexNet on ImageNet [S]; 69.7% top-1 with 3-hidden-layer auxiliaries [S, secondary, unverified] — [arXiv 1812.11446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.11446)
- Blockwise SSL (Siddiqui et al., TMLR 2024): ResNet-50 split into 4 blocks, each trained with its own Barlow Twins loss, ImageNet linear probe 70.48 vs 71.57 end-to-end. Uses backprop inside each block [S] — [arXiv 2302.01647](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.01647v2)
- Successive Gradient Reconciliation: ResNet-50 ImageNet 76.35 vs 76.55 BP with K=2 modules, at roughly double wall-clock for K=3 [S, review page only] — [liner review](https://liner.com/review/towards-interpretable-deep-local-learning-with-successive-gradient-reconciliation)
- NoProp (Li, Teh, Pascanu; arXiv Mar 2025; PMLR v330, 2026): each block learns to denoise a noisy label (diffusion-style), evaluated on MNIST/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, "comparable" to BP in the studied architecture; v2 retitled to "without Full Back-propagation" because each block still uses backprop internally [S] — [arXiv 2503.24322v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24322v2), [PMLR v330](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v330/li26a.html)
- 2024 comparison paper: "Local Losses" closest to BP: CNN CIFAR-10 72.18 vs 73.64; CIFAR-100 35.39 vs 52.45 [V] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)

**Three-factor / reward-modulated rules and e-prop (spiking)**
- e-prop (Bellec et al., Nature Communications 2020): eligibility traces × online learning signal for recurrent spiking nets; "approaches the performance of BPTT" [S] — [PMC7367848](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7367848/)
- e-prop on a SpiNNaker 2 prototype (Frontiers in Neuroscience 2022): 91.12% accuracy with 680 KB training memory for a 25K-weight network, batch size 1 [S; task not named in the summary] — [PMC9742366](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9742366/)
- ReckOn (ISSCC 2022), 28 nm spiking RNN processor with end-to-end on-chip learning from random initial weights [S] — [arXiv 2208.09759](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09759v1.pdf)
- Experimental basis: Gerstner et al. 2018 review of eligibility traces and neo-Hebbian three-factor rules [S] — [PMC6079224](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6079224/)
- NeurIPS 2025: meta-learned neo-Hebbian three-factor rules for training RNNs from sparse reward; no benchmark accuracy in the summary [S] — [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/133744)
- 2026 preprint on spiking encoders with reward-modulated STDP plus a hybrid rate readout: best ablation 95.52 ± 1.11% (dataset not visible in the summary; small-scale) [S] — [arXiv 2603.00710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00710)

### Inferences
- Rank by closeness to BP at the largest scale each has reached: block-local losses with backprop inside blocks (ImageNet ResNet-50 within about 1 point) > EP/PCN energy-based methods (ImageNet VGG10 within about 1 point top-5, June 2026, single preprint) ≈ layer-local SSL (Tiny ImageNet parity) > DTP (ImageNet32 parity, 6 layers) > FF (ImageNet 26% VGG11 / 52% wide ResNet18 vs 46% BP VGG8) > DFA/FA (single-digit ImageNet for CNNs) > pure Hebbian (27% ImageNet).
- The more "local" and feedback-free a rule is, the larger the gap; the NeurIPS 2026 "Price of Locality" block-size sweep (53% → 79% as locality is relaxed) is the cleanest quantitative statement of this.
- Specialist papers report much better numbers than neutral comparisons (e.g. PC 89% in PCX vs 56% in the 2024 nine-algorithm comparison on CIFAR-10), so headline numbers reflect heavy per-method tuning and specific architectures.
- A feedback-free Hebbian vision front end reaching roughly 70-80% CIFAR-10 with a supervised linear/delta-rule readout is at the published state of the art for that class; nothing published in that class is near BP on ImageNet.

### Gaps
- Exact hEP ImageNet32 numbers, CLAPP++ ImageNet numbers, Meta-PCN accuracies and depths, and NoProp's CIFAR tables were not retrieved.
- Recalled but unverified here: Launay et al. was published at NeurIPS 2020; the SpiNNaker 2 e-prop 91.12% figure is on Google Speech Commands; sign-symmetry ResNet-50 ImageNet numbers; Krotov & Hopfield (PNAS 2019) Hebbian results; Illing et al. (2019) showing shallow local nets saturate. None of these were checked in a source during this session.
- No head-to-head benchmark of reward-modulated/three-factor rules against BP on standard deep-learning datasets was found; the three-factor literature is mostly RNN/RL toy tasks and neuroscience evidence.
- No post-2023 purely Hebbian result beating SoftHebb's 27.3% ImageNet was found; I cannot rule out that one exists.

## 2. Backprop-free or local learning on language and vision-language tasks

### Takeaway
Language results exist but fall into two very different groups: (a) engineering-style block-local training of Transformers, which in September-October 2026 reached 340M-2B parameters and near-BP quality while still using backprop inside each block; and (b) strictly local rules (DFA, FF, forward gradients, predictive coding), which lag BP by 1.2× to 9× in perplexity and are confined to models of at most about 124M parameters. Grounded word learning with pure Hebbian plasticity has been shown only in simulated assemblies with stylised sensory input (the NEMO line); I found no published backprop-free system that learns words from real images plus a speech-like stream.

### Cited Findings
- DFA Transformer, WikiText-103 (2020): best perplexity 52.0 (macro) / 93.3 (micro) vs BP 29.8; macro DFA still relies on some weight transport inside blocks [V] — [arXiv 2006.12878](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.12878)
- Predictive coding Transformers (Pinchetti et al., NeurIPS 2022): one-block, one-head BERT-style model, 32-token inputs: BP about 162.6 test perplexity, KL-based PC about 175.9, original Gaussian PC about 590.1 [S] — [arXiv 2211.03481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.03481)
- Incremental PC (Song et al.), two-block Transformers, hidden 128, 1B Word Benchmark: masked LM about 106 (iPC) vs 120 (BP); conditional LM 143 (iPC) vs 113 (BP); standard PC 523 / 206 [S] — [arXiv 2212.00720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00720)
- "Price of Locality" (NeurIPS 2026), decoder-only Transformers on OpenWebText, perplexity BP / FF / local cross-entropy (LCE): 7M params (2 layers) 138 / 516 / 166; 16M (4) 68 / 313 / 84; 30M (6) 48 / 275 / 64; 51M (8) 39 / 256 / 54; 124M (12) 28 / 258 / 42. FF/BP ratio grows 3.73× → 9.19×; LCE/BP 1.20× → 1.51×. Local methods carry extra per-layer heads [V] — [arXiv 2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33240)
- Split forward gradients (arXiv July 2026): GPT-2-style on WikiText-103, perplexity 386.7 vs BP reference 149.6 (single seed; a separately tuned AdamW BP run reached 75.5); 34.5% less memory, about 3.2× step time [S] — [arXiv 2607.16612](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16612)
- Greedy block-local LM pretraining (Moon et al., arXiv 4 Oct 2026, v2 26 Oct): 125M and 400M params, K ∈ {1,2,4} blocks, Chinchilla-optimal token budgets. Gap to end-to-end more than doubles from K=2 to K=4 but shrinks from 125M to 400M; Transformer auxiliary heads recover 22-41% of the gap; best K=4 config +0.062 nats (vs +0.075) at 400M; activation memory down up to 2.2×. Authors call it a direction "rather than a finished method" [V, abstract] — [arXiv 2610.04867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04867)
- SOLO (Yin, Wang, Pan, Li; arXiv 28 Sep 2026, preprint): modules are gradient-isolated but share a read-only copy of the final readout from the previous step; "approaches backpropagation on Transformers of 340M to 2B parameters pretrained on 15B tokens", within one point of BP in average zero-shot accuracy, perplexity gap narrows with scale, up to 1.44× the throughput of pipeline BP; claims no billion-parameter LM had previously been pretrained with local learning [V, abstract] — [arXiv 2609.35440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440)
- FF-trained Transformer for machine translation: about 20% less training time, 30% less memory, perplexity 12.5 → 13.2 vs BP (regional journal; not comparable to LM benchmarks) [S] — [hrcak.srce.hr/332173](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/332173)
- NEMO "language organ" (Mitropolsky & Papadimitriou, arXiv June 2023): simulated stylised neurons, brain areas, random synapses, Hebbian plasticity, "without backpropagation"; learns nouns, verbs and their meanings "from the grounded input of only a modest number of sentences"; sensory input is a simplified model, not real images; abstract gives no quantitative scale [V, abstract] — [arXiv 2306.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15364)
- Follow-up (Mitropolsky & Papadimitriou, arXiv 15 July 2025): from tabula rasa learns word semantics, noun/verb role and word order from grounded whole sentences, and generates novel sentences; six principles (excitatory neurons, areas, random synapses, Hebbian plasticity, local inhibition, inter-area inhibition); no sentence counts or vocabulary size in the abstract; self-described as "basic" language [V, abstract] — [arXiv 2507.11788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11788)
- Objective-free local next-token learning (arXiv June 2025): short-term-memory synapses storing pairwise correlations between projected context features and the next token; no global loss, no backprop; focus on emergent token boundaries/morphology rather than benchmark perplexity [S] — [arXiv 2506.23293](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2506.23293)
- "Hebbian learning the local structure of language" (arXiv March 2025): Hebbian rules learn word-like units from unlabeled text; no vision or grounding [S] — [arXiv 2503.02057](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2503.02057)
- "Dragon Hatchling" (BDH; Kosowski et al., arXiv 30 Sep 2025, preprint): brain-inspired architecture claimed to rival GPT-2 at 10M-1B parameters, with Hebbian synaptic plasticity as inference-time working memory and emergent modular scale-free structure; the search results do not show that the weights are trained by a local rule, and no independent replication or critique was found [S] — [arXiv 2509.26507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26507)
- PC-style recurrent models on Penn Treebank: a BP-trained Transformer still had the lowest perplexity of all methods compared [S] — [arXiv 2309.04106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04106)

### Inferences
- The largest "backprop-free" language results (SOLO, greedy block-local) are backprop-free only between blocks; they are motivated by memory and pipeline throughput, not biological plausibility. They show that gradient isolation between large modules costs little at scale, not that Hebbian-type rules can learn language.
- For strictly local rules, the best like-for-like LM figure is local cross-entropy at 1.2-1.5× BP perplexity (≤124M params); goodness-based FF is 4-9× worse and gets relatively worse with depth.
- A system that learns word-referent mappings from real pictures and a byte-level speech-like stream with Hebbian/delta-rule learning, an episodic store and growth has no direct published benchmark competitor that I could find. The nearest relatives are the NEMO papers (same spirit, but simulated sensory input and assembly calculus rather than real pixels) and SoftHebb (real images, no language). That makes the combination unusual, but it also means there is no accepted benchmark or baseline, so claims would need self-constructed BP baselines on the same data.
- Question answering without backprop: nothing found beyond zero-shot evaluation averages in SOLO.

### Gaps
- No vision-plus-language model (captioning, VQA, cross-situational word learning on real images) trained without backprop was found. This is an absence of search hits across about 45 queries/fetches, not proof of absence.
- SOLO's dataset names, absolute perplexities and which zero-shot tasks were used are not in the abstract; the full paper was not read.
- I recall BDH being trained with standard backprop (the Hebbian element being fast weights at inference); not verified in a source here.
- Developmental-curriculum language learning (e.g. small-data "baby" language-model challenges, cross-situational word learning models from cognitive science) was not searched; it is adjacent and may be covered by other researchers.

## 3. Purely Hebbian / unsupervised features with whitening versus learned deep features

### Takeaway
Whitened-patch, single-layer, non-backprop features with a linear readout have been known since 2011 to reach about 80% on CIFAR-10, and a 2021 result pushes the same recipe to 85.6-86.9% (linear) and 88.5% (one hidden layer) on CIFAR-10 and 33-39% top-1 on low-resolution ImageNet, which is above every multi-layer Hebbian network published. The best BP-trained nets on comparable small architectures sit at about 90-93% (VGG8/ResNet-18 here), and 95-96% for deeper ResNets.

### Cited Findings
- Coates, Lee & Ng, AISTATS 2011 (PMLR 15:215-223), "An Analysis of Single-Layer Networks in Unsupervised Feature Learning": K-means, sparse autoencoders, sparse RBMs and GMMs in single-layer nets; studied receptive field size, number of features, stride and whitening; 79.6% on CIFAR-10 and 97.2% on NORB, then the best published; conclusion that many hidden nodes and dense extraction matter more than the learning algorithm or depth [S, abstract quote] — [PMLR v15](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/coates11a.html)
- Later citations of the same pipeline: single-layer K-means 80.6% with 4800 feature maps; multi-layer extension 82.0% [S, as cited in the DCGAN paper] — [arXiv 1511.06434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06434)
- Thiry, Arbel, Belilovsky, Oyallon, ICLR 2021, "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Patches in Deep Convolutional Kernels Methods": dictionary of whitened patches sampled uniformly at random from training images (not K-means, not learned), nearest-neighbour style encoding, linear classifier. CIFAR-10: 85.6% (10k patches), 86.7% (60k patches), 86.9% (60k, no vector quantisation); 82.5% with 2,048 patches (appendix); 88.5% with a one-hidden-layer classifier. Their table lists Coates et al. 2011 with 1k patches at 68.6%. Replacing patches with Gaussian white noise costs 8.1 points; optimising the dictionary end-to-end changes accuracy by about −0.1%. ImageNet with 2k patches: 33.2/54.3 top-1/top-5 at 64×64, 35.9/57.4 at 128×128, 39.4/62.1 at 64×64 with a one-hidden-layer classifier. The paper says whitening matters (regulariser acts as an eigenvalue threshold) but reports no explicit no-whitening accuracy [V] — [arXiv 2101.07528](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.07528), [ICLR 2021 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07528)
- SoftHebb single conv layer on CIFAR-10: 71.10 (SoftHebb), 62.69 (hard WTA), 63.93 (random weights), 72.42 (BP); three layers 80.31 vs BP 83.97. Its reported pipeline uses standard-score normalisation; whitening is not mentioned on the page read [V] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- K-means features (Dundar et al.) STL-10 74.1 vs SoftHebb 76.2 vs hard WTA 54.8 [V-table] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- BP references measured in papers read here: VGG8 CIFAR-10 91.68 / CIFAR-100 69.11 — [arXiv 2509.12394v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12394v2); ResNet-18 CIFAR-10 93.21 / CIFAR-100 72.32 — [arXiv 2407.01163v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v2); ResNet-18/24/56 CIFAR-10 about 95-96 — [arXiv 2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33240) [all V]
- Best layer-local SSL (not Hebbian, but local) linear probe: CIFAR-10 81.18, equal to BP-SSL on the same VGG-style net [V] — [arXiv 2601.21683](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21683v1)

### Inferences
- Rough CIFAR-10 ladder for linear-readout features without end-to-end backprop: random conv filters about 64 → hard WTA about 63-72 → SoftHebb one layer 71 → Coates-style K-means + whitening about 80 → SoftHebb three layers 80 → local SSL 81 → large whitened random-patch dictionary 86-87 (88.5 with one hidden layer) → BP small nets 90-93 → BP deep ResNets 95-96.
- Dictionary size and dense, whitened extraction buy more than the sophistication of the unsupervised rule: 1k patches 68.6% vs 60k patches 86.7% in one paper, and random patches match an end-to-end-optimised dictionary. For a competitive-learning + whitening front end, widening the dictionary is the documented cheap lever; depth via Hebbian stacking has so far added little (71 → 80 over three layers).
- The cost is feature dimensionality and a large supervised linear solve, and the ImageNet numbers (33-39%) show the recipe does not close the gap on harder data, where BP nets exceed 75%.

### Gaps
- No single paper found that ablates ZCA whitening on/off for Hebbian/competitive features with exact numbers; Coates et al.'s whitening ablation is in the full text, which I did not open.
- I recall Coates & Ng-style results being very sensitive to whitening and to the "triangle" soft encoding (SoftHebb's "Triangle" activation is borrowed from it); not verified here.
- No 2024-2026 update of the whitened-patch baseline was found.

## 4. What reviews and position papers (2022-2026) conclude about scaling local rules and about NeuroAI

### Takeaway
The consistent empirical conclusion from 2018 through 2026 is that plausibility and performance trade off: the closer a rule is to strictly local Hebbian learning, the worse it scales, and the methods that do scale either approximate the BP gradient (EP, PC, DTP) or keep backprop inside blocks. Optimists point to parity at moderate depth, new ImageNet-scale demonstrations in 2026, advantages in online/low-interference settings and hardware; sceptics, now with a NeurIPS 2026 theory paper, argue the gap for goodness-style local objectives is structural and widens with depth. NeuroAI position papers argue for the programme on embodied/sensorimotor grounds rather than on evidence that local learning rules beat BP.

### Cited Findings

**Sceptical or cautionary**
- Bartunov et al. (NeurIPS 2018): TP and FA variants "perform significantly worse than BP" on CIFAR and ImageNet; a reviewer's summary: all proposed bio-plausible alternatives "fall quite short on complex image recognition tasks" [S] — [NeurIPS 2018 reviews](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/63c3ddcc7b23daa1e42dc41f9a44a873-Reviews.html)
- Nine-algorithm comparison (UCL, arXiv June 2024): bio-plausible algorithms "often fall short of achieving the high performance demonstrated by traditional backpropagation"; Hebbian and STDP are worst (CIFAR-10 CNN 29.86 / 33.19 vs BP 73.64), "Local Losses" closest; secondary summaries add that no algorithm achieves both high plausibility and high performance [V for numbers and first quote; S for the last clause] — [arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)
- Dendritic Localized Learning (ICML 2025) literature assessment: algorithms satisfying all three of its plausibility criteria "tend to demonstrate significantly lower performance compared to backpropagation", and consensus on an effective plausible method "remains out of reach" [S] — [ICML 2025 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45791), [arXiv 2501.09976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09976)
- PCX benchmark authors (2024-25): PC matches BP up to about seven layers, then falls behind; generalisation to very deep models is "the main open issue"; PC training several times slower [V] — [arXiv 2407.01163v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v2)
- "Price of Locality" (NeurIPS 2026): FF's "total learning diversity is bounded by a constant independent of depth" whereas BP's grows; the gap "widens with depth and scale"; remedies offered are inter-layer communication and larger blocks, i.e. less locality; authors caution the diagnosis "may not be an exhaustive explanation" [V] — [arXiv 2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33240)
- GrAPE (ICLR 2026): DFA fails on deep CNNs/Transformers without alignment fixes and still needs periodic BP recalibration [S] — [liner review](https://liner.com/ko/review/scaling-direct-feedback-learning-with-jacobian-alignment-guarantees)
- Hinton on FF (2022): it is somewhat slower than backprop and generalises less well on some toy problems, so he doubted it would replace backprop where power is not a constraint; its value would be as a model of cortical learning and for low-power analog "mortal" hardware [S, summary of the paper's text] — [FF paper PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/FFA13.pdf), [bdtechtalks summary](https://bdtechtalks.com/2022/12/19/forward-forward-algorithm-geoffrey-hinton/)
- SoftHebb authors (ICLR 2023): BP in more complex models "significantly outperforms" their Hebbian approach; unclear how to extend beyond width-scaled conv nets [V] — [arXiv 2209.11883](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)
- NoProp's retitling to "without Full Back-propagation" after community criticism that each block still uses backprop illustrates how loosely "backprop-free" is used [S] — [arXiv 2503.24322v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24322v2)

**Optimistic**
- Lillicrap, Santoro, Marris, Akerman, Hinton, "Backpropagation and the brain" (Nature Reviews Neuroscience 21:335-346, April 2020): the brain could approximate backprop's benefits using activity differences and feedback connections while sidestepping its implausible requirements; recurrent-network credit assignment left unaddressed [S] — [Lillicrap et al. 2020 PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/2020-lillicrap.pdf)
- Song et al., "Inferring neural activity before plasticity as a foundation for learning beyond backpropagation" (Nature Neuroscience 27:348-358, 2024): "prospective configuration" (settle activities first, then change weights) reduces interference and is argued to be more efficient than BP in contexts organisms face; Oxford's release says such models can learn faster in tasks typical for animals and humans [S] — [Nature Neuroscience PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01514-1.pdf), [Oxford news](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-01-03-study-shows-way-brain-learns-different-way-artificial-intelligence-systems-learn)
- A published commentary tempers this: PCNs outperform BP "when interference is prominent" (deep or online settings) while BP stays competitive otherwise, and PCNs should be seen as "a fundamentally different learning paradigm" rather than a biologically superior replacement [S] — [Intelligent Computing commentary](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.34133/icomputing.0244)
- CLAPP++ (Jan 2026): the best layer-local SSL rule "matches the performance of a comparable global BP-SSL baseline" on CIFAR-10, STL-10, Tiny ImageNet [V for table; S for abstract wording] — [arXiv 2601.21683](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21683v1)
- Xiao et al. (ICLR 2019): title-level claim that "Biologically-plausible learning algorithms can scale to large datasets" via sign-symmetry [S] — [arXiv 1807.04587 summary](https://api.emergentmind.com/papers/1807.04587)
- Physical-neural-network review ("Training of Physical Neural Networks", arXiv June 2024): lists EP among methods that perform gradient descent without a digital twin, with the caveat that energy gains require a lab implementation without digital processing or external memory [S] — [arXiv 2406.03372](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.03372)

**NeuroAI positions**
- Zador et al., "Catalyzing next-generation Artificial Intelligence through NeuroAI" (Nature Communications, March 2023; co-signers include Bengio, LeCun, Hawkins): proposes an "embodied Turing test" centred on animal-level sensorimotor ability rather than games and language; argues for large investment in NeuroAI [S] — [Zador et al. 2023 reprint](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/lcv/zador23-reprint.pdf), [arXiv 2210.08340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.08340v2)
- Feather, Khosla, Murty, Nayebi (arXiv Feb 2025): "NeuroAI Turing Test" position, arguing that current brain-likeness evaluations are not rigorous and that representational convergence with brains must be tested [S] — [arXiv 2502.16238](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16238v1)
- 2025 survey/position (arXiv 2507.10722) lists learning rules as a principal area where neuroscience, AI and neuromorphic computing converge and diverge; a November 2025 survey "The brain as a blueprint" covers brain-inspired learning approaches [S, titles/summaries only] — [arXiv 2507.10722](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10722v1), [arXiv 2511.04455](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04455v1)

### Inferences
- No review found claims a strictly local, feedback-free rule matches BP at scale. The defensible optimistic claim in late 2026 is narrower: gradient-approximating local methods match BP at about 5-10 conv layers including (as of one June 2026 preprint) ImageNet, and local SSL matches BP-SSL on small nets.
- The stated motivations for the field have shifted from "replace backprop" towards (i) explaining the brain, (ii) memory/parallelism savings (block-local LM pretraining), and (iii) on-chip learning for analog/neuromorphic hardware.
- Research prioritising organic/developmental properties over benchmark accuracy is consistent with how the more careful proponents frame the value of the area (Hinton's "worth further investigation", the PCN commentary's "different paradigm"), but the literature provides no evidence that such properties will yield competitive accuracy by themselves.

### Gaps
- I did not find a named, on-the-record 2025-2026 opinion piece by a leading researcher arguing specifically that local learning rules cannot scale (searches for such commentary, including science-journalism outlets, returned nothing usable). The sceptical case above is assembled from empirical papers' own conclusions.
- Hinton's later public remarks that backprop may be a better learning algorithm than whatever the brain uses were found only via a secondary blog ([gonzoml substack](https://gonzoml.substack.com/p/mortal-computers)); no primary transcript was verified.
- Richards et al. (2019) "A deep learning framework for neuroscience" and the NeuroAI-for-safety roadmap literature were not retrieved.
- Full texts of the 2025-2026 surveys (arXiv 2507.10722, 2511.04455) were not read; their conclusions on scaling are unknown to me.

## 5. Notable 2025-2026 results that changed the picture

### Takeaway
Three things moved in 2025-2026: (1) ImageNet-scale demonstrations for families that previously stopped at CIFAR (FF via ASGE, PCN/EP via a VGG10 result); (2) block-local training of Transformer language models reaching 340M-2B parameters; (3) a theoretical account, accepted at NeurIPS 2026, of why strictly layer-local goodness objectives fall further behind with depth. Nothing found changes the status of purely Hebbian learning, and hardware progress is mostly simulation or small-device demonstrations.

### Cited Findings
- July 2025: SCFF published in Nature Communications; unsupervised FF at CIFAR-10 80.75 / STL-10 77.3 [S] — [arXiv 2409.11593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11593)
- Sep 2025 / Jan 2026: ASGE claims first FF-based training on ImageNet: 26.21% top-1 (VGG11), 51.58% (ResNet18-CH×4) vs BP VGG8 46.16%; CIFAR-10 90.62 [V; preprint] — [arXiv 2509.12394v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12394v2)
- Jan 2026: CLAPP++ layer-local SSL reaches BP-SSL parity on CIFAR-10 / STL-10 / Tiny ImageNet with a VGG-style net [V; preprint] — [arXiv 2601.21683](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21683v1)
- ICLR 2026: Meta-PCN stabilises deep PCNs, claims to beat BP in most tested configurations on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet [V, abstract] — [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/319e084cd710c3db50a9f9dd7a02a9a8-Abstract-Conference.html)
- ICLR 2026: GrAPE revisits DFA with Jacobian alignment for deep CNNs/Transformers, still with periodic BP recalibration [S] — [liner review](https://liner.com/ko/review/scaling-direct-feedback-learning-with-jacobian-alignment-guarantees)
- June 2026: first PCN and first EP training on full-size ImageNet, VGG10, top-5 error 13.23% vs 12.2% BP [V; preprint] — [arXiv 2606.03584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584)
- June 2026: residual-CNN FF with learnable channel-class assignment claims new FF state of the art on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet [S, numbers not confirmed] — [paper listing](https://newx.sg/paper/detail/cf065a56-648d-11f1-b84c-00163e10baa7)
- July 2026: split forward gradients on GPT-2-style LM, far behind BP (386.7 vs 149.6 / 75.5 perplexity) [S] — [arXiv 2607.16612](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16612)
- Sep 2026: SOLO pretrains 340M-2B parameter Transformers on 15B tokens with gradient-isolated modules, within one point of BP zero-shot average, up to 1.44× pipeline-BP throughput [V, abstract; preprint] — [arXiv 2609.35440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440)
- Sep 2026 (NeurIPS 2026): "The Price of Locality" — FF/BP perplexity ratio 3.73× at 7M params rising to 9.19× at 124M; locality sweep on CIFAR-10 from 53% (layer-local) to 79% (global) [V] — [arXiv 2609.33240](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33240)
- Oct 2026: greedy block-local LM pretraining study at 125M-400M; gap shrinks with model size, best K=4 gap +0.062 nats [V, abstract; preprint] — [arXiv 2610.04867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04867)
- March 2025 → 2026: NoProp (diffusion-style per-block label denoising), CIFAR-scale only, backprop within blocks [S] — [PMLR v330](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v330/li26a.html)
- July 2025: NEMO Hebbian simulated language acquisition (semantics, syntactic role, word order, generation) [V, abstract] — [arXiv 2507.11788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11788)
- Sep 2025: BDH "Dragon Hatchling" brain-inspired LM claiming GPT-2-level scaling at 10M-1B params [S; preprint, unreplicated] — [arXiv 2509.26507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26507)
- Hardware, 2025-2026: EP on oscillator Ising machines, simulation-level, about 97.2% MNIST / 88.0% Fashion-MNIST (NeurIPS 2025 workshop) [S] — [NeurIPS 2025 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/123075); EP extended to complex-valued wave systems, numerical only (PRX Intelligence 2026) [S] — [PRX Intelligence](https://journals.aps.org/prxintelligence/pdf/10.1103/qd75-znrn); conference abstract claiming first experimental on-chip training of a stochastic spintronic multilayer network (MATSUS Fall 2026; not peer-reviewed journal work) [S] — [nanoGe abstract](https://www.nanoge.org/proceedings/MATSUSFall26/6a42ece3a196d614486862fd); Loihi 2 work found was offline-trained inference (Oct 2025 preprint: up to 18× faster, 250× less energy than a Jetson Orin Nano), not on-chip plasticity [S] — [arXiv 2510.13757 summary](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2510.13757v1_enmode)
- 2026 surveys exist on local/three-factor training for spiking networks and on backprop-free continual learning [S, titles only] — [preprints.org 202607.0555](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202607.0555), [arXiv 2605.15058](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15058v1)

### Inferences
- "Backprop-free training of Transformers" in 2026 means two different things: block-local with shared readout (works at 2B parameters, near BP) and strictly local objectives (works poorly, worsening with depth). Only the first changed the picture, and it is not a biologically plausible rule.
- Most 2026 headline results (ASGE ImageNet, PCN/EP ImageNet, SOLO, greedy LM) are single-group arXiv preprints from the last 2-14 months; none had an independent replication that I could find. Treat them as claims.
- The energy-based family (EP/PCN) made the largest relative jump: from "fails beyond about 7-9 layers" (PCX, Feb 2025) to a 10-layer ImageNet result within about 1 point top-5 (June 2026).
- For an independent researcher with a small Hebbian/competitive/episodic/growing system: the published frontier offers no evidence that more Hebbian depth will help vision much (SoftHebb plateau, FF rank-collapse argument), some evidence that wider whitened dictionaries will, and essentially an open field for grounded word learning on real sensory input, where the only comparable work uses simulated inputs and reports no standard metrics.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025-2026 paper was found reporting on-chip e-prop or three-factor learning on Loihi 2 / SpiNNaker 2 with benchmark accuracy; aggregator pages mention e-prop extensions to deep and large sparse networks (Nov-Dec 2025) but I could not verify them.
- The MATSUS link above was reconstructed from a search result; the abstract itself was not opened. The June 2026 FF residual-CNN paper's numbers and the two 2026 surveys' contents were not read.
- Whether any of the 2026 preprints (SOLO, ASGE, PCN-ImageNet, greedy LM) has since been accepted at a venue is unknown, except "Price of Locality" (NeurIPS 2026, stated on its arXiv page) and Meta-PCN/GrAPE (ICLR 2026).
- I recall the "Training of Physical Neural Networks" review being published in Nature in 2025; not verified here.
