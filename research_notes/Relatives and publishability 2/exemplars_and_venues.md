### 1.2 Per-paper notes and sources

1. **SoftHebb.** arXiv 2209.11883, submitted 23 Sep 2022, v2 2 Aug 2023; comments field: published ICLR 2023, notable top-25% [V https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11883]. Claims: multilayer Hebbian soft-WTA training with no feedback/target/error; with a linear classifier 99.4 / 80.3 / 76.2 / 27.3% on MNIST / CIFAR-10 / STL-10 / ImageNet [V]. "Each experiment was performed 4 times, with random initializations"; ImageNet "only one random seed was tried"; BP on the same networks ("We also evaluated the same networks trained in a fully label-supervised manner with end-to-end BP"), CIFAR-10 80.31 ± 0.14 vs BP 83.97 ± 0.07; **"Unsupervised Hebbian learning received only one presentation of the training set, i.e. epoch"**; linear head then trained 50–200 epochs [V https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883]. Reviews: not read — forum https://openreview.net/forum?id=8gd4M-_Rj1 [S]. Relevance: the best-known precedent for "one Hebbian pass, modest accuracy, same-shape BP reference".

### 1.4 What the accepted set has in common

- **Seeds:** 3 (Trifecta, Das), 4 (SoftHebb), 5 (Nimmo, Sarfraz). Nobody verified here was accepted on fewer than 3; nobody needed more than 5. Mean ± std in every table.
- **Same-architecture backprop reference:** universal, usually described modestly ("far from SOTA").
- **One standard public benchmark** at minimum (MNIST/CIFAR-10 family, or standard CL splits). None of the accepted papers rests only on a self-built evaluation.
- **Ablation table** isolating each mechanism; in TMLR the rebuttal typically *adds* a control experiment (Tosato, Lampinen).
- **Claims get reduced, not rescued:** "better" → "competitive" (Folchini), title change (Trifecta), efficiency claim softened (Das), "revised claims and limitations" (Lampinen).
- **Stated negatives are normal** and did not block acceptance (Trifecta, Nimmo, SoftHebb).
- TMLR time from submission to publication in this sample: 72, 76, 105, 127, 127, 155, 177, 198, 377 days; median ≈ 127 days [V, computed from OpenReview dates for 9 accepted papers; n is small].

> "Unlike many other journals, TMLR only accepts original contributions that don't reuse the authors' own prior work. In particular, we do not accept submissions that are expanded versions of conference papers. There should not be any reuse of written text, figures or results between the submitted paper and any paper which has been published, accepted for publication, or submitted in parallel at another archival, peer-reviewed venue. LLMs "may be used as general-purpose assistive tools"; "LLMs are not eligible for authorship".

**Consequence for the earlier plan (TMLR + ICDL/CCN):** the same text/figures/results cannot be in TMLR *and* in an archival proceedings. ICDL full papers (IEEE Xplore) and CCN 8-pagers (DOI in CCN Proceedings) are archival → conflict. Safe companions to a TMLR submission: CCN 2-pager (no DOI, parallel submission allowed), ICDL Late-Breaking Results, DevAI-type non-archival workshops, ICBINB non-archival track. CogSci declares its proceedings "not considered archival" in writing and offers abstract-only publication, which matches TMLR's wording, but a 6-page paper in a CC-BY proceedings is a grey area — ask the TMLR editors or choose the abstract-only option.

## 4. arXiv practicalities (late 2026)

**Rules.**
- Endorsement: "As of January 21, 2026, arXiv will no longer accept institutional email addresses… as the sole qualifier of endorsement for new authors." Two paths: (i) institutional email **and** prior authorship of a paper accepted in the same endorsement domain, or (ii) "personal endorsement directly from an established arXiv author in the same endorsement domain". "arXiv staff cannot waive endorsement requirements or provide a personal endorsement for authors." Reason given: "an unsustainable increase in the number of non-scientific submissions" [V https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/01/21/attention-authors-updated-endorsement-policy/]. For an unaffiliated first-timer only path (ii) exists.
- Who can endorse: "Endorsers must have authored a certain number of papers within the endorsement domain"; "we only count papers that have been submitted between three months and five years ago"; find them via the "Which authors of this paper are endorsers?" link on abstract pages of related papers [V https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html]. Mechanics: starting a submission in an unendorsed category triggers an email with a six-character code to pass to the endorser; one positive endorsement per domain; endorser can decline (recorded) or ignore [S https://papex.org/blog/how-to-get-arxiv-endorsement, a vendor page dated 15 Oct 2026]. Endorsement domains are mostly whole archives (e.g. all of cs; q-bio separately) [R for the cs/q-bio detail; the blog says "most are whole subject areas… with the notable exception of physics" V].
- Etiquette, arXiv's own words: "it is inappropriate to email large numbers of potential endorsers at once" and "to repeatedly email the same endorser" [V endorsement help page].
- Rate limit since 1 Oct 2026: "arXiv now limits submitters to up to two submissions per calendar month"; up to three active submissions; rejected submissions count. Context: 40,363 submissions in Sep 2026 vs 20,569 in Sep 2024 [V https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/].
- Generative AI: authors must report significant use of generative-AI tools and take responsibility for all content; tools cannot be authors [V https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html]. Since mid-May 2026: one-year ban on "incontrovertible evidence" of unchecked LLM output (hallucinated references, leftover chatbot text), after which submissions must first be accepted at a peer-reviewed venue; moderator flags, section chair confirms, appeal possible [S: Times Higher Education, TechCrunch 16 May 2026, CERN Courier].
- What moderators decline: work lacking "original or substantive research" (course projects, proposals, news); work that "lacks originality, novelty, significance"; "submissions in need of significant review and revision"; off-topic for the classification scheme. Moderation "is not a peer-review process"; no feedback; appeal decisions final and some appeals "may require prior acceptance in a conventional journal"; previously declined authors get closer scrutiny [V moderation page]. CS review/position papers need prior peer review [S].

**How unaffiliated first-timers actually get endorsed.** No statistic on response rates exists that I could find — treat any number as invented. Documented: (a) public "please endorse me" threads on the Hugging Face forums in 2026 — the one I opened (14 Apr 2026, cs.AI) had **zero replies** [V https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/seeking-arxiv-cs-ai-endorsement-independent-researcher-4-published-papers-neurips-2026-submission/175249]; related threads likewise, one poster reported success [S]; (b) arXiv blog commenters complain that independents must email professors who may never reply [S]. Working practice consistent with arXiv's rules: write individually to 2–4 authors whose recent papers the manuscript genuinely builds on (verified as endorsers via the abstract-page link), attach the PDF and code link, one short paragraph, accept silence, do not mass-mail. The strongest lever is a prior credential: a paper accepted or publicly under review at TMLR/CogSci, or a workshop poster, turns a cold request into a routine one. Note TMLR does **not** require an arXiv preprint; a TMLR submission is itself public on OpenReview while under review.

**OpenReview account caveat (matters for TMLR, CCN, ICDL, workshops).** OpenReview documents a sign-up path for independent researchers, and notes that a profile whose history lists only "Independent Researcher" gets sent back with a request to add another institution record [S https://docs.openreview.net]. Non-institutional-email profiles go through manual moderation that can take up to two weeks [R]. Create the profile well before any deadline.

## 5. Candid assessment

**Against the accepted set, result by result.**

- **(a) One-pass data efficiency vs same-shape backprop (0.52 vs 0.39 after one pass; 0.50 after five).** This is the standard move (SoftHebb and Nimmo both use a single Hebbian epoch and a same-architecture BP reference). But as it stands it is one number on a self-built naming test. Reviewers will ask: how many seeds; was the BP learning rate tuned for the one-pass regime; does BP get the same decorrelated-window preprocessing; what does a nearest-neighbour or linear probe on raw/whitened pixels get. 0.52 vs 0.50 is a tie with five-pass BP, so the defensible sentence is "reaches in one pass what a same-shape BP net needs about five passes for" — the kind of scoped claim TMLR editors push authors towards (Folchini, Das).
- **(b) Fast/slow dissociation (0.9 with the episodic store, 0.1 without).** The cleanest result and the one with the best current precedent (Lampinen et al., TMLR 2026; Constant et al., Cerebral Cortex 2023). The predictable objection: an exemplar store doing one-shot naming is close to nearest-neighbour by construction. It needs the control "same store on top of BP-learned features / raw features" to show what the dissociation depends on.
- **(c) Three of three seeds pass a self-built "two words" stage.** Weakest for ML reviewers (author-chosen thresholds on an author-built test); acceptable to ICDL/CogSci/DevAI readers if tied to developmental measures. Three seeds is the minimum seen in accepted papers; five is safer.
- **(d) Honest negatives (no mutual-exclusivity bias; forgetting at stage change, replay did not fix it; preschool stage failed; two corrected methodological errors).** An asset, not a liability, at TMLR, CogSci and ICBINB — every accepted exemplar lists failures, and BabyLM's best-known curriculum paper is a negative result. The absence of a mutual-exclusivity bias connects to a live literature (Vong & Lake 2022; "Visually grounded speech models have a mutual exclusivity bias", TACL 2024 [S]). They cannot carry a paper alone.

**Is it likely to be accepted somewhere peer-reviewed?** (my judgement, not data)

- Non-archival, peer-reviewed-lite (CCN 2-pager 1 Apr 2027; ICDL Late-Breaking; a 2027 DevAI/ICBINB-type workshop): **very likely** as it stands — CCN says in-scope 2-pagers "will generally be accepted".
- **CogSci 2027 six-pager (deadline 1 Feb 2027):** plausible if framed as a cognitive model (fast vs slow word learning, absence of mutual exclusivity, staged development) with 5 seeds. Cost: attendance in Bilbao is required.
- **ICDL 2027 full paper (~Mar 2027):** the best *topical* fit (developmental staging, Hebbian/competitive learning, growth and pruning are home territory; single-blind review does not penalise an unaffiliated author more than double-blind would help). Costs: registration + presenting in Campinas; archival, so it would be *the* paper, not a companion to TMLR.
- **TMLR:** the best *structural* fit (no novelty/significance bar, claims-vs-evidence only, free, no travel, long format holds the negatives and the methodology corrections). Realistic only after seeds + tuned baseline + ablations exist; with them and conservatively worded claims I would put it around even odds, better if a public benchmark is included. Overall TMLR acceptance among reviewed papers is roughly 70% [S], but the SNAP rejection shows small Hebbian papers are not waved through.
- Neural Networks / Neurocomputing: precedents exist (Nimmo & Mondragón) but those papers report CIFAR-10-level results with five seeds and statistics; slower, closed review.
- Main-track NeurIPS/ICLR/ICML: no.

**Best fit:** TMLR as the archival home for the full paper, preceded by a CCN 2027 two-pager and/or a non-archival developmental workshop for visibility and feedback (both compatible with TMLR's written policy). If a conference paper is preferred over a journal, ICDL 2027 instead of TMLR, not in addition.

**The single additional result that would most raise its chances.** A seeded 2 × 2 factorial on one *public* held-out benchmark under the identical one-pass stream: learning rule (local/Hebbian vs tuned online backprop, same shape, same preprocessing) × episodic store (on vs off), ≥5 seeds, reported as accuracy versus number of samples seen, including the one-shot test. One experiment does four jobs: it turns (a) and (b) from two anecdotes into a controlled finding; it answers the certain reviewer question "is the gain from the learning rule, from the store, or from the preprocessing?"; it replaces the self-built yardstick in (c) with one reviewers already trust; and it supplies the seeds. It is also exactly the shape of the one verified single-author TMLR acceptance in 2026 (a "controlled factorial study" with three seeds). If the factorial shows the store helps backprop features equally, that is still a publishable, honestly scoped result.

**Smaller things that cost little and matter:** public code and fixed seeds; a limitations section that states the forgetting and preschool failures up front; a disclosure paragraph on any substantial AI-tool use (required by arXiv, by ICBINB, and consistent with TMLR policy); every reference checked by hand (arXiv's one-year ban targets hallucinated references).
