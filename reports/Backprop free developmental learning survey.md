# A backprop-free word learner stands nearly alone

No published system combines a plastic Hebbian visual cortex, dendritic word cells, a one-shot episodic store, a reward-modulated route chooser and growth in one learner that acquires words from real photographs, so the organic brain sits in unoccupied territory — but that territory has no accepted benchmark, and the literature offers no evidence that this path leads to competitive accuracy. The nearest relatives split in two: backprop-free grounded word learners that work on simulated input with lexicons of about five words (the NEMO assembly models), and backprop-trained child-view models that set the reference numbers (**61.6% on a four-picture choice for 22 words**, Science 2024). Eight years of scaling studies say strictly local rules fall further behind backpropagation as depth grows; the verified strengths of local, sparse, associative systems are one-shot learning, low forgetting and low compute, which is where this work should stake its claim. The work is publishable as a behaviour-and-methodology paper with narrow claims, and it is not ready today: one seed in three at the pass mark is not a result, the 0.5 naming figure has no chance level or baseline beside it, and forgetting across stage changes is unresolved. What is missing is mostly cheap for a small network: five to ten seeds, a random-features baseline, a size-matched backprop reference, one published local rule, an ablation per organ and a shuffled-curriculum control. The recommended route is a Zenodo snapshot, a personal arXiv endorsement from an author of a cited paper (mandatory for unaffiliated first-time submitters since 21 January 2026), an arXiv posting in cs.NE, and submission to TMLR, which is free, double-blind and accepts modest contributions whose claims are supported. A spring 2027 developmental venue (ICDL or a CCN extended abstract) adds a venue stamp, and a dedicated AI-use statement naming what the coding assistant did and how the author verified it belongs in the paper.

### The Hebbian word learners are proofs of concept, which leaves real images open

The assembly-calculus line is the one direct precedent for a backprop-free system that acquires grounded words and word order. Its 2023 paper runs a main example with a **five-word lexicon**, needs about 10–20 grounded sentences per word with cost growing linearly in lexicon size, and found that tutoring on single words cut early training time by over 40% ([Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15364v1)). The 2025 successor adds word order and generation of withheld sentences, reports no benchmark accuracies and makes no quantitative comparison with child data ([Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11788v1)). The sensory side is a simplified model in both papers. The older cognitive-science relatives — Hebbian-linked self-organising maps in Psychological Review, the iCub associative architecture — prove that local-rule word learners publish in strong venues when they reproduce a specific infant pattern, and the most admired result of that line is a robot model whose prediction about posture and naming was then confirmed in new infant experiments ([Morse et al. 2015](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/69097/)). The survey behind this report found no backprop-free system that learns words from real images, across roughly 45 queries on the local-learning side alone; that is an absence of hits, not proof of absence.

### The reference numbers for picture–word learning come from backprop models

CVCL defines how the field reports grounded word learning: a forced choice among four pictures with chance at 25%, a shuffled-pairing control that fell to 26.7%, a frozen random vision encoder at 38.0%, a supervised linear probe at 81.6% as upper bound, and a separate out-of-distribution test ([Vong et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374)). A speech-based counterpart with infant-scale exposure reached 57.8%, 67.8% and 72.8% on a two-alternative test over 80 categories at simulated 8, 10 and 12 months ([Khorrami and Räsänen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05259)). The organic brain's **0.5 on held-out photographs** is the natural neighbour of these figures, with two differences that cut in opposite directions. Producing a name is harder than choosing a picture, which favours the organic brain; and a bare 0.5 is uninterpretable without the number of candidate names, the chance level and the same controls, which is the first thing a reviewer will ask. CVCL's authors list their own weaknesses as multi-epoch training rather than a continual stream, passive learning and still frames ([Vong et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374)); a single-pass online learner answers the first of these directly.

### The vision front end sits at a known ceiling, and width beats depth

A feedback-free Hebbian front end with a supervised readout tops out near 80% on CIFAR-10: SoftHebb reaches 80.31% with three layers and 71.10% with one, where hard winner-take-all gives 62.69% and random weights 63.93% in the same single layer ([Journé et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)). The older whitening recipe does as well with one layer: K-means features on whitened patches reached 79.6% in 2011 ([Coates, Lee and Ng](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/coates11a.html)), and a dictionary of whitened patches sampled at random from the training images reaches **85.6–86.9% with a linear classifier**, rising from 82.5% at 2,048 patches to 86.7% at 60,000, while optimising the dictionary end to end changes accuracy by about 0.1 points ([Thiry et al., ICLR 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.07528)). Two lessons follow for a competitive-learning cortex with decorrelation. Dictionary width and dense whitened extraction buy more than a cleverer unsupervised rule or more Hebbian layers. And hard winner-take-all sits level with random filters, so a random-filter baseline is mandatory, because the literature already shows it is hard to beat.

### The fast store beside slow weights is textbook theory with toy-scale local implementations

The split between an episodic store that names a just-seen picture at 0.9 and slow weights at 0.1 is what complementary learning systems theory predicts: a fast store with sparse, non-interfering codes beside a slow learner that extracts regularities ([Kowadlo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15999)). Local-rule implementations of that theory exist only at tens of items ([Singh, Norman and Schapiro 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9636926/fullTextXML)); the implementations with benchmark numbers train the slow side with backprop, such as a Hopfield store beside a variational autoencoder at 89.71% on class-incremental Split-MNIST ([Jun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11393v1)). A reviewer will read the 0.9 figure as the expected behaviour of a lookup memory, and the published way to make it informative is to compare against nearest-neighbour retrieval on the same features and to test under noise and occlusion, as the artificial-hippocampus work did on Omniglot ([Kowadlo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15999)).

### Babbling, echo and two-word speech are modelled in separate systems

Published models treat babbling, imitation and word meaning in different systems, and the survey found no 2024–2026 model that carries reward-modulated babbling through to grounded word meaning. For early multi-word speech the quantitative standard is set by count-based learners on child-directed speech: the chunk-based learner regenerates **59.8% of children's own utterances** across 13 corpora against 49.6–54.2% for n-gram alternatives ([McCauley and Christiansen 2011](https://csl-lab.psych.cornell.edu/files/2021/01/2011-mc-cogsci.pdf)). No neural or Hebbian model with a child-benchmarked one-word-to-two-word transition was located. One learner that passes babbling, echo, naming and two-word stages in sequence is therefore unusual in itself.

## The literature backs the niche and rejects the scaling story

### Strictly local rules trail backprop, and the gap widens with depth

The empirical record from 2018 to 2026 is consistent. Bartunov and colleagues found target-propagation and feedback-alignment variants performed significantly worse than backprop on CIFAR and ImageNet ([NeurIPS 2018](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2018/poster/11892)). A neutral nine-algorithm comparison in 2024 put generic Hebbian and STDP convolutional nets at **29.86% and 33.19% on CIFAR-10 against 73.64% for backprop** ([arXiv 2406.16062](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.16062)). A NeurIPS 2026 paper gives the cleanest statement of the trade-off: on a 12-layer network, strictly layer-local training scores 53.02% on CIFAR-10, rising to 78.74% as locality is relaxed to one global block, with backprop at 85.67%; on language models the Forward-Forward to backprop perplexity ratio grows from 3.73 times at 7M parameters to 9.19 times at 124M ([Wu, Liu and Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33240)). The SoftHebb authors state that backprop in more complex models significantly outperforms their approach ([Journé et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11883)).

The methods that close the gap do so by approximating the backprop gradient or by keeping backprop inside blocks. Predictive coding matches backprop on five-layer networks (89.47% versus 89.43% on CIFAR-10) and collapses on ResNet-18 (62.45% versus 93.21%) ([PCX benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v2)); a June 2026 preprint trains a 10-layer predictive-coding network on full ImageNet with equilibrium propagation at 13.23% top-5 error against 12.2% ([Kerjan, Høier and Scellier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03584)); a September 2026 preprint pretrains 340M–2B-parameter Transformers with gradient-isolated modules within one point of backprop ([SOLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35440)). None of these is an emergent Hebbian system. Most 2026 headline results are single-group preprints without independent replication. The honest summary: **nothing in the literature says Hebbian competition, a delta rule and an episodic store will reach competitive accuracy by themselves**, and a paper that implies otherwise will be rejected on the existing evidence.

### Four findings do support this path

First, continual and one-shot learning is where local, sparse, associative systems win. The fruit-fly model beats EWC, SI and MAS on class-incremental learning without replay or task labels ([Bricken et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11934)); Monty learns from one view where a from-scratch vision transformer lags and shows only minor interference across 77 sequential tasks ([Leadholm et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1)); prospective-configuration networks are argued to learn more efficiently than backprop in the online, low-interference settings organisms face ([Song et al., Nature Neuroscience 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01514-1.pdf)).

Second, the individual organs have biological and theoretical support. Hebbian feedforward plus anti-Hebbian lateral learning is derivable from a similarity-matching objective and performs online whitening ([Pehlevan and Chklovskii](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.09468)), and decorrelating layer inputs gave more than a two-fold speed-up in deep networks ([Dalm et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02385v5)) — consistent with the jump the organic brain saw when decorrelation went in between cortex and word cells. A February 2026 Nature paper reported neuron-specific, signed teaching signals in apical dendrites of mouse cortex, which legitimises teacher-driven local rules such as a delta rule on dendritic compartments ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/neurons-receive-precisely-tailored-teaching-signals-as-we-learn-0309)).

Third, the combination itself is what the field has asked for. A 2022 Nature Machine Intelligence position paper lists neurogenesis, neuromodulation, metaplasticity, context gating and replay as the mechanisms lifelong learners must combine ([Kudithipudi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00452-0)); the survey found published systems combining two or three at a time and none combining all with language acquisition.

Fourth, the mainstream developmental-language community has hit limits that a grounded associative learner addresses. Across four BabyLM rounds the multimodal track never produced an entry that beat its baselines and was dropped as a separate track in 2026 ([BabyLM 2026 call](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20092)). Forty-five text-only GPT-2 models trained on child-directed speech showed the opposite of mutual exclusivity on 85–100% of items at every scale, and the author argues referential grounding may be required ([Cacioli 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.19/)), while an associative robot model produced the effect from word–object association alone ([Twomey et al.](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/news/articles/2016/toddler-robots-help-solve-how-children-learn-/)). A child-like signature that gradient-trained models lack is the most valuable thing a small Hebbian learner can show.

### The scepticism that applies to this system specifically

Staged curricula are not a selling point. Curriculum learning was the most popular BabyLM approach and the organisers judged it largely unsuccessful in 2023, estimated a negative effect in 2024 (beta = −3.6, not significant) and again found wins came from architecture and objective changes in 2025 ([2023 findings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08165), [2024 findings](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1), [2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)). An age-of-acquisition curriculum produced child-like acquisition order at rho 0.63–0.78, but the order was supplied by the curriculum, and alignment fell to about 0.3 with presentation order partialled out ([Huda 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.14/)). Developmental stages need a shuffled-order control or reviewers will treat them as decoration.

Forgetting when the training mix changes is the known unsolved problem of exactly this design. Sparse-code continual learners fail when the upstream representation keeps moving: jointly training the sparse memory layer with its feature extractor produced dead neurons and continual learning failed, and pruning weights caused catastrophic forgetting ([Bricken et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11934)). Growth can itself induce forgetting ([SparseGrow](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10566v5)). Every continual-learning result with competitive numbers freezes or pre-trains the feature extractor; the survey found no fully local system with a plastic feature extractor at even CIFAR class-incremental scale. Weight protection alone stays near last-task accuracy (EWC 20.37% on class-incremental MNIST), while **rehearsal with 0.75% of old data reaches 79.91%** and rehearsal plus Hebbian sleep replay 86.47% against 98.02% for joint training ([Tadros et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9755223/fullTextXML)).

Low forgetting with low absolute quality is the typical profile of 2026 local-rule learners. A July 2026 sparse-code and delta-rule system cut forgetting from +2.790 to +0.148 bits per byte against a fine-tuned Transformer while sitting at 3.1–3.3 bits per byte, well below the Transformer, with a null result on Split-MNIST ([CMP](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26523v1)). Reviewers know this pattern and discount forgetting metrics presented without absolute accuracy.

Whole-system architectures are criticised for whatever they engineered by hand. Spaun's weights are solved offline and it cannot learn new tasks ([commentary](https://philosophyofbrains.com/?p=98)); NEMO pre-wires areas and pre-initialises phonology ([arXiv 2306.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15364v1)); Monty learns with supervision ([arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04494v1)). And outsiders ablate brain-inspired claims: an independent analysis of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model reproduced about 32% on hidden ARC-AGI-1 data against the claimed 41% and showed the brain-inspired hierarchy had minimal impact compared with a refinement loop and augmentation ([ARC Prize](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)). A stage pass mark, a curriculum and an adult-worded text source are all engineered pieces that the organic brain's paper must name before a reviewer does.

## Narrow claims and eight missing controls separate the notebook from a paper

### The strongest framing is a behaviour study of one integrated online learner

The framing the literature rewards is specific: a single learner, trained in one pass on real photographs and text with local rules only, that spans stages the field models in separate systems, reported with held-out generalisation, per-organ ablations and its failures. That maps onto what TMLR accepts — claims supported by clear evidence and of interest to some of its audience, "even if the contribution or significance of the work is modest" ([TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)) — and onto what the NeurIPS DevAI workshop and BabyLM workshop solicit ([DevAI call](https://sites.google.com/view/devai-workshop-2026/call-for-papers), [BabyLM 2026 call](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20092v2)). Three framings fail. "An alternative to backprop" contradicts the scaling evidence. "Biologically plausible" without a statement of which constraints each update respects triggers scepticism; the fix is a table of what information every update uses. "A model of child development" requires child data, and the model's input (adult-worded text, photographs) is not a child's input; the 2026 BabyLM audit found even the standard child-directed corpus matches real infant input only moderately, rho = 0.42 ([Huda 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.14/)). Developmental stages belong in the paper as a training protocol under test, with human comparison kept qualitative.

The paper needs one sharp question. The best candidate on today's evidence is the fast–slow dissociation plus the decorrelation effect: what each organ buys in a single-pass grounded word learner, measured by ablation. The episodic store's 0.9 against 0.1 mirrors the distinction McMurray, Horst and Samuelson draw between fast in-the-moment referent selection and slow associative learning ([Psychological Review 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029872)), which gives the result a home in the developmental literature. The second candidate, higher risk and higher reward, is a signature test: mutual exclusivity on a novel-word, novel-object trial and shape bias on generalisation. A positive mutual-exclusivity result from an associative, grounded, backprop-free learner would be the most quotable finding available, because gradient-trained text models demonstrably lack it.

The baseline set is the minimum a reviewer in this area looks for, and equal tuning effort per method should be stated, since tuning asymmetry is a recurring objection to results of this kind. On statistics, three to ten runs is the described norm, with interquartile means and bootstrap confidence intervals recommended over significance tests ([Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13264)); the NeurIPS checklist requires error bars to be defined, the method of computing them explained, and data splits, hyperparameter selection and compute reported ([NeurIPS checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)). For a small network five to ten seeds is cheap and removes a standard objection. For the two-word stage, a productivity test from child-language research is available: determiner–noun overlap against a Zipfian expectation, which children aged two to three pass and no model with a child-comparable data budget passes on both of its measures ([Vázquez Martínez and Yang 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28616)).

### Forgetting is either the paper's hole or its second result

Two published findings point to a cheap experiment. Replay focused only on recent material overwrites older cortical knowledge, and alternating recent-focused with remote-focused replay fixes it ([Singh et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9636926/fullTextXML)); and interleaving only those old items that resemble the new material preserves old knowledge with far fewer replayed items ([similarity-weighted interleaved learning](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1rw1b1m4)). The organic brain already owns an episodic store, so replaying a small, similarity-selected slice of earlier-stage episodes when the mix changes is the theory-motivated remedy and a direct test of complementary learning systems in a fully local system with a plastic cortex — the configuration the survey found unstudied. If it works, that is a second result. If it fails, a diagnosed failure is publishable: NeurIPS instructs reviewers not to penalise honesty about limitations ([NeurIPS checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)), and the 2026 CMP preprint reported its Split-MNIST null result openly ([CMP](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26523v1)). What the paper cannot do is leave forgetting unmeasured.

## Endorsement is the gate; arXiv plus TMLR is the route

### arXiv in late 2026: one personal endorsement, one solid paper

Since 21 January 2026 automatic endorsement requires both an institutional email and prior authorship in the endorsement domain, so an unaffiliated first-time submitter must obtain a **personal endorsement from an established arXiv author**, and arXiv staff cannot waive this ([arXiv blog, 21 Jan 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/01/21/attention-authors-updated-endorsement-policy/)). The mechanics: start the submission, choose the category, receive a six-character endorsement code, use the "Which authors of this paper are endorsers?" link on the abstract pages of papers the manuscript cites, and email one eligible author with the code and the manuscript; mass emailing is called inappropriate, and the endorser is asked to confirm fit to the subject area, not correctness ([arXiv endorsement help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)). A finished draft with working code makes the request easy to grant. Authors of the closest relatives in this report are the natural people to ask. Endorsement is per domain: a computer-science endorser covers a cs.NE primary, and a q-bio.NC primary would need a q-bio endorser; the help page does not say whether cross-lists need their own.

The best literal category is **cs.NE** ("neural networks, connectionism, genetic algorithms, artificial life, adaptive behavior"), with one or two cross-lists such as cs.LG and cs.CL or q-bio.NC; more than two cross-lists is discouraged ([category taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/category_taxonomy), [cross-list help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/cross.html)). A single-author Hebbian language paper from March 2025 sits in cs.CL with cross-lists to cs.AI and q-bio.NC, which shows the lane is open ([arXiv 2503.02057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02057)). Four rules shape the manuscript. Review and position papers in arXiv CS now need prior peer-reviewed acceptance, so the paper must read as an original research article with experiments, not a vision piece ([arXiv blog, 31 Oct 2025](https://blog.arxiv.org/?p=1740)). Moderators decline work "needing significant review and revision" and give no feedback ([arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)). Submitters are capped at two submissions per calendar month since 1 October 2026, with rejected submissions counting, and announced versions are permanent ([arXiv blog, 1 Oct 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)). And the arXiv CS chair announced in May 2026 a one-year submission ban for incontrovertible evidence of unchecked LLM output such as hallucinated references — stated on X and in an interview, not on a formal help page ([SMU Libraries explainer](https://library.smu.edu.sg/topics-insights/arxiv-tightens-policy-hallucinated-references-what-researchers-should-know-about)). Every reference must be opened and checked by hand. That includes the references in this report: the underlying survey notes mark part of the older literature as recalled rather than re-read, and several figures come from search summaries.

### Alternatives and peer-reviewed homes

Zenodo accepts all research artefacts from any registered user, issues a DOI and versions files, with no pre-publication moderation described ([Zenodo policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/)); it is the place for code, data and a manuscript snapshot while endorsement is pending. bioRxiv requires an affiliation and its life-science scope is a poor fit ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript)); PsyArXiv, pre-moderated since August 2025, fits only a paper framed around child data ([PsyArXiv notice](https://improvingpsych.org/2025/08/20/changes-to-moderation-at-psyarxiv-preprints/)). **TMLR is the strongest peer-reviewed home**: no fees, double-blind, rolling, prior arXiv posting allowed, novelty not a necessary criterion, and rejection reserved for bold statements unsupported by evidence ([TMLR editorial policies](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html), [reviewer guide](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/reviewer-guide.html)). Double-blind review is the only verified structural protection for an unaffiliated author; no venue publishes affiliation statistics. The survey found no documented case of an unaffiliated individual whose backprop-free paper gained wide traction in 2022–2026, so credibility has to come from released code with one-command reproduction, a venue stamp and precise scoping.

### Disclosing the AI assistant

Every policy read agrees on three points: AI tools cannot be authors, human authors bear full responsibility, and use beyond routine editing is disclosed. arXiv requires authors to "report in their work any significant use of sophisticated tools" and bars listing generative AI as an author ([arXiv moderation help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html)). NeurIPS exempts "basic coding assistance" ([NeurIPS 2026 guidance](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/EvaluationsDatasetsReviewerGuidelines)), but ICLR 2027 makes an AI-disclosure section mandatory and lists "implementing methods", designing experiments and interpreting results among required disclosures ([ICLR 2027 policy](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors)); TMLR allows LLMs as assistive tools with authors fully responsible ([TMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)). An assistant that wrote much of the code and ran experiments is well beyond basic coding help under any of these. The portable form is a short titled section naming the tool and version, the tasks it performed (implementation, experiment scripts, analysis, drafting), the tasks it did not, and how the author verified the output — reran experiments from seeded scripts, read the code, checked every citation — repeated briefly in the acknowledgments and the repository README. The trend from 2025 to 2027 runs toward more disclosure, and with arXiv receiving 40,363 submissions in September 2026 and naming AI-written papers as a cause ([arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)), a clear verification statement is also the best defence against the assumption that a single-author paper is unreviewed machine output.

### The recommended route

Hold the paper until the evidence in the table above exists; arXiv versions are permanent and the first impression on an endorser counts. Through the winter, run the seeds, baselines, ablations and the shuffled-order control, quantify forgetting, and try episodic replay across the stage change. When the claims are frozen, deposit code, data and a manuscript snapshot on Zenodo for a DOI and timestamp. Start the arXiv submission in cs.NE, send the endorsement code with the manuscript and repository link to one author of a paper the manuscript builds on, and post once endorsed. Submit the same manuscript to TMLR. For a venue stamp with a developmental audience, the 2026 pattern puts ICDL full papers and CCN two-page abstracts in early April and the BabyLM workshop's direct deadline in mid-July; CogSci's early-February date arrives before the evidence will. The survey did not check TMLR's dual-submission wording, so pair TMLR with non-archival options (a CCN extended abstract, BabyLM's non-archival track) until that is confirmed. Skip the fee-charging journals, and keep everything in one paper: the rate limit and the moderation climate both punish thin splits.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the publication question from "is the network's behaviour interesting?" to "which one claim can this network support that nobody else has made?". The unoccupied ground is real — single-pass learning of words from real photographs with local rules, across stages the field models separately — but its emptiness reflects a field that has learned to distrust integrated brain-inspired systems until someone ablates them. The paper that survives is the one where the author has done that ablation first and reports what each organ buys, including the organs that buy nothing.

The weakness the researcher most wants to hide is the most valuable open question in the neighbourhood. Complementary learning systems theory has never been tested in a fully local system whose sensory cortex stays plastic, and the organic brain is such a system with an episodic store already in place. Whether replay from that store holds earlier stages together when the training mix changes is an experiment the literature has not run, and either outcome is a finding. The accuracy numbers will never impress this literature; a clean answer to that question, or a mutual-exclusivity effect that gradient-trained models lack, will.
