# Review of the whole project (2026-11-10)

Asked by Kris: an overall pass on the idea, the status and the results, and how to make
learning more organic and emergent without forcing architecture. Performance is not the aim.
This replaces docs/STATUS.md (2026-10-21) as the place to start.

## 1. Where it stands

**The idea.** A brain without backpropagation: clusters of neurons grow where nothing has
affinity to the situation, learn with local rules, are myelinated, consolidated in sleep and
pruned by use. Organs (eye, raw-byte ear, mouth) feed it. It goes through a graded curriculum
(babbling → 4th grade) and each passed stage is released as a level of one lineage (kid-a).

**Lives so far.**

| brain | size | lived | got to |
|---|---|---|---|
| kid_s3 (lineage; L1 babbler, L2 echo, L3 first words) | 21.5k clusters, 10 GB | 745k moments | stops at two words (0.35 < 0.5). Its first words passed on a one-wording test; with varied wordings they are ~0.23 |
| small, no hub | 2k clusters, 2–3 GB | 110k | stops at first words (0.10–0.15); same answer across wordings 0% |
| small, hub | 2k clusters | 150k | first words 0.36 (passed at 50k); wording consistency 77–97%; stops at two words (0.35), where picture naming collapses |
| small, hub + answer kinds | 2k clusters | 110k | first words 0.26–0.38, never 0.4; consistency 83–100%; echo eroded 0.93 → 0.68–0.80 |

No life is running now (kid_s3 and the last small life both ended). The data daemons and the
judge are healthy.

**What emerged without being told** (the wins, by Kris's criterion):
- the same answer whatever the wording: 0% → 80–100% once words link to organ cells across situations;
- answer kinds from distribution alone: names, colours, numbers, yes/no, family words, body parts;
- organs settling by themselves: the ear's code stops drifting in a life; a visual cortex learning
  from scratch falls from drift 0.40 to 0.014 with no clock;
- fast and slow answers: pictures ~5 ticks, questions about words 7.5–10;
- babbling in the sounds of the language heard, from covert imitation alone;
- a hub brain ten times smaller than kid_s3 reaches the same two-words score in a fifth of the moments.

## 2. Three things the results say when read together

**A. First words are at the ceiling of what the eyes deliver.** A nearest-neighbour probe on the
visual code names first-words objects 0.36–0.42 of the time; the hub names them 0.39; the pass
mark is 0.4. The developed visual cortex is no better than random filters (0.42 both), and
the trace rule alone was worse (0.17). No work on the language side can lift picture naming above
~0.4. Vision, not word binding, is now the limit for anything about pictures.

**B. Much of the noise is the ruler, not the brain.** Each stage is tested on 60 items; at a
score of 0.35 that is a sampling error of ±0.06 per checkpoint, and scores were seen to move ±0.08.
The gate at 0.4 next to a ceiling of ~0.4 is close to a coin flip. With 300 items the error is
±0.03. Results are also one seed each. Robust (large effect or large n): wording consistency
0 → 0.9; hub 0.15 → 0.36; answer kinds offline (n = 900) 0.19 → 0.32 and 0.27 → 0.43; vision
learned = random. Within noise, not to be relied on: cluster size 4 vs 8; "overheard speech
hurts" (0.14 vs 0.11); "kinds are slower than the additive hub" (0.31 vs 0.36).

**C. The newest parts work best and are the least organic.** See the audit.

## 3. Audit: what is given, what emerges, what is forced

*Given, and fine (the genome's share):* raw transduction (pixels, bytes → fixed neuron patterns),
which organs exist and what is wired to what, that there is sleep, the local rule of a neuron.
*The world's share:* the curriculum, the adults, the judge.

*Forced, to be dissolved* (in order of how much they matter):

1. **Words are handed over, not discovered.** The hub's lexical neurons are the adults' whole
   answer strings: "eating grass" is one neuron. The ear's units and the mouth's motor chunks,
   which the brain did form itself, are not what words are made of.
2. **The hub is an algorithm, not tissue.** Count tables, exact normalisation P(word | unit), a hard
   pick of one kind, a batch clustering in sleep with a threshold. The principle is right
   (cross-situational statistics over separate organ cells), the implementation is bespoke. The
   best single result used the orchestrator's own neurons for the visual area (consistency 0.99).
3. **Three answer routes wired by hand** (copy / own / recall) with a chooser. Recall competes with
   copy and erodes echo (0.93 → ~0.72, which also blocks promotion: 11 of 20 checkpoints failed
   retention).
4. **Every moment is a lesson.** A question, then the right answer as a target for the output
   weights, then a reward. Local, but fully supervised. Children mostly observe: they hear speech
   about what they are looking at, unasked.
5. **The ear's cuts** still use two hand thresholds (1.2 bits, 85%); the copy route treats the
   space byte as a pause and weights recency by 0.75.
6. **Vision is frozen in lives** and its rule learns nothing useful; the eye's zoom order (coarse →
   medium → fine) and 25 gaze targets are fixed.
7. **About forty hand-set numbers** in the cortex (thresholds 0.15 / 0.55 / 0.95, temperature 0.04,
   myelin and stability steps, patience steps, sleep every 2,000, merge 0.97, retire 30,000).
8. **Stage code in the life loop:** babbling is scheduled by stage (BB, B) instead of fading as
   words take over.

A pattern to watch: when something failed, a module was added (fatigue, predictive ear, hub, kinds,
routes). Three were negative. The two that worked did so because of a principle, and each is now
a special-purpose structure.

## 4. How to make it more organic (research and proposals)

**4.1 One tissue, many areas.** Keep one kind of neuron and cluster (the Cortex). The genome's share
is only the wiring diagram: a visual area fed by the eye, an auditory area fed by the ear, a motor
area driving the mouth, and an association area fed by the others. What is now "hub", "kinds" and
"routes" should come out of that tissue: association clusters over auditory and visual cells are
cross-situational links; clusters over lexical contexts are kinds. Side effect: each area's
clusters span only their own inputs, so the 10 GB of kid_s3 (every cluster spans all ~8k inputs)
shrinks by a large factor.

**4.2 Vision: both halves of the rule, driven by the eye's own movements.** Halvagar & Zenke (2023,
Nature Neuroscience, "latent predictive learning") show that Hebbian learning alone does not give
object invariance, and prediction alone collapses; a layer-local rule with both (predict your own
next activity; keep your variance; decorrelate from neighbours) learns invariant objects from
sequences of views, with no labels. Our two negatives are those two halves tested apart
(competitive Hebbian = random filters; trace alone = worse). The brain already produces the
sequences: successive fixations and zoom levels on one picture are views of one thing, unused
for learning so far. Commons' many breeds per word add natural variety.

**4.3 Words from the brain's own units, with meaning as a cue.** Lexical cells should be the
ear's units and the mouth's chunks that recur with something seen, not answer strings. Räsänen
& Khorrami's infant-scale models learn word–referent links from unsegmented raw speech and pixels:
segmentation and meaning grow together. Here: a heard chunk that predicts something visual is
worth keeping whole; one that predicts nothing is a frame. That is ear plan steps 4–5 with the
referent as the teacher instead of a rule.

**4.4 Observation before interrogation.** Add moments where an adult simply talks about the picture
("look, a dog!"; COCO has five captions per photo), with no question and no answer expected. Links
form from co-occurrence alone; question moments then show what was learned. The reward (now the
judged acceptance) stays as the third factor that reinforces what the brain itself said (neo-
Hebbian three-factor rules: Gerstner et al. 2018).

**4.5 Set-points instead of thresholds.** Replace fixed thresholds by homeostasis: each neuron
keeps its own activity near a target rate (intrinsic plasticity, BCM's sliding threshold). One
measured quantity (sparseness) instead of forty numbers; "familiar enough" becomes relative to the
neuron's own history.

**4.6 Gates as measurements; curiosity as the driver.** Infants sample what they are making progress
on (Oudeyer's learning progress; Twomey & Westermann 2018; Altmann et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2020).
The brain already computes surprise per moment; its fall per kind of moment is learning progress.
Let that bias which moments it attends to, and keep the stages as the yardstick for released
levels, not as a schedule imposed on the brain.

**4.7 The ruler.** 300 test items per stage, three seeds for any claim, and an alert when a daemon
or a life stops (the daemons were down 3.5 days unnoticed).

## 5. Proposed order

1. The ruler (4.7): small, and everything after depends on it.
2. One tissue, many areas (4.1) with words from the brain's units (4.3), offline against today's
   hub (0.39 right, 0.99 consistent).
3. Vision with the two-part rule on the eye's own fixations (4.2), offline against 0.42.
4. Observation moments (4.4) in the world.
5. kid_s4 from babbling, with set-points (4.5) and gates as measurements (4.6) as they are ready.
